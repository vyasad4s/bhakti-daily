Bhagavad-gītā BG 18.2

श्रीभगवानुवाच काम्यानां कर्मणां न्यासं सन्न्यासं कवयो विदु: । सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं प्राहुस्त्यागं विचक्षणा: ॥ २ ॥
śrī-bhagavān uvāca kāmyānāṁ karmaṇāṁ nyāsaṁ sannyāsaṁ kavayo viduḥ sarva-karma-phala-tyāgaṁ prāhuḥ tyāgaṁ vicakṣaṇāḥ
The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: The giving up of activities that are based on material desire is what great learned men call the renounced order of life [sannyāsa]. And giving up the results of all activities is what the wise call renunciation [tyāga].
Sunday is the Sun's day — Sūrya, the revealer, the one who shows things as they are. And here Kṛṣṇa answers Arjuna's question with surgical precision: two words, two distinct operations. *Sannyāsa* is not "giving up action" — it is *kāmyānāṁ karmaṇāṁ nyāsaḥ*, the deposition (nyāsa) of *kāmya-karma*, action driven by *kāma* (desire for fruit). The root *ni-as* means "to place down, to deposit" — you place the desire-driven action down. *Tyāga* is different: *sarva-karma-phala-tyāgaḥ*, the relinquishment of the fruit of *all* action, not just desire-driven action. The wise (*vicakṣaṇāḥ*) see that the fruit was never theirs to claim; they perform the action, the fruit arises, and they let it fall where it may. Sunday's Sun reveals this distinction: light does not cling to what it illuminates. The panchang mirrors this: Uttarāṣāḍhā ("the latter invincible one," the star of final victory, Viśvedevas) yields to Śravaṇa ("hearing," the ear that receives the Veda) at 12:58 PM — the listening that follows victory. Gāṇḍa (the knot, the obstruction) yields to Vṛddhi (growth, expansion) at 8:45 PM — the knot unties into expansion. Ravivār (Sunday) is the day the knot is cut by the blade of discrimination. The Ṣaṣṭhī tithi (Skanda's day, the commander who leads the divine host) is the day the army of *kāma* is routed. The verse is not a definition; it is a surgical instrument. Use it. 🏹

Verse Selection Rationale

Chapter 18 (Mokṣa/Sannyāsa Yoga) Day 2. Sunday is Ravivār — Sūrya's day, the revealer. Kṛṣṇa answers Arjuna's question with the surgical distinction between sannyāsa (renunciation of desire-driven action) and tyāga (renunciation of all fruit). The panchang amplifies this: Uttarāṣāḍhā (the latter invincible, final victory) yields to Śravaṇa (hearing, reception) at 12:58 PM — the victory that listens. Gāṇḍa (the knot) yields to Vṛddhi (expansion) at 8:45 PM — the knot unties into growth. Ravivār (Sun day) is the day the Sun cuts the knot of *kāma* with the blade of *viveka*. Ṣaṣṭhī is Skanda's day — the commander who leads the divine host against the *kāma*-asuras. No repeat: BG 18.1 used for 11-16; BG 18.2 is the Day 2 fit. The verse is the surgical instrument that separates *kāmya-karma* from *karma* itself. 🏹

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS II.3 — avidyāsmitārāgadveṣābhiniveśāḥ kleśāḥ — "The kleśas are ignorance, egoism, attachment, aversion, and clinging to life." The bridge is surgical. BG 18.2 identifies *kāma* (desire for fruit) as the root of *kāmya-karma*; YS II.3 identifies *rāga* (attachment) and *dveṣa* (aversion) as the kleśas that bind. *Kāma* is *rāga* in its fruitive mode — the attachment to the fruit. *Sannyāsa* (deposition of *kāmya-karma*) is the surgical removal of *rāga*; *tyāga* (relinquishment of all fruit) is the dissolution of *dveṣa* (aversion to fruitlessness). The Sun (Sunday) is the light that reveals the kleśas for what they are: not enemies to fight, but knots to untie. YS II.10 — te pratyavaśyāḥ sūkṣmāḥ — in their subtle form they are resolved by *pratiprasava* (involution, returning to source). The Sun is the *pratiprasava* fire. 🏹

Sanskrit Terms

  • kāmya (काम्य) — born of desire, desire-motivated; like a feature flag gated by user demand — the code only runs if the metric demands it
  • karma (कर्म) — action, activity; like the executable — the process that runs regardless of flags
  • nyāsa (न्यास) — deposition, placing down, renunciation; like git rm --cached — removing from the index but keeping the working tree
  • sannyāsa (सन्न्यास) — renunciation of desire-driven action; like deprecating the desire-gated codepaths — removing only what was gated by *kāma*
  • tyāga (त्याग) — renunciation of fruit; like running the code and discarding stdout — the process executes, the output is abandoned
  • vicakṣaṇa (विचक्षण) — wise, discriminating, expert; like the senior architect — the one who sees the dependency graph whole
