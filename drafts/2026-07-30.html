Bhagavad Gita Chapter 11, Verse 3

एवमेतद्यथात्थ त्वमात्मानं परमेश्वर ।
द्रष्टुमिच्छामि ते रूपमैश्वरं पुरुषोत्तम ॥ ३ ॥

 

evam etad yathāttha tvam ātmānaṁ parameśvara
draṣṭum icchāmi te rūpam aiśvaraṁ puruṣottama

 

O greatest of all personalities, O supreme form, though I see You here before me in Your actual position, as You have described Yourself, I wish to see how You have entered into this cosmic manifestation. I want to see that form of Yours.

 

Today is Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa Pratipada — the first day after the full moon of Pūrṇimā, a day of new beginnings and fresh vision. The nakṣatra is Śravaṇa, the "ear" star of Viṣṇu, transitioning to Dhanisṭha (from 8:13 AM), whose deity is the Vasus and whose symbol is the mṛdaṅga — the drum of cosmic rhythm. Thursday is Gurodvāra, ruled by Bṛhaspati (Jupiter), the Guru of the gods who governs divine knowledge and the dharma-sūtras. The Moon remains in Makara rāśi until 9:07 PM, then enters Kumbha (Aquarius) — moving from Capricorn's discipline into the field of collective liberation. The yoga transitions from Āyuṣman (longevity) to Saubhagya (radiant fulfillment).

 

BG 11.3 is not a casual request. It is the opening question of the entire Viśvarūpa Darśana — the most intense revelation in the Gītā. Arjuna has just heard Kṛṣṇa describe His own glories in Chapter 10 — "I am the source of all that appears and disappears" — and in Chapter 11.2, he acknowledges hearing about appearance and disappearance from Kṛṣṇa. In 11.3, he moves from theoretical knowledge to direct demand: draṣṭum icchāmi te rūpam aiśvaram — "I want to see Your form." This is the pivotal moment where devotion transitions from relationship- Bhakti to darśana — direct vision.

 

The word puruṣottama is significant. Arjuna addresses Kṛṣṇa as "the best of all persons" — puruṣa + uttama. In the Sāṅkhya schema, puruṣa is the conscious witness, eternally distinct from prakṛti's modifications. By calling Kṛṣṇa "puruṣottama," Arjuna is not merely flattering a friend. He is identifying Kṛṣṇa as the supreme puruṣa — the witness who is not merely observing but actively entering into the cosmic manifestation. Kṛṣṇa as puruṣottama is the singular consciousness that permeates all lokāḥ while remaining aloof, just as YS 4.24 describes citta purified functioning for puruṣa's purposes.

 

The transition from Pūrṇimā to Pratipada mirrors this shift in consciousness. Yesterday's Pūrṇimā held the fullness of Pūrṇa — complete knowledge, the compiler's work finished. But completeness is not the end; it is the gateway. Pratipada is the first step after completion, and that step is always toward darśana — direct seeing. The cosmic drum of Dhanisṭha (mṛdaṅga) sounds today; its rhythm is the heartbeat of prakṛti's creative play, the cosmic manifestation throwing itself into form. Kṛṣṇa says "paśya" — see — not "śṛṇu" (hear). What began as śruti in Śravaṇa nakṣatra transforms today into direct perception in Dhanisṭha. The ear becomes the eye; hearing becomes seeing; Śravaṇa becomes Darśana. This is the progression of every genuine spiritual path: from hearing about the truth to beholding it directly.

 

YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION

YS 1.23: īśvara-praṇidhānāt vā — "By devotion to Īśvara, one can attain samādhi." Īśvara here is the special puruṣa — untouched by afflictions, karma, vipāka, and aśaya. Kṛṣṇa as puruṣottama is precisely this Īśvara. Arjuna's request is not based on speculative strength; it is based on praṇidhāna — total surrender. The Yoga Sūtras state that Īśvara is the "guru of even the ancients, because He is not limited by time" (YS 1.26). Arjuna's "I wish to see Your form" is praṇidhāna in its purest form: the devotee surrendering the claim to know in favor of direct revelation.

YS 3.27-28: tadā viveka-kaṇṭha-śreṇi-dvau carame — in the state of highest discernment, the sun/moon/yogis become visible in their own light. Arjuna's request introduces the chapter where the Viśvarūpa will be revealed — a form that is not limited by the material senses. The YS describes this as the culmination of abhyāsa and vairāgya: when the citta becomes like a clear crystal, it reflects puruṣa's light without distortion. The Viśvarūpa is what happens when the reflecting surface of prakṛti becomes transparent to the puruṣa — all faces, all forms, all colors, all dimensions simultaneously visible.

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Pratipada Darśana: This is the day of new vision. After Pūrṇimā's fullness — full moon, full knowledge of Kṛṣṇa as the source — the natural response is the wish to see. Today, before your practice, sit and ask the same question Arjuna asked: "Where have You entered? Where are You within this form?" Don't settle for abstract answers. Seek darśana — direct contact. Chant one round with the explicit sankalpa: "Not just to know about You, but to see You." This is how Chapter 11 begins.

Dhanisṭha Rhythm Meditation: Dhanisṭha's symbol is the mṛdaṅga — the drum. The rhythm of the material world is caused by prakṛti playing her drum, the three guṇas in cyclic permutation. Today, listen to the rhythm of your own citta-vṛttis. Not to suppress them, but to recognize them as cosmic rhythm. When citta becomes steady (YS 1.2), the drum's sound becomes musikā — the transcendental sound of the flute of Kṛṣṇa. Rhythm that leads inward toward puruṣa is nāda; rhythm that leads outward toward sense-objects is noise. Practice sensing the difference.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

puruṣottama (noun) — "the best of all persons"; puruṣa (conscious witness) + uttama (supreme). Kṛṣṇa as the supreme puruṣa who enters into the cosmic manifestation while remaining its source. The one witness who is not merely observing but actively sustaining all lokāḥ.

aiśvaram (adjective) — divine, relating to Īśvara. The form that Kṛṣṇa reveals in the Viśvarūpa is aiśvara — it is not a mental projection but the actual display of the Lord's unlimited power.

darśana (noun) — seeing, vision, especially the sacred vision of the Divine. In the Gītā context, it means direct beholding of the cosmic form, not merely hearing about it. The progression: śravaṇam → darśanam → smaraṇam → bhajana.

viveka-kaṇṭha-śreṇi (YS 3.27 reference) — "the hair-ridge of highest discrimination." The YS uses vivid anatomical imagery: when the yogi's viveka ripens fully, even the hair tips on the crown become eyes/ears that perceive subtlest realms. Arjuna's request shows viveka at the level of the throat — fully articulated, ready to ascend.

mṛdaṅga (noun) — the clay drum of Dhanisṭha. Symbolically, the heartbeat of prakṛti's creative līlā. Its teacherly significance: rhythm precedes melody. Before you can hear the flute, you must first find your own drum — the steady beat of svādhyāya and abhyāsa.

Verse Selection Rationale

Why BG 11.3 on Pratipada? This is the first question of Chapter 11 — the gateway into the Viśvarūpa Darśana. Yesterday's Pūrṇimā closed BG Chapter 10 with "Of the sages I am Vyāsa" — the compiler giving us the text. Today's Pratipada opens BG Chapter 11 with "I want to see that form of Yours" — the text's purpose fulfilled in direct vision. This is the perfect narrative arc: śruti → darśana. The ear that received the śāstra yesterday now demands to see the source of that śāstra. This is not a repeat; it is the natural next step in the paramparā's rhythm: hear the teaching → want to see the teacher.

Why on Dhanisṭha? Dhanisṭha's symbol is the mṛdaṅga — the drum that establishes cosmic rhythm. The verse is Arjuna's heart opening to the cosmic vision. The drum's sound is nāda, and the Viśvarūpa reveals Kṛṣṇa through nāda-rūpa — diverse sounds and forms simultaneously. Dhanisṭha governs music and rhythm because it is presided over by the Vasus, who embody the luminous, creative principle of prakṛti. Arjuna's request is the first beat of that drum: the oscillation between form and formlessness, between the personal Kṛṣṇa and the universal Kṛṣṇa.

Why Guru's Day? Thursday is Gurodvāra, ruled by Bṛhaspati — the planet that governs dharma, wisdom, and the Guru-tattva. The verse is Arjuna addressing the Supreme Teacher. "Teach me to see." The day's ruler amplifies the disposition toward seeking knowledge directly from Īśvara.
