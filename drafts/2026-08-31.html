ज्ञेयं यत्तत्प्रवक्ष्यामि यज्ज्ञात्वāmṛtam aśnute । अनादिमत्परं ब्रह्म न सत्तन्नासदुच्यते ॥ १३ ॥
jñeyaṁ yat tat pravakṣyāmi yaj jñātvāmṛtam aśnute anādi mat-paraṁ brahma na sat tan nāsad ucyate
I shall now explain the knowable, knowing which you will taste the eternal. Brahman, the spirit, beginningless and subordinate to Me, lies beyond the cause and effect of this material world.

Special Observance: Sankashti Chaturthi (Ganesha Chaturthi)

Location: Philadelphia, PA (loc=5128581)

Today's celestial alignment marks the beginning of the waning moon cycle under the auspices of Sankashti Chaturthi, dedicated to Lord Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. The Moon's transit through Revati (nourished by Pushan, the celestial guardian) until late afternoon, then shifting to Ashwini (the star of healing and new beginnings), mirrors the devotee's journey: first receiving divine nourishment and completion, then embarking on fresh endeavors with renewed vitality.

The yoga transition from Ganda (the knot, representing challenges and entanglements) to Vridhi (growth and increase) signifies how obstacles, when approached with devotion, transform into opportunities for spiritual expansion. Similarly, the karaṇa shift from Bava (associated with prosperity) to Balava (sacredness) and then Kaulava (social enjoyment) reflects the day's progression from material success to sacred celebration and community harmony.

Bhagavad Gita 13.13 reveals the knowable Brahman as beginningless (anādi), beyond cause and effect, and the source of immortal nectar (amṛta). This aligns perfectly with today's energies: just as Ganesha clears the path for new undertakings, the realization of the eternal Self clears the path of ignorance, allowing one to taste the nectar of liberation. The Supersoul's omnipresence, described in verse 14, echoes Ganesha's pervasive presence as the Lord of beginnings and obstacles.

Thus, today invites us to invoke Ganesha's grace at the outset of any endeavor, recognizing that the true remover of obstacles is the knowledge of the imperishable Self. By fixing our mind on the beginningless Brahman, we convert every challenge into a stepping stone toward eternal growth.

Today's Practice

Today, honor Ganesh by removing one internal obstacle:

  • Identify a mental knot: Notice a recurring pattern of doubt, fear, or procrastination that binds you.
  • Offer it to the Divine: Visualize placing this knot at the feet of Ganesha, chanting "Om Gam Ganapataye Namaha."
  • Replace with nectar: Immediately after, affirm: "I am the eternal knower, beyond all obstacles." Feel the influx of courage and clarity.

Perform this not as a ritual but as a spontaneous surrender, allowing the Divine to untangle what you cannot.

Yoga Sutra Bridge

BG 13.13's description of the beginningless, eternal Brahman finds its classical Yoga counterpart in Sutra 1.3:

तद् द्रष्टुर्भ svarूपे अवस्थानम् ..
tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe ‘vasthānam

Translation: "When that is accomplished, the seer abides in its own true nature."

This sutra describes the result of yoga: the cessation of mind-fluctuations (citta-vṛtti-nirodha) leads to the seer (draṣṭu) resting in its own true nature (svarūpa), which is the puruṣa – the eternal, beginningless consciousness. BG 13.13's "anādi mat‑paraṁ brahma" (beginningless Brahman subordinate to the Lord) is precisely this puruṣa, the knower of the field, which is ever‑free from the changes of prakṛti. Where the Bhagavad Gita presents this knowledge as the knowable that yields immortality, the Yoga Sutras offer the direct method to realize it: still the mind and abide as the Self.

In computational terms: BG 13.13 describes the eternal firmware (the Self) that underlies all changing processes, while Yoga Sutra 1.3 provides the user interface for realizing that firmware through the cessation of mental modifications.

Sanskrit Terms

Anādi (अनादि)

Literal: Beginningless, without origin

Hacker Metaphor: Like a quine – a program that outputs its own source code, the beginningless Self contains within itself the cause of its own manifestation, needing no external origin.

Amṛta (अमृत)

Literal: Nectar, immortality

Hacker Metaphor: The ultimate self‑healing binary patch – when applied to the conditioned mind, it overwrites the mortality subroutine with eternal life, requiring no reboot.

Mat‑para (मत्पर)

Literal: Subordinate to Me, dependent on the Lord

Hacker Metaphor: A process that runs with elevated privileges only when invoked by the root user (the Lord) – the individual soul's powers are delegated, not inherent.

Ganda (गण्ड)

Literal: Knot, mixture, obstacle

Hacker Metaphor: A deadlock in the process scheduler – two threads each waiting for the other to release a resource, causing a complete halt of progress until divine intervention breaks the cycle.

Vridhi (वृद्धि)

Literal: Growth, increase, prosperity

Hacker Metaphor: A memory leak that is actually a feature – the process continuously allocates wisdom‑bytes, never freeing them because they accumulate as eternal knowledge.
