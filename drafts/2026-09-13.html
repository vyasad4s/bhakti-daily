Commentary: The Knowable Beyond Duality

Today’s cosmic landscape is defined by Tritiya – the third day of the waxing moon, where the foundation-building of Dwitiya gives way to active growth and manifestation. This creates a potent field for expansion and realization, as the energy supports bringing forth that which has been conceived. The Sun occupies Siṃha (Leo) while the Moon begins in Kanyā (Virgo) and transitions to Tula (Libra) at 03:56 PM, creating a solar-lunar connection that illuminates the regal, discerning, and balanced nature of spiritual realization. Chitra nakṣatra (until 04:25 AM) carries the energy of beauty, craftsmanship, and divine architecture, while Brahma yoga (until 03:16 AM) is explicitly auspicious for creation, sacredness, and virtuous expansion. Ravivāra (Sunday) enhances solar vitality, enthusiasm, and the courage to pursue spiritual truths.

BG 13.13 reveals the supreme object of knowledge: jñeyam (the knowable) that, when known, grants amṛtam (immortality/nectar). The Lord declares that He will now explain this knowable - Brahman as spirit (brahman), beginningless (anādi), and subordinate to Him (mat-param) - existing beyond the dualities of sat (existence) and asat (non-existence). This directly addresses the Tritiya condition where after establishing foundations, we focus on manifestation and growth. Just as a seed that has established roots now pushes forth toward the sunlight, the spiritual aspirant now realizes that which transcends all limitations. This same realization is both expansive and precise - it opens awareness to the infinite while grounding it in direct experience.

The hacker’s lens provides a precise metaphor: consider consciousness as a quantum computer operating beyond classical limitations – capable of superposition, entanglement, and processing that defies ordinary binary logic. The material world (prakṛti) operates as classical computing – deterministic, sequential, and bound by cause-effect chains. The individual soul (jīva) operates as a process running on both systems, capable of accessing quantum insights while functioning in classical reality. The Absolute Truth (brahman/pāramātman) operates at the quantum field level – the fundamental reality that enables both classical and quantum processes to occur. Like a quantum programmer who writes algorithms that exploit superposition and entanglement while ensuring classical compatibility, the spiritual aspirant cultivates realization of the knowable to access dimensions beyond ordinary perception while remaining functional in daily life. Chitra nakṣatra’s energy of divine craftsmanship supports the precise realization of this transcendental knowledge, while Brahma yoga’s energy of creation aids in manifesting these insights in tangible ways. Ravivāra provides the vitality to sustain this pursuit despite distractions, while Kanya→Tula Chandra Rashi ensures the discernment and balance needed to integrate these truths without losing stability.

Yoga Sutra Bridge

BG 13.13’s revelation of the knowable beyond duality finds its classical Yoga counterpart in Sutras 1.2-1.3 (the definition and goal of yoga) and 2.28 (the means to realization):

योगश्चित्त-वृत्त-ниродहः ..
yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ
तदा द्रष्टुः स्वरูпе अवस्थानम् ..
tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe ‘vasthānam
त्र्यङ्गे अनुष्ठानाद् अशुद्धिक्षये ज्ञानदिघ्तिर-asmin व vivenka-discrimination ..
trayāṅge anuṣṭhānād aśuddhi-kṣaye jñāna-dṛṣṭiḥ

Translation: "Yoga is the cessation of the fluctuations of the mind. Then the seer abides in their own true nature. Through practice of the three limbs of yoga, when impurities are destroyed, there arises spiritual discernment."

These sutras describe the process (yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ), the goal (tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe ‘vasthānam), and the means to realization (trayāṅge anuṣṭhānād aśuddhi-kṣaye jñāna-dṛṣṭiḥ). BG 13.13’s declaration that knowing the knowable leads to tasting the eternal (amṛtam aśnute) directly aligns with the Yoga Sutras’ promise that through cessation of mental fluctuations, the seer abides in their true nature - which is beginningless (anādi) and beyond existence/non-existence duality. Where the Bhagavad Gita presents Brahman as beginningless and subordinate to the Supreme Lord, the Yoga Sutras present the puruṣa (true self) as eternal consciousness that, when realized, reveals its intrinsic nature as pure awareness.

In computational terms: BG 13.13 describes the Absolute as both the quantum field underlying all reality (transcendent foundation) and the conscious observer capable of collapsing wave functions (immanent functionality), while the Yoga Sutras’ citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ provides the meditation techniques for stabilizing the quantum processor of consciousness, and svarūpe ‘vasthānam describes the state where the processor operates at its native capacity without interference - like a quantum computer running error-free algorithms that reveal fundamental truths about reality itself.

Today's Practice

  • Transcendental Meditation: Sit in silence and inquire into the nature of the knower – who is it that knows thoughts, feelings, and sensations? Rest as pure awareness beyond identification with body or mind.

  • Hartalika Teej Observance: If married or in a committed relationship, honor the divine union principle by expressing gratitude and love to your partner as manifestations of Shiva and Shakti. If single, meditate on the inner union of Shiva and Shakti within your own being.

Perform this not as technique but as surrender, allowing the buddhi to discern what the senses grasp in duality.
