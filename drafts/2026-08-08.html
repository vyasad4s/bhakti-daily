Bhagavad-gītā 11.36

arjuna uvāca
sthāne hṛṣīkeśa tava prakīrtyā
jagat prahṛṣyaty anurajyate ca
rakṣāṁsi bhītāni diśo dravanti
sarve namasyanti ca siddha-saṅghāḥ

arjuna uvāca — Arjuna said; sthāne — rightly; hṛṣīkeśa — O master of all senses; tava — Your; prakīrtyā — by the glories; jagat — the world; prahṛṣyati — rejoices; anurajyate — becomes attached; ca — and; rakṣāṁsi — demons; bhītāni — frightened; diśaḥ — in all directions; dravanti — flee; sarve — all; namasyanti — offer respects; ca — also; siddha-saṅghāḥ — the gathered perfected beings

Arjuna said: O master of the senses, the world becomes joyful upon hearing Your name, and thus everyone becomes attached to You. Although the perfected beings offer You their respectful homage, the demons are afraid, and they flee here and there. All this is rightly done.

Cosmic Context — Panchāṅga Analysis (August 8, 2026, Philadelphia)

Tithi: Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa Ekādaśī (04:29 AM – Aug 9, 01:35 AM). Kamika Ekādaśī — one of the most sacred Ekādaśīs in the Vaiṣṇava calendar, devoted to the worship of Viṣṇu in His Mohininātha form. Ekādaśī is the dissolution tithi: the eleventh lunar day that breaks open attachments, purifies the body and mind, and creates the conditions for liberation. Here in BG 11.36, Arjuna describes the cosmic result of hearing the Lord's glories: demons flee, perfected beings bow. This is the inner Ekādaśī effect made manifest in the cosmic form.

Nakṣatra: Rohini → Mṛgśirṣa (transition at 7:21 AM). Rohini, ruled by Prajāpati (Brahmā), is the creative, stable, abundance-nakṣatra. Its devatā is Prajāpati, the cosmic progenitor. The transition to Mṛgśirṣa — the deer's head, the seeking mind, ruled by Soma — moves from settled creation to restless seeking. Rohini's stability is what allows the perfected beings to bow without fear; Mṛgśirṣa's seeking is what drives the demons to flee rather than surrender.

Yoga: Vyāghāta → Harṣaṇa (transition at 8:07 PM). Vyāghāta — "the attacker, the obstacle-smasher." This yoga carries the force that shatters fixed positions, that forces confrontation with what has become rigid. In BG 11.36, Vyāghāta operates as the mechanism by which demonic consciousness flees — it cannot bear the Lord's glories, so it runs. Harṣaṇa (joy, delight) follows — the state achieved once the obstacles are cleared and the perfected beings can bow.

Karaṇa: Bava → Balava (3:04 PM). Bava — the karana of material prosperity, suitable for beginnings. Balava — the karana of strength and courage, suitable for confronting obstacles. The Ekādaśī dissolution is supported by Balava's courage: you need strength to let attachments break.

Vara: Śanivāra (Saturday). Saturn's day — the day of karma, discipline, delay, and slow working-out. Saturday is not a day for quick wins; it is the day when what is genuinely yours surfaces and what is not yours falls away. BG 11.36 describes exactly this process: the demons discover they are not genuine and flee; the perfected beings find their home and bow.

Special Observances: Kamika Ekādaśī AND Rohini Vrat converging on a Śanivāra — this is a day of deep purification. The three-fold observance (Ekādaśī + Rohini + Saturday) creates a convergence of forces: dissolution (Ekādaśī), creation/abundance (Rohini), and karmic processing (Śanivāra).

Verse Selection Rationale

Kamika Ekādaśī demands a verse about the dissolution of what is false and the revelation of what is true. BG 11.36 delivers exactly this: Arjuna, witnessing the cosmic form's display, observes that demons flee and perfected beings bow. The Lord's glories act as a discriminating agent — they do not convert everyone; they reveal each being's essential nature.

The Vyāghāta yoga is the decisive factor. Vyāghāta means "the attacker" — the force that shatters comfortable illusions. In this verse, the glories of the Lord attack the demons' self-delusion and they are destroyed. For the siddhas, the same glories confirm what they already know and they bow in recognition. The yoga name and the verse content are identical: the Lord's glories are the Vyāghāta that breaks what is brittle and confirms what is genuine.

Rohini nakshatra adds the creative dimension: Prajāpati's creation is not neutral — it includes both the sacred and the demonic. The perfected beings and the demons both arise from the same creation. The Lord's glories do not create discrimination; they reveal the discrimination that already exists within each being's heart.

Śanivāra confirms the karmic reading: everything this verse describes is already in progress. The demons are already fleeing; the siddhas are already bowing. The verse is not about a new event — it is about recognition of what is already happening at the cosmic level.

Yoga Sūtra Connection: YS 2.47 — prayatna śaithilyānanta samāpatattra

YS 2.47 states: prayatna śaithilyānanta samāpatattra — "Effort [in asana] is relaxed when the effort to unite with the infinite is attained." Patanjali describes the moment when striving ends because union has been achieved — not by force but by grace, not by tightening effort but by yielding into something larger.

BG 11.36 demonstrates this exact dynamic. The demons strived to maintain their positions, their armies, their plans. When the Lord's glories manifest, their effort collapses — they do not surrender through yoga; they are destroyed by their own inability to tolerate the truth. The siddhas, by contrast, are already in a state of ananta samāpatti — their effort has already been relaxed into limitless absorption. When they hear the Lord's glories, they bow not in fear but in recognition: this is what they have been seeking all along.

The distinction is critical for the practitioner. Vyāghāta yoga will attack your practice — not by external obstacles but by revealing whether your effort is sincere (siddha) or performative (demon-like). When the going gets hard, the fake drops away immediately. The real cannot be killed. BG 11.36 asks: when your practice becomes difficult, do you flee or do you bow?

YS 1.23 — īśvara-praṇidhānād vā — reinforces this. The perfected beings (siddhas) are siddhas precisely because they have taken refuge in Īśvara. Their bowing is not new devotion; it is the consummation of a lifelong orientation toward the Lord.

Practical Application for the Day

For the Ekādaśī practitioner: Kamika Ekādaśī is not a fasting ritual — it is a psychological event. The day's energy breaks attachments by showing you the futility of holding what cannot be held. The demons fleeing in verse 36 are not external beings; they are your own rāga-dveṣa patterns — the attachments and aversions that run the software of your life. When you undertake Ekādaśī, you are inviting the Vyāghāta force to run through your citta and reveal what will hold and what will fall.

For the Rohini Vrat observer: Rohini's creative energy asks: what are you creating? The verse says the world rejoices and becomes attached to the Lord's name. On Rohini, your creative impulse is naturally directed toward bhakti. Use this day to create something devotional — a new mantra, a new offering, a new chapter of service. Let Prajāpati's creative force flow through Ekādaśī's purification into something genuinely new.

For the Śanivāra observer: Saturday is the slow reveal. The demons don't flee all at once; they trickle away over time. Your attachments will not all dissolve in one day. What matters is the direction. The siddhas bow gradually, consistently, until one day you notice you are bowing without thinking about it. This is the Saturnine path: slow, relentless, honest about what is real.

Discernment in practice: The verse offers a precise diagnostic tool. When you engage with bhakti and feel drawn, peaceful, more yourself — that is the siddha response. When you engage with bhakti and feel defensive, irritated, finding reasons to dismiss it — that is the demonic response. Neither is a judgment; both are information. Arjuna's observation in verse 36 is not condemnation; it is accurate assessment of what is.

Sanskrit Terms

sthāne (adv.) — "rightly, fitly, appropriately"; Arjuna's assessment that the Lord's actions are correct in every instance. This is not passive acceptance; it is the culmination of discriminative knowledge that recognizes the Lord's justice is always precisely calibrated.

hṛṣīkeśa (n.) — "master of all senses"; a name of Kṛṣṇa acknowledging His control over every indriya, internal and external. The hṛṣīka — senses, mind — are not autonomous; they belong to Īśvara. When this is recognized, the senses stop running wild and become instruments of bhakti.

rakṣāṁsi (n.) — "demons, rākṣasas"; beings who operate from ego-identification and who cannot tolerate the Lord's glories. Not metaphysical monsters but the inner pattern of defending a false identity. When the truth is spoken, they flee.

siddha-saṅghāḥ (n.) — "the gathered perfected beings"; those whose practice is complete, whose citta is purified, who recognize the Lord immediately upon seeing Him. They bow not out of fear but from recognition — like a long-lost friend finally found.

Vyāghāta (n.) — "the attacker, the obstacle-smasher"; the yoga active today. Vyāghāta does not discriminate between valuable and worthless attachments — it smashes all of them. This is not gentle. Ekādaśī is not gentle. Those who need gentleness should practice on a day without Vyāghāta yoga.

prakīrtyā (n.) — "by the glories, by the recounting"; the mechanism by which the verse's transformation occurs. The Lord's glories are not abstract philosophy; they are active. When you speak or hear the Lord's glories, reality reorganizes around them. The demons recoil; the perfected beings advance.

Summary

BG 11.36 is Arjuna's recognition that the Lord's glories operate as a cosmic discriminatory force: they do not change beings, but they reveal what beings already are. The perfected beings bow — not because they have been converted, but because they finally see clearly. The demons flee — not because they have been punished, but because their structure cannot tolerate the truth.

Kamika Ekādaśī + Rohini Vrat + Śanivāra create a day where this revelation is maximally available. Ekādaśī breaks the false. Rohini generates new creation from what remains. Śanivāra processes the karma of what was broken, slowly, thoroughly. The practice is to let this three-fold force work without interference — do not rescue your fleeing demons; do not interfere with the bowing of what is genuine in you.

YS 2.47 whispers the same teaching: true effort is not effort against something but effort that relaxes into the infinite. The perfected beings have already done this work. The demons are still running from it. Today asks: which direction are you moving?
