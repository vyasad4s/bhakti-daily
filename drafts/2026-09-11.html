Bhagavad Gita Chapter 13, Verse 29

समं पश्यन्हि सर्वत्र समवस्थितमीश्वरम् । न हिनस्त्यात्मनात्मानं ततो याति परां गतिम् ॥ २९ ॥
samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram na hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ tato yāti parāṁ gatim
One who sees the Supersoul equally present everywhere, in every living being, does not degrade himself by his mind. Thus he approaches the transcendental destination.
From a first-principles Sankhya-Yoga perspective, this verse reveals the egalitarian vision of the realized soul who perceives the one Supreme Lord dwelling equally in all beings. The field (kṣetra) is prakṛti manifest as the body-mind complex, governed by the three gunas and their modifications. The knower of the field (kṣetra-jña) is puruṣa, pure consciousness that illuminates but does not act. Yet here we encounter the transformative realization: samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram – "One who sees the Supersoul equally situated everywhere." This vision directly addresses the Chandra Darshan (first sighting of the moon) energy of today, where after the darkness of Amavasya, the slender crescent moon reappears, reminding us that the same divine light illuminates all creation. Just as the moon's light reflects the same sun whether seen on a dewdrop or the ocean, the Supersoul shines equally in the ant and the elephant, the saint and the sinner. The verse further states na hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ – "does not degrade himself by his mind." When one sees the divine equality in all beings, envy, hatred, and superiority complexes dissolve. The mind, which typically degrades itself through comparison and judgment, becomes purified when it rests in the perception of universal divinity. This mental purification is the prerequisite for tato yāti parāṁ gatim – "then he approaches the transcendental destination." This understanding aligns perfectly with Chandra Darshan, a special observance celebrating the first glimpse of the waxing moon after Amavasya. Just as the moon's reappearance signals the return of light, the realization of the Supersoul's equal presence signals the return of spiritual clarity. The darkness of comparison and distinction gives way to the light of unity – not a uniformity that erases individuality, but a recognition that the same divine consciousness animates all diverse forms. The Supersoul is further described as samavasthitam – "equally situated." This is not a claim that all beings are identical in their external circumstances, but that the divine essence within them is one and the same. Like electricity powering different appliances, the same consciousness powers different bodies, each functioning according to its unique design while sharing the same fundamental source.

Commentary: The Vision of Divine Equality

Today’s cosmic landscape is defined by Chandra Darshan – the first sighting of the waxing moon after the Amavasya (new moon). This creates a potent field for hope and renewal, as the return of lunar light symbolizes the return of clarity after darkness. The Sun occupies Siṃha (Leo) while the Moon begins in Siṃha and transitions to Kanyā at 09:38 AM, creating a solar-lunar connection that illuminates the regal, discerning nature of truth-seeking. Uttara Phalguni nakṣatra (until 03:25 AM) carries the energy of friendship, patronage, and communal harmony, while Subha yoga (until 05:38 AM) is explicitly auspicious for virtuous actions and positive outcomes. Śukravāra (Friday) enhances creativity and aesthetic sensibility through Venus’s influence.

BG 13.29 cuts to the heart of spiritual vision: the realized soul sees the one Supersoul equally present in all beings. The verse declares samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram – "One who sees the Supersoul equally situated everywhere" – directly addressing the Chandra Darshan condition where the returning lunar light reminds us of the ever-present divine illumination. Just as the moon’s reappearance after Amavasya creates hope in the night sky, the recognition of divine equality creates hope in the human heart. This same Truth is both samam (equal) and samavasthitam (equally situated), revealing its impartial nature that sees no distinction between beings based on external qualities.

The hacker’s lens provides a precise metaphor: consider consciousness as the universal power grid – always present, always active, yet invisible when we focus only on the individual appliances. The material world (prakṛti) operates as the diverse electrical devices – visible, tangible, and constantly varying in form and function. The individual soul (jīva) operates as a specific device drawing power from the grid, with its unique voltage requirements and operational characteristics. The Absolute Truth (brahman/pāramātman) operates at the power station level – the fundamental source that enables all operations to occur regardless of the appliance type. Like a smart grid that automatically adjusts to serve all connected devices efficiently, the Absolute pervades all levels while remaining distinct from any single manifestation. Uttara Phalguni nakṣatra’s energy of harmonious connection supports recognizing this universal source, while Subha yoga’s energy of virtuous action aids in aligning one’s conduct with this vision of equality.

Together, these energies support the direct perception of the Absolute as the ever-present equal light – the core realization celebrated when the moon returns its face after darkness.

Yoga Sutra Bridge

BG 13.29’s description of seeing the divine equally in all beings finds its classical Yoga counterpart in Sutra 1.36:

विशोकावा ज्योतिषमती ..
viśokā vā jyotiṣmatī

Translation: "Or from the inner light that is free from sorrow."

This sutra describes one of the methods for stabilizing the mind: focusing on the inner light (jyotiṣmatī) that is free from sorrow (viśoka). BG 13.29’s "samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram" (one who sees the Supersoul equally situated everywhere) and "na hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ" (does not degrade himself by his mind) describe this same inner light – not as an object to be grasped by the intellect, but as the very subject that perceives without sorrow. Where the Bhagavad Gita presents this truth as seeing the divine equality in all beings, the Yoga Sutras offer the direct method to realize it: turn the mind inward to perceive the light that is free from sorrow, which naturally manifests as equal vision.

In computational terms: BG 13.29 describes the Absolute as both the universal power supply (transcendent energy source) and the uniform voltage flowing through all circuits (impartial energy), while Yoga Sutra 1.36 provides the user interface for realizing that one’s inner perception (the observer) is non-different from this light – like a voltage meter confirming that all circuits receive the same pure, undistorted power regardless of their individual resistance.

Today's Practice

Today, honor the vision of divine equality by cultivating equal vision:

  • Equal Vision Meditation: Sit quietly and bring to mind three different beings – a loved one, a stranger, and someone you find challenging. Not as visualization, but as direct recognition of the same divine consciousness animating all three forms.

  • Moon Offering: If possible, glimpse the waxing moon tonight and offer a simple prayer: "Om samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram" – recognizing that the same light that shines on the moon shines equally in all beings.

  • Mindful Speech: Throughout the day, observe when your mind tends to degrade itself through comparison (hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ). When noticed, gently redirect your attention to the equal divine presence in yourself and others.

Perform this not as technique but as surrender, allowing the buddhi to discern what the senses grasp in duality.
