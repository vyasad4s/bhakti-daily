Bhagavad-gītā 11.12

दिवि सूर्यसहस्रस्य भवेद्युगपदुत्थिता ।
यदि भा: सदृशी सा स्याद्भ‍ासस्तस्य महात्मन: ॥ १२ ॥
divi sūrya-sahasrasya
bhaved yugapad utthitā
yadi bhāḥ sadṛśī sā syād
bhāsas tasya mahātmanaḥ
If hundreds of thousands of suns were to rise at once into the sky, their radiance might resemble the effulgence of the Supreme Person in that universal form.
Synonyms: divi — in the sky; sūrya — of suns; sahasrasya — of many thousands; bhavet — there were; yugapat — simultaneously; utthitā — present; yadi — if; bhāḥ — light; sadṛśī — like that; sā — that; syāt — might be; bhāsaḥ — effulgence; tasya — of Him; mahā-ātmanaḥ — the great Lord.

What Arjuna saw was indescribable, yet Sañjaya is trying to give a mental picture of that great revelation to Dhṛtarāṣṭra. Neither Sañjaya nor Dhṛtarāṣṭra was present, but Sañjaya, by the grace of Vyāsa, could see whatever happened. Thus he now compares the situation, as far as it can be understood, to an imaginable phenomenon (i.e., thousands of suns).

The universal form (viśva-rūpa) is not an ordinary vision. It is the cosmic manifestation concentrated into a single darshan — all planets, all beings, all time, all space, simultaneously present in the body of the Supreme. The radiance is not metaphorical; it is the actual tejas (effulgence) of the Lord's personal potency, which sustains and illumines every universe. Material suns are but fragmented reflections of that one original light. When Kṛṣṇa reveals His viśva-rūpa, the conditioned soul receives a direct transmission of that supreme effulgence — not through the mind's speculation, but through divya-cakṣu, divine vision granted by grace.

Today's cosmic configuration makes this verse unavoidable. Rohini Nakṣatra (Aldebaran, the "red one," Brahma's own star) rises at 9:13 AM — a fixed star of immense brilliance in the eye of the bull, Vṛṣabha. Dhruva Yoga (the "fixed/immovable" yoga) holds until night — named for the pole star, the unmoving pivot around which the heavens rotate. Navami Tithi, the ninth waning phase, marks a threshold of completion before the final descent to Amāvāsyā. The cosmos is literally staging the verse: a supremely brilliant star (Rohini/Aldebaran) in a fixed, immovable configuration (Dhruva), on a day of reckoning (Navami). The "thousands of suns" are not poetic hyperbole — they are the astronomical reality of what the viśva-rūpa's effulgence surpasses.

Why BG 11.12 Today

The verse selection is not arbitrary — it is forced by the day's cosmic architecture:

Rohini Nakṣatra (9:13 AM onward): Aldebaran (α Tauri), the "red one," the eye of the bull, Brahma's own star. A red giant 44× the Sun's diameter, 150× its luminosity. When the Moon transits Rohini, the cosmic spotlight falls on creative potency, generative power, the "red" ray of manifestation. BG 11.12's "hundreds of thousands of suns" is the only adequate metaphor for Rohini's stellar reality — and the viśva-rūpa's effulgence surpasses even that.

Dhruva Yoga (all day): Dhruva = fixed, immovable, the pole star. The yoga of unwavering position. The viśva-rūpa is the ultimate Dhruva — the one fixed point (Kṛṣṇa) in whom all moving universes are suspended. "Everything rests upon Me as pearls on a string" (BG 7.7). Today's yoga names the verse's subject: the mahā-ātman whose effulgence is the immutable ground of all mutable light.

Navamī Tithi: The 9th waning phase — completion energy, the final reckoning before the dark moon. Arjuna's vision on the battlefield was a Navamī moment: the culmination of the Gītā's teaching, the final proof before the war. We stand at a similar threshold. The viśva-rūpa darshan is not entertainment; it is the final argument that settles all doubt. Navamī demands: have you seen?

Moon in Vṛṣabha (Taurus): The Moon is exalted here — stable, grounded, sensually receptive. But Rohini in Vṛṣabha is where the Moon's receptivity meets the star's creative fire. The vision of the universal form requires exactly this: a consciousness stable enough to receive (Vṛṣabha) and sensitive enough to be ignited (Rohini). Dhruva Yoga provides the stillness; Rohini provides the voltage.

YS 1.41: kṣīṇa-vṛtter abhijātasyeva maṇer grahītṛ-grahāṇa-grahyeṣu tat-stha-tad-añjanatā samāpattiḥ
When the mental modifications are attenuated, the mind becomes like a transparent jewel, taking on the color of whatever is placed near it — whether the knower, the knowing, or the known. This is samāpatti (coalescence/absorption).
