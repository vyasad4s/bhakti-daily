Bhagavad Gita Chapter 10, Verse 37

वृष्णीनां वासुदेवोऽस्मि पाण्डवानां धनञ्जय: । मुनीनामप्यहं व्यास: कवीनामुशना कवि: ॥ ३७ ॥

 

vṛṣṇīnāṁ vāsudevo ’smi pāṇḍavānāṁ dhanañ-jayaḥ munīnām apy ahaṁ vyāsaḥ kavīnām uśanā kaviḥ

 

Of the descendants of Vṛṣṇi I am Vāsudeva, and of the Pāṇḍavas I am Arjuna. Of the sages I am Vyāsa, and among great thinkers I am Uśanā.

 

Today is Pūrṇimā — the full moon — and not just any full moon. It is Vyāsa Pūjā, the day the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition honors Śrīla Vyāsadeva, the compiler of all Vedic literature. The nakṣatra is Śravaṇa, whose deity is Viṣṇu and whose very name means "hearing" — the act of receiving knowledge through śabda, sacred sound. The Moon entered Makara rāśi (Capricorn) at dawn, grounding the full moon's luminosity in the Saturn-ruled sign of discipline, structure, and endurance. The yoga Prīti (nourishment) transitions to Ayuṣman (long-lived) at 2:27 PM — from the sweetness of devotion to the longevity that comes from living in alignment with dharma. On this day, Kṛṣṇa's declaration in BG 10.37 — "of the sages I am Vyāsa" — lands with the force of cosmic precision. It is not metaphor. It is not sentiment. It is the Supreme Lord identifying Himself as the very compiler of the knowledge that leads souls back to Him. Vyāsadeva is not merely one sage among many. He is the sage who structured the Vedic corpus into four, who composed the Mahābhārata, the Purāṇas, and the Brahma-sūtras. Without Vyāsa, the śruti would remain an undifferentiated mass; the path of return would be unparsed, uncompiled, inaccessible to the Kali-yuga mind. When Kṛṣṇa says "munīnām apy ahaṁ vyāsaḥ," He is declaring that the systematic transmission of spiritual knowledge IS His vibhūti — His divine opulence. The act of compiling, organizing, and transmitting truth is not a human achievement; it is Kṛṣṇa acting through His empowered servant. This is why Vyāsa Pūjā is not merely a birthday celebration — it is the recognition that knowledge itself is a manifestation of the Divine. The Śravaṇa nakṣatra deepens this connection. Śravaṇa means "the ear" — the organ of hearing, the primary instrument of śruti ("that which is heard"). In the Yoga Sūtras, Patañjali identifies three sources of right knowledge (pramāṇa): direct perception, inference, and āgama — verbal testimony from a trustworthy source (YS 1.7). Śruti is the highest form of āgama, and it flows through the guru-paramparā, the disciplic succession that traces back to Īśvara Himself, who is "the guru of even the ancients, because He is not limited by time" (YS 1.26). Vyāsa is the node in that succession where the infinite stream of Vedic knowledge was formatted for human reception. To honor Vyāsa is to honor the entire infrastructure of transmission — the paramparā that makes liberation possible in an age of forgetfulness. The full moon of Pūrṇimā represents this completeness: the Moon reflecting the Sun's full light, just as Vyāsa reflects the full light of Kṛṣṇa's knowledge into the world.

 

YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION

YS 1.7: pratyakṣa-anumāna-āgamāḥ pramāṇāni — "Right knowledge consists of direct perception, inference, and authoritative testimony." Vyāsadeva is the supreme āgama-transport — the trustworthy source who compiled śruti into accessible forms for the Kali-yuga mind.

YS 1.26: pūrveṣām api guruḥ kālenānavacchedāt — "Īśvara is the guru of even the ancients, because He is not limited by time." The guru-paramparā traces back to Kṛṣṇa Himself; Vyāsa is His empowered conduit. When Kṛṣṇa says "I am Vyāsa," He is identifying as the source of the paramparā — the unbroken chain of transmission that connects the eternal to the temporal.

TERM DEFINITIONS:

vṛṣṇīnām (genitive plural) — of the Vṛṣṇi dynasty, the Yadu lineage from which Kṛṣṇa descended; the royal house of Mathurā and Dvārakā.

vāsudevaḥ (noun) — Vāsudeva, Kṛṣṇa as the son of Vasudeva; also indicates Balarāma as the original expansion. Here, the Supreme among the Vṛṣṇis.

pāṇḍavānām (genitive plural) — of the Pāṇḍavas, the five sons of Pāṇḍu; the protagonists of the Mahābhārata.

dhanañ-jayaḥ (noun) — "Conqueror of Wealth," an epithet of Arjuna; he who amassed treasures through his bow and skill.

munīnām (genitive plural) — of the munis, the silent sages; those who have attained mastery over speech and mind through tapas.

vyāsaḥ (noun) — Vyāsadeva, "the compiler"; the avatar of Kṛṣṇa who divided the Veda into four (Ṛg, Yajur, Sāma, Atharva), composed the Mahābhārata, Purāṇas, and Brahma-sūtras. The greatest muni because he structured transcendental knowledge for mass transmission.

kavīnām (genitive plural) — of the kavis, the great thinkers; poets and intellectuals capable of deep penetration into any subject.

uśanā (noun) — Uśanā, also known as Śukrācārya; the preceptor of the asuras, master of politics and strategy; a kavi of unparalleled intelligence.

śravaṇa (noun, nakṣatra context) — "hearing"; the 22nd lunar mansion, ruled by Viṣṇu, symbolized by three footprints (Viṣṇu's strides). The power of listening as the gateway to knowledge.

āgama (noun, YS context) — authoritative verbal testimony; the third source of pramāṇa (valid knowledge) in Patañjali's system. Śruti — "that which is heard" — is the highest āgama.

paramparā (noun) — disciplic succession; the unbroken chain of guru-to-disciple transmission that preserves the integrity of śruti across generations.
