Bhagavad-gītā 14.24

समदु:खसुख: स्वस्थ: समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चन: । तुल्यप्रियाप्रियो धीरस्तुल्यनिन्दात्मसंस्तुति: ॥ २४ ॥
samaduḥkhasukhaḥ svasthaḥ samaloṣṭāśmakāñcanaḥ
tulyapriyāpriyo dhīrastullynindātmastutistuḥ
One who is equal in happiness and distress, steady, who considers a lump of earth, a stone and gold as the same, who is equal toward the desirable and undesirable, who is steady, who is equal in praise and blame, who is equal toward honor and dishonor, who is equal toward friend and foe, who has abandoned all fruitive activities — such a person is said to have transcended the modes of nature.

When a living entity rises to the platform of transcendence by his advancement in spiritual knowledge, he achieves the characteristics mentioned in this verse. He does not distinguish between happiness and distress because he knows that both are temporary and are products of the modes of material nature. He is fixed in his constitutional position as an eternal servitor of the Lord. He considers a lump of earth, a stone and gold as the same because he knows that they are all products of the three modes of material nature and are therefore temporary. He is equal toward the desirable and undesirable because he knows that both are temporary and are products of the modes of material nature. He is steady because he is fixed in his eternal position as a servitor of the Lord. He is equal in praise and blame because he knows that both are temporary and are products of the modes of material nature. He is equal toward honor and dishonor because he knows that both are temporary and are products of the modes of material nature. He is equal toward friend and foe because he knows that both are temporary and are products of the modes of material nature. He has abandoned all fruitive activities because he knows that they are products of the modes of material nature and are therefore temporary. Such a person is said to have transcended the modes of material nature.

Verse Selection Rationale

Today’s pañcāṅga features Vyatipata yoga (obstacles, calamitous influences) in the morning, transitioning to Variyan yoga (auspicious for good deeds) later, combined with Mrigashirsha nakṣatra (searching, gentle pursuit) shifting to Ardra (teardrop, sorrow, renewal) in the afternoon. Friday (Shukravāra) is ruled by Venus, governing love, beauty, and relationships. The Mahalakshmi Vrat Ends signifies the completion of a vow for prosperity and material fulfillment. This aligns perfectly with BG 14.24, which describes the transcendental person who maintains perfect equanimity amidst all dualities—happiness and distress, honor and dishonor, friend and foe—seeing all as temporary manifestations of the three modes. The Venusian influence of Friday supports the cultivation of beauty and harmony in equilibrium, while the transition from Vyatipata to Variyan yoga reflects the journey from overcoming obstacles through righteous action to engaging in virtuous deeds. The Sun in Virgo provides the discernment (viveka) to perceive the equality of all opposites, and the Moon in Gemini supports the adaptable communication needed to express this transcendental vision. Completing the Mahalakshmi Vrat signifies moving beyond mere material prosperity to the higher realization of BG 14.24.

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS II.48: tato dvandva-anabhighātaḥ — “Thence follows an absence of disturbance from dualities.”

Just as BG 14.24 describes the transcendental person who is equal in happiness and distress, steady, and equal toward all opposites, YS II.48 states that through pranayama practice, one achieves freedom from disturbance caused by dualities (dvandva). Both emphasize that transcending the pairs of opposites is a hallmark of spiritual advancement. In Sāṅkhya terms, this reflects the puruṣa’s ability to remain unwavering as the witness while prakṛti’s dualistic transformations (happiness/distress, honor/dishonor, etc.) continue to play out. The hacker’s equivalent: achieving root-level stability where system interrupts (dualistic fluctuations) no longer cause kernel panics—the process runs steadily regardless of input signals.

Morning Application

Meditate on the equality of all opposites: happiness and distress, honor and dishonor, friend and foe. Contemplate how recognizing the temporary nature of all material experiences allows you to remain steady amidst life’s fluctuations. Let this awareness stabilize your mind like a process running with elevated privileges that cannot be preempted by lower-priority tasks, and remember that true prosperity (as celebrated in the concluding Mahalakshmi Vrat) is found not in external accumulation but in inner equanimity.
