Bhagavad-gītā 15.6

न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः । यद्गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते तद्धाम परमं मम ॥ ६ ॥
na tad bhāsayate sūryo
na śaśāṅko na pāvakaḥ
yad gatvā na nivartante
tad dhāma paramaṁ mama
That supreme abode of Mine is not illumined by the sun or moon, nor by fire or electricity. Those who reach it never return to this material world.
List every source of light you have ever seen anything by: the sun, the moon, fire, electricity. Notice what they all have in common — every one of them is itself an object. A lamp is a thing you can look at. This verse makes a structural claim, not a poetic one: the supreme abode is not the kind of thing that can be illumined, because it is not an object standing in front of a beam. It is the substrate on which all beams become visible. You cannot shine a flashlight at the screen the movie is projected on and thereby see the screen. The screen is what makes the flashlight's cone visible in the first place. The dhāma is upstream of every lamp. From Sāṅkhya first principles this is exact, not mystical. Prakṛti is jaḍa — insentient, inert. Nothing in it is self-aware; nothing in it is self-luminous in the root sense. Every illuminator in the material field — including sūrya, the archetype of light itself — is an evolute: generated, dependent, running on fuel, exhaustible. The sun is a lamp with a fuel gauge. Puruṣa alone is cit — the witnessing light by which all contents are manifest. Consciousness is not an object in the visual field; it is the precondition of there being a visual field at all. That is why the sun does not illuminate the abode — the sun is an appearance IN consciousness, and an appearance cannot contain or reveal its precondition. You cannot see your own eye with your own eye. The instrument is forever downstream of the thing it would need to inspect. Now the second line: yad gatvā na nivartante — having gone, they do not return. First principles of "return": to come back requires having been displaced, and displacement requires distance. But if the destination is the seer's own nature — the dhāma that no lamp can light because it IS the light — then there was never any distance to begin with. The entire journey is the dissolution of the distance-illusion. You cannot come back from where you never actually left. What "returns" to saṁsāra is only the misidentification — the claim "I am this branch" — and that is precisely what is dropped at the boundary. Yesterday's verse swung the blade: cut the tree, then seek the place of no-return. Today the place is described. And the description is a shock: it is not far. It is what remains when every borrowed light — including the one you call "me" — is switched off. This is why the verse ends with mama — Mine. The abode is not a null state, not darkness. It is personal: the Lord's own dhāma, self-luminous, requiring nothing, lacking nothing. Not the extinguishing of light but its source. The waning moon outside your window this week is the sky's own commentary: all borrowed light diminishes. What does not diminish is the One whose light the sun itself borrows.

Verse Selection Rationale

Today is Ravivāra — the Sun's own day — and the verse selected dethrones the sun as light's source: na tad bhāsayate sūryaḥ, the abode the sun cannot illumine. The day's devatā points beyond itself; the sky arranges the lesson. Punarvasu, "the return of light," holds until 2:43 PM — and it is Aditi's star, the mother of the Ādityas, the sun-gods themselves. The mother of all suns carries the teaching: the light that returns each cycle is borrowed; the abode needs none. Yesterday's verse (BG 15.3–4) cut the aśvattha tree and commanded the search for "that place from which having gone, none return" — today 15.6 describes exactly that place, and the two-day arc completes: severance, then revelation. At 2:43 PM, Puṣya joins Sunday to form Ravi Pushya and Sarvartha Siddhi — "the accomplishment of all purposes" — which is the abode's other name, for one who reaches it wants nothing further: wanting itself was the symptom of distance. Śiva yoga (auspicious) replaces yesterday's Parigha (the iron bar) — the residue of the Kalashtami cut is now peace. The Moon enters Karka at 9:01 AM — Cancer, its own sign, the sign of shelter and home — while the tithi observance is Avidhava Navami, and the waning Moon itself performs the teaching: all borrowed light diminishes toward Amāvāsyā. The dhāma does not.

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS I.3: tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam — "Then the Seer abides in its own form."

YS IV.34: puruṣārtha-śūnyānāṁ guṇānāṁ pratiprasavaḥ kaivalyaṁ svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā vā citi-śaktir iti — "Kaivalya is the counter-flow of the guṇas, empty of purpose for puruṣa; it is the establishment of the power of consciousness in its own form."

The dhāma paramaṁ of BG 15.6 and the svarūpa of YS I.3 are two descriptions of one thing: the self-luminous, which no lamp can light because it is what lamps are lit FOR. Patañjali's entire technology — nirodha, the stilling of vṛttis — is not the manufacture of light but the removal of flicker: every fluctuation is a borrowed light wobbling across the screen of awareness. When the flicker stills, the Seer is found to have been shining the entire time. And YS IV.34 names the mechanism of Punarvasu's "return" with precision: pratiprasava — the counter-flow. The guṇas reverse and return to their source; the soul does not "go" anywhere — it is established (pratiṣṭhā) in its own citi-śakti, its own power of consciousness. Gītā and Sūtra meet at the same point from opposite directions: 15.6 says the abode needs no sun; IV.34 says the establishment IS the citi-śakti itself. No distance, no journey, no return — only the recognition of what was never absent.

Sanskrit Terms

  • bhāsayate (भासयते) (causative verb) — illumines, causes to shine; like rendering an object onto the display — but this abode is not in the scene graph; it is what the display runs on.
  • sūryaḥ (सूर्यः) (noun) — the sun; the system's primary external light source — itself a rendered object with a fuel gauge.
  • dhāma (धाम) (noun) — abode, domain, seat; the root directory of reality — except this one was never a subdirectory, and every path resolves into it.
  • paramam (परमम्) (superlative adjective) — supreme, beyond which nothing exists; the top of the stack — nothing further to pop.
  • nivartante (निवर्तन्ते) (verb) — they return, come back; a function with no return path — terminal call, the stack frame is never restored because there is nothing to restore it into.
  • gatvā (गत्वा) (gerund) — having gone; the paradox of arriving at your own nature — less a journey than an unmount of everything you were never mounted on.

Morning Application

Today, run a borrowed-light audit. Sunrise is at 7:00 AM — if you can, watch it, and as the sun climbs, remember what this verse says: the greatest lamp in your sky is a lamp. All day, notice what you depend on for light, literal and figurative — the screen's glow, another's approval, the shine of a result. Each is sūrya, śaśāṅka, pāvaka: an object, exhaustible, downstream. Then, for ten minutes, attempt the impossible on purpose: try to see the seer. You will fail — the eye cannot see itself — and that failure is the confirmation of 15.6, the fingerprint of the self-luminous. Rest in that which cannot be looked at. And at 2:43 PM, when Ravi Pushya and Sarvartha Siddhi open, make one single act a complete offering — work, meal, or word — performed as if the doer were light itself. That is how a lamp practices for the abode that needs none.
