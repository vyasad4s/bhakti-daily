Bhagavad-gītā BG 18.4

निश्चयं शृणु मे तत्र त्यागे भरतसत्तम ॥ ४ ॥ त्यागो हि पुरुषव्याघ्र त्रिविध: सम्प्रकीर्तित: ॥ ४ ॥
niścayaṁ śṛṇu me tatra
tyāge bharata-sattama
tyāgo hi puruṣa-vyāghra
tri-vidhaḥ samprakīrtitaḥ
O best of the Bhāratas, now hear My judgment about renunciation. O tiger among men, renunciation is declared in the scriptures to be of three kinds.
Saturday is Saturn's day — Śani, the judge, the auditor, the one who weighs every deed on the scales of karma. And here Kṛṣṇa delivers His *niścaya* (final judgment, definitive verdict) on the matter that has haunted the chapter: what is *tyāga*? The word *niścaya* means "certainty, conclusion, final decision" — it is not an opinion, not a suggestion, but the *niścaya* (settled truth) that comes from the Supreme Judge. He addresses Arjuna as *puruṣa-vyāghra* — "tiger among men," the one who can hunt down the truth. And the verdict is surgical: *tyāga* is *tri-vidhaḥ* — threefold. Not one, not two, but three. Because *prakṛti* operates in three *guṇas*, *tyāga* must also be threefold: *sāttvika* (renunciation in knowledge), *rājasika* (renunciation in passion), *tāmasika* (renunciation in ignorance). The judge has spoken; the code is compiled; the three branches are defined. Saturday is Śani's day — the day the audit is final, the appeal is denied, the verdict stands. The panchang amplifies: Pūrvāṣāḍhā (the former invincible, the first victory) yields to Uttarāṣāḍhā (the latter invincible, the final victory) at 9:54 AM — the first victory yields to the final victory. Śūla (the spear, the piercing pain) yields to Gāṇḍa (the knot, the obstruction) at 7:52 PM — the spear that pierces becomes the knot that binds. The tithi is Pañcamī (Labh Pañcamī, the fifth day of gain, the day the merchant opens new ledgers) — the day the books are balanced. Saturday is the day the audit closes; the threefold nature of *tyāga* is the final entry in the ledger. 🏹

The verse is Kṛṣṇa's *niścaya* (final judgment) on the central question of the chapter: what is *tyāga*? The panchang amplifies this: Pūrvāṣāḍhā (first victory) yields to Uttarāṣāḍhā (final victory) at 9:54 AM — the first hearing yields to the final verdict. Śūla (the spear) yields to Gāṇḍa (the knot) at 7:52 PM — the spear that pierces becomes the knot that binds. The tithi is Labh Pañcamī (the gain-day, the merchant's new ledger) — the day the books are opened and the threefold nature of *tyāga* is entered as the first entry. Saturday is the day the audit closes; the threefold *tyāga* is the final entry. No repeat: BG 18.1 (11-16), 18.2 (11-15), 18.3 (11-17), 18.5 (11-18); BG 18.4 fresh for Day 4. The verse is the judge's gavel: *niścayaṁ śṛṇu* — hear the final verdict. 🏹

Yoga Sutra Connection

YS II.5 — anityāśuci-duḥkhānātmasu nitya-śuci-sukhātmakhyātir avidyā — "Ignorance is regarding the impermanent as permanent, the impure as pure, the painful as pleasant, the non-Self as Self." The bridge is exact. BG 18.4's *niścaya* (final judgment on the threefold nature of *tyāga*) is the *viveka-khyāti* (discriminative discernment) that YS II.26 calls *viveka-khyātir aviplavā* — "unwavering discriminative discernment." The threefold *tyāga* (sāttvika, rājasika, tāmasika) corresponds to the three *guṇas* that *avidyā* misidentifies as Self. The *niścaya* (certainty) is the *viveka-khyāti* that discriminates *puruṣa* from *prakṛti*. Saturday is Śani's day — the planet that enforces the boundary between *puruṣa* and *prakṛti*. The *niścaya* is the *viveka-khyāti* that Saturn enforces: the boundary is real, the judgment is final. 🏹

Sanskrit Terms

  • niścaya (निश्चय) — certainty, final judgment, settled conclusion; like the final compiler pass — the optimization pass that emits the final binary, no more edits
  • tyāga (त्याग) — renunciation, abandonment, relinquishment; like the garbage collector — the process that releases what is no longer referenced
  • puruṣa-vyāghra (पुरुषव्याघ्र) — tiger among men, the one who hunts truth; like the principal architect — the one who hunts down the root cause
  • tri-vidha (त्रिविध) — threefold, three kinds; like the three build configurations — debug, release, profile — each for a different mode
  • samprakīrtita (सम्प्रकीर्तित) — declared, proclaimed, well-known; like the API specification — the contract that all implementations must satisfy
