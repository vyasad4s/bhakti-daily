paśyādityān vasūn rudrān aśvinau marutas tathā bahūny adṛṣṭa-pūrvāṇi paśyāścaryāṇi bhārata
O best of the Bhāratas, see here the different manifestations of Ādityas, Vasus, Rudras, Aśvinī-kumāras and all the other demigods. Behold the many wonderful things which no one has ever seen or heard of before.
Even though Arjuna was a personal friend of Kṛṣṇa and the most advanced of learned men, it was still not possible for him to know everything about Kṛṣṇa. Here it is stated that humans have neither heard nor known of all these forms and manifestations. Now Kṛṣṇa reveals these wonderful forms. Why BG 11.6 Today?

Today's nakṣatra transition from Revatī to Aśvinī is profoundly significant. Revaṭī, governed by Pūṣan (the nourisher, guide of souls), gives way to Aśvinī, governed by the Aśvinī-kumāras (the divine physicians, bringers of healing and dawn). This mirrors the verse's revelation of various divine manifestations - the Ādityas (solar deities), Vasus (earthly elements), Rudras (storm deities), and specifically the Aśvinī-kumāras.

The tithi transition from Ṣaṣṭhī to Saptami represents the shift from effort to inner journey, complementing the verse's call to "behold" - to move beyond striving to direct perception. The yoga transition from Dhṛti (steadfastness) to Sūla (spear/penance) reflects the determination needed to perceive the divine amidst challenges.

Most significantly, the Moon's transition from Meena (Pisces) to Mesha (Aries) mirrors the journey from universal, boundless awareness to focused, individual action - exactly what the Arjuna experiences when beholding Krishna's manifold divine forms.

Today's Practice

Just as Arjuna was granted divine vision to see the various manifestations of the divine, today's alignment invites us to cultivate our own "divine sight" - the ability to perceive the sacred in all forms and transitions.

  1. During the auspicious Abhijit muhūrta (12:34 PM - 1:30 PM), meditate on the various divine manifestations mentioned in BG 11.6
  2. After 12:24 PM, when Amṛt Siddhi and Sarvārtha Siddhi are active, undertake one concrete action toward your spiritual goal
  3. Notice how different aspects of divinity manifest in ordinary beings and situations throughout your day
  4. Offer your actions as worship, recognizing the divine in all forms

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS II.50: bahya ābhyantara stambha vṛttiḥ deśa kāraṅkya saṅkhyā paridṛṣṭaḥ dīrgha sūkṣmaḥ - "The external, internal, and restrained modulations [of breath] are regulated by place, time, and number, and become slow and subtle."

Just as pranayama regulates the breath to perceive subtler realities, today's celestial alignments regulate our perception to behold the divine manifestations. The breath becomes the bridge between the finite and infinite - just as BG 11.6 reveals the infinite varieties of divine expression within the finite perception of Arjuna.

YS I.36: viśokā vā jyotiṣmatī - "Or [the mind is stabilized] by a luminous, sorrowless [vision]." The vision of the various divine forms in BG 11.6 provides such a luminous, sorrowless focus for the mind.
