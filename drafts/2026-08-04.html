पश्यादित्यान्वसून्‍रुद्रानश्विनौ मरुतस्तथा । बहून्यदृष्टपूर्वाणि पश्याश्चर्याणि भारत ॥ ६ ॥
paśyādityān vasūn rudrān aśvinau marutas tathā bahūny adṛṣṭa-pūrvāṇi paśyāścaryāṇi bhārata
O best of the Bhāratas, see here the different manifestations of Ādityas, Vasus, Rudras, Aśvinī-kumāras and all the other demigods. Behold the many wonderful things which no one has ever seen or heard of before.
This verse is not about exotic divine spectacles - it's a perception upgrade patch. Arjuna isn't being shown rare gods for entertainment; he's getting diagnostic access to the source code of manifestation. The Ādityas (solar principles), Vasus (material substrates), Rudras (transformative forces), and Aśvinī-kumāras (healing duality) aren't separate deities to worship - they're background processes running in universal consciousness. The key phrase is "adṛṣṭa-pūrvāṇi" (not seen before). This isn't about adding new beliefs to your mental model - it's about upgrading your perceptual firmware to see what was always running in the background. Just as a debugger reveals hidden processes in a system, divine vision reveals the ever-present modalities of prakṛti that the conditioned mind filters out as "ordinary." Today's astronomical alignments mirror this perceptual shift: Revatī (Pūṣan, the navigator) yielding to Aśvinī (the divine physicians) represents the transition from guidance to healing - exactly what happens when you stop interpreting experience through conditioned labels and start perceiving underlying patterns. The Moon's transit from Meena (boundary-dissolving Pisces) to Mesha (initiatory Aries) moves you from oceanic unity to focused perception - not losing the wholeness, but gaining the ability to discern its parts.

Why BG 11.6 Today?

The Gandamool nakshatra transition is particularly significant - it's considered inauspicious for beginnings but powerful for completion and release. Today isn't about starting new spiritual practices; it's about releasing the illusion that divinity is separate from the ordinary manifestations already present in your experience.

The Ṣaṣṭhī→Saptami tithi shift (12:33 PM) moves from the effort of striving to the fruits of perception - perfectly aligned with the verse's call to "behold" what's already visible to purified sight. This isn't about achieving something new; it's about recognizing what you've been overlooking.

Most practically, the post-12:24 PM window of Amṛt Siddhi and Sarvārtha Siddhi isn't for passive reception - it's for active implementation. When you perceive the divine in the ordinary (as BG 11.6 describes), what do you DO with that perception? That's where today's auspicious window applies: to act in accordance with what you've seen.

Today's Practice

Forget trying to "see God" in some spectacular form. Today's practice is about debugging your perception:

  1. 10:00 AM - 12:00 PM (Dhṛti yoga window): Before the shift, practice sustained attention on one mundane object (a pen, a leaf, your hand). Not contemplating its divine nature - simply observing its actual characteristics without labeling. Train the mind to see what's there, not what you assume is there.
  2. 12:24 PM - 6:00 PM (Amṛt Siddhi + Sarvārtha Siddhi window): Apply the morning's training. Choose three routine interactions today and instead of reacting from habit, pause to perceive the actual person/situation before you. What qualities are actually manifesting? Respond to what IS, not to your story about it.
  3. Throughout the day: Notice when you catch yourself saying "This is just X" (where X is something dismissive like "just traffic," "just my job," "just a conversation"). That's your filtering system at work. Pause and ask: "What am I NOT seeing in this moment that might be worth perceiving?"
  4. Evening review: Not "Did I see God today?" but "Where did my preconceptions block direct perception today? What appeared when I suspended judgment for just 10 seconds longer than usual?"

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS II.10-11: te pratiprasavaheyaḥ sūkṣmāḥ - "These [kleśas] are to be resolved back into their primal cause; the active ones are to be overcome by meditation." The adṛṣṭa-pūrvāṇi (unseen before) perceptions in BG 11.6 happen precisely when the kleśas (particularly avidyā - mistaken identity) are temporarily suspended through focused attention.

YS I.2: yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ - Yoga is the cessation of mental modifications. The "wondrous things never seen before" appear not through added vision, but through subtracted distortion. When the mind stops interpreting, what was always visible becomes visible.

YS III.4-5: trayam ekatra saṃyamaḥ - The threefold practice of concentration, meditation, and samyama leads to prajñā (illumination). Today's practice isn't about gaining supernatural sight - it's about applying the standard yogic technology of attention to ordinary perception.
