Bhagavad-gītā BG 17.2

त्रिविधा भवति श्रद्धा देहिनां सा स्वभावजा ॥ २ ॥
tri-vidhā bhavati śraddhā
dehināṁ sā svabhāva-jā
The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: According to the modes of nature acquired by the embodied soul, one's faith can be of three kinds – in goodness, in passion or in ignorance.
Ṭhākur repeated: threefold faith. And why three? Because the entire material creation is built on the interaction of sattva, rajas, and tamas — the guṇa‑tri‑kāṇḍa. Every being inherits a unique proportion, and that proportion decides everything: what we crave, how we think, what we reject, what we feed. On Ekadashi the fast sharpens the internal ratio; the light that remains is the proportion that defines us. The question BG 17 answers is not "why does the mode dominate" — that's psychology — but "who is the witness of the mode?" The faith that answers is not a belief-system but a discriminative knowing: the self is not the faith, the self is the field in which faith arises. And since the field is tri‑guṇīya, the faith within it is necessarily tri‑varīya. Prabhupāda's purport is plain: some are in the mode of goodness, some of passion, some of ignorance — and the nature of one's faith matches that mode. The person in sattva has clear, pure faith; the rajas person has fruitive, result‑oriented faith; the tamas person has faith that is misguided, obstructive, or absent. This is not judgment; it is observation. And the observation has a purpose: it tells the sādhaka where to apply the sādhanā. If one's faith is tamas‑bound, no amount of scriptural hearing will elevate; the vehicle is broken. If one's faith is rajas‑bound, hearing may spark curiosity but will not sustain without transformation. If one's faith is sattva‑bound, the path is open. Ekadashi is the perfect day for this diagnosis: the fast naturally reduces rajas and tamas, revealing the sattvic proportion that is already there. The verse is not moralistic; it is descriptive. And the observation, as always, has a purpose: it tells the sādhaka where to apply the sādhanā. 🏹

Verse Selection Rationale

The panchāṅga demanded BG 17.2. Two signals converge. First: Budhwar (Wednesday) + Mercury-energy naturally amplifies intellect and discrimination, and the verse's core subject is that faith is not arbitrary — it is born of the embodied soul's own nature (sva-bhāva-jā). Every being's faith matches their constitutional proportion. A Mercury-day that opens with a personal-preference verse would be a misreading of the sky. Second: Rama Ekadashi, the fast of Lord Rāma, the threshold of transcendence. The verse's theme — faith born of one's own nature — matches perfectly: the fast reduces external dependence and reveals the internal proportion. The panchanga's Indra→Vaidhruthi transition ("the king → the cautionary") mirrors the verse's movement: faith's proportion is revealed, then the caution is required. The moon in Simha (Leo) until 11:04 PM, then Kanya (Virgo) — the royal seat yields to the analytical sign, exactly the shift from ego-identified faith to discriminating faith. No repeat: 17.1 used for 11-06; 17.3 used for 11-05; 17.2 is the Ekadashi fit. Purva Phalguni→Uttara Phalguni activates the "rear legs"/prosperity theme: the legs that walk the path, the stability that supports the fast.

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS I.20 — abhāve svarūpe śuddhiḥ — "In the absence of the false, the true nature reveals itself." The bridge is structural. BG 17.2 names three modes of faith born of the embodied soul; YS I.20 names the absence of the false. The mechanism is the same: when the false identification (faith as arbitrary preference) is removed, the true nature (faith as guṇa‑proportion) reveals itself. And YS II.32's niyama īśvara‑praṇidhāna — surrender to the Supreme — is the natural culmination of BG 17's knowledge: knowing one's guṇa‑proportion is not the end; it is the pivot for surrender. If faith is sattvic, surrender deepens it; if rājasic, it transforms it; if tāmasic, it elevates it. The verse provides the diagnosis; the sūtra provides the method. Together they form the complete sādhana‑circle: know → surrender → transform. 🏹

Sanskrit Terms

  • tri-vidhā (त्रिविधा) — threefold; like a triple‑branch switch — the circuit routes power through one of three paths.
  • śraddhā (श्रद्धा) — faith, trust, confidence; like the authentication token — the credential that grants access to the system's inner functions.
  • dehinām (देहिनām) — of the embodied; like per‑instance settings — the configuration that applies only to this body‑hardware.
  • svabhāva-jā (स्वभावजा) — born of one's own nature; like the default build — the version that ships with the machine, before any customization.
