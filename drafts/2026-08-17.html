Bhagavad Gita Chapter 11, Verse 7

इहैकस्थं जगत्कृत्स्नं पश्याद्य सचराचरम् । मम देहे गुडाकेश यच्च‍ान्यद्‍द्रष्टुमिच्छसि ॥ ७ ॥
ihaika-sthaṁ jagat kṛtsnaṁ
paśyādya sa-carācaram
mama dehe guḍākeśa
yac cānyad draṣṭum icchasi
O Arjuna, whatever you wish to see, behold at once in this body of Mine! This universal form can show you whatever you now desire to see and whatever you may want to see in the future. Everything – moving and nonmoving – is here completely, in one place.
No one can see the entire universe while sitting in one place. Even the most advanced scientist cannot see what is going on in other parts of the universe. But a devotee like Arjuna can see everything that exists in any part of the universe. Kṛṣṇa gives him the power to see anything he wants to see, past, present and future. Thus by the mercy of Kṛṣṇa, Arjuna is able to see everything.

Cosmic Theme: Discrimination (Viveka)

Today's panchanga highlights the power of discrimination. The Moon in Kanyā (Virgo) brings analytical precision, while Ṣaṣṭhī tithi (associated with Kartikeya, the divine commander) sharpens our ability to discern truth from illusion. Citrā nakṣatra, ruled by Vishvakarma (the divine architect), reveals the intricate design underlying all manifestation.

BG 11.7 perfectly captures this energy: Kṛṣṇa offers Arjuna the divine vision to see both the moving (sa-carā) and non-moving (acaram) aspects of existence simultaneously. This is viveka in action — the ability to discriminate between the eternal (puruṣa) and the temporary (prakṛti), to perceive the unchanging consciousness amidst all change.

As the Moon transitions from Kanyā to Tulā at 6:49 AM, we move from discriminative analysis to balanced relationship — mirroring how true viveka leads not to cold detachment, but to harmonious engagement with the world as Krishna's manifestation.

Yoga Sutra Connection

BG 11.7's promise to show "everything – moving and nonmoving – here completely, in one place" directly illustrates Yoga Sutra 2.15:

parināma tāpa saṁskāra duḥkaiḥ guṇa vṛtti virodhāt ca duḥkham eva sarvam vivekinah
"To the discriminative one, everything is indeed sorrow because of its consequences: the anxiety and fear over losses; the deep impressions left by experiences; and the opposition of the qualities."

This sutra teaches that vivekinah (the discriminative one) sees beyond surface pleasure to recognize the inherent sorrow in all conditioned existence — exactly what Arjuna begins to perceive in the universal form. The ability to see both moving and nonmoving in one place is the mark of the viveki who understands that puruṣa (consciousness) remains unchanged while prakṛti (nature) dances in endless forms.

Further, this vision connects to Yoga Sutra 1.2 (yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ — yoga is the cessation of mental modifications). When Arjuna sees the totality of existence in Krishna's body, his mental fluctuations cease — he experiences yoga as direct perception of the unified field.

Today's Practice

Morning (after 6:49 AM Moon in Tulā): Practice discrimination by observing one object — see its form (moving/changing) while simultaneously recognizing the space/consciousness (nonmoving/unchanging) in which it appears. This is the vision Krishna offers in BG 11.7.

During Rāhu Kāla (7:55-9:36 AM): When doubt or illusion arises, remember that Krishna gives divine eyes to see through maya's deception. The very obstacle (rāhu) becomes the opportunity to develop clearer vision.

Evening (Moon in Tulā): Reflect on relationships — see both the individual personalities (moving) and the underlying bond of consciousness (nonmoving) that connects all beings. This is samarasya (equal vision) born of viveka.
