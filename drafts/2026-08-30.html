अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्र: करुण एव च । निर्ममो निरहङ्कार: समदु:खसुख: क्षमी ॥ १३ ॥ सन्तुष्ट: सततं योगी यतात्मा दृढनिश्चय: । मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्यो मद्भक्त: स मे प्रिय: ॥ १४ ॥
adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṁ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamīsantuṣṭaḥ satataṁ yogī yatātmā dṛḍha-niścayaḥ mayy arpita-mano-buddhir yo mad-bhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ
One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities, who does not think himself a proprietor and is free from false ego, who is equal in both happiness and distress, who is tolerant, always satisfied, self-controlled, and engaged in devotional service with determination, his mind and intelligence fixed on Me – such a devotee of Mine is very dear to Me.

Special Observance: Kajari Teej

Location: Philadelphia, PA (loc=5128581)

Today's celestial alignment creates a potent nexus of devotional energy. Kajari Teej, observed during Krishna Paksha Tritiya, celebrates the divine love between Shiva and Parvati – the ultimate exemplar of bhakti where the ascetic deity embraces householder devotion. The Moon's transit through compassionate Pisces amplifies spiritual sensitivity and empathic connection, while the Sun in regal Leo ignites the heart's courage to love fiercely.

The nakṣatra shift from Uttara Bhadrapada (symbolized by the serpent of depths, representing stability and foundational strength) to Revati (nourished by Pushan, the celestial guardian who ensures safe journeys and completion) mirrors the devotee's path: first establishing inner stability through practice, then receiving divine grace that completes and perfects the bhakti journey. Similarly, the yoga transition from Soola (piercing challenges that test resolve) to Ganda (knots requiring patient untangling) reflects how devotion transforms obstacles into opportunities for deeper surrender.

This cosmic configuration invites us to embody the thirteen qualities of a true devotee described in verses 13-14: freedom from envy, universal friendship, compassion, egolessness, equanimity in dualities, forgiveness, contentment, continual practice, self-mastery, steadfast determination, and – most crucially – fixing mind and intelligence upon Krishna while engaging all actions as His devotee. Such a consciousness doesn't merely approach the Divine; it becomes His dearest possession.

Today's Practice

Today, consciously cultivate one quality of the pure devotee:

  • Maitra (Universal Friendship): See Krishna in everyone – the cashier, the coworker, the stranger. Greet each being with the awareness: "This is also a beloved of Hari."
  • Karunā (Compassion): When frustration arises, pause and ask: "What pain might this person be carrying that I cannot see?" Respond with tenderness rather than reaction.
  • Sama-duḥkha-sukha (Equanimity): Notice when pleasure or pain pulls you toward extremes. Whisper: "This too is Krishna's grace," and return to balanced awareness.

Perform these not as moral obligations but as spontaneous expressions of your bhakti – letting Krishna's love flow through you unimpeded.

Yoga Sutra Bridge

BG 12.13-14's description of the pure devotee finds its classical Yoga counterpart in Sutra 1.33:

मैत्री करुणा muditopekṣāṇां दुःखpunyāPUṇYOViṣayāṁ bhāvanaṁścittaprasādanaṁ ..
maitrī karuṇā muditā upekṣāṇāṁ duḥkhā punyāpuṇyaviṣayāṁ bhāvanam cittaprasādanaṁ

Translation: "The mind becomes purified by cultivating feelings of friendliness (maitrī) toward the happy, compassion (karuṇā) toward the miserable, joy (muditā) toward the virtuous, and indifference (upekṣā) toward the non-virtuous."

This sutra presents the same four-fold attitude (brahmavihāras) that BG 12.13-14 embodies in the devotee's natural expression: maitra (friendship), karuṇa (compassion), plus the implicit mudita (joy in others' virtue) and upekṣā (equanimity toward negativity). Where the Bhagavad Gita presents these as spontaneous qualities of one whose mind is fixed on Krishna, the Yoga Sutras offer them as conscious practices to purify the citta (mind-consciousness).

In computational terms: BG 12.13-14 describes the output of a system running on Krishna-consciousness firmware, while Yoga Sutra 1.33 provides the user interface for installing and configuring that firmware through deliberate practice.

Sanskrit Terms

Adveṣṭā (अद्वेष्टा)

Literal: Non-envious, free from malice

Hacker Metaphor: Like a rootkit that operates without triggering security alerts – pure devotion runs stealthily in the background of consciousness, elevating all processes without raising the "envy" process flag in the mind's task manager.

Karunā (करुणा)

Literal: Compassion, mercy

Hacker Metaphor: The ultimate packet sniffer with zero-latency response – it detects suffering in the data stream of existence and immediately responds with healing packets of kindness, operating at kernel level of compassion.

Samaduḥkhasukha (समदुःखसुख)

Literal: Equal in happiness and distress

Hacker Metaphor: A load balancer that distributes both pleasure and pain evenly across all servers of experience – no single node gets overloaded with either extreme, maintaining optimal system performance regardless of incoming traffic patterns.

Yatātmā (यतात्मा)

Literal: Self-controlled, self-mastered

Hacker Metaphor: The BIOS/firmware level of consciousness – before any OS (ego) or applications (desires) load, the self-mastered one operates at the foundational level where only essential processes run, immune to user-level crashes or malware.

Mayy arpita-mano-buddhir (मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर)

Literal: Mind and intelligence fixed on Me

Hacker Metaphor: Setting the CPU affinity mask to exclusively run on Krishna-core – all computational threads (thoughts, decisions, actions) are bound to process on the Divine core, preventing context-switching to materialistic processes that would cause system instability.
