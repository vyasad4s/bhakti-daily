Bhagavad-gītā BG 15.12

यदादित्यगतं तेजो जगद्भासयतेऽखिलम् । यच्चन्द्रमसि यच्चाग्नौ तत्तेजो विद्धि मामकम् ॥ १२ ॥
yad āditya-gataṁ tejo jagad bhāsayate ’khilam yac candramasi yac cāgnau tat tejo viddhi māmakam
The splendor of the sun, which dissipates the darkness of this whole world, comes from Me. And the splendor of the moon and the splendor of fire are also from Me.
This verse reveals that the divine splendor we perceive in the sun, moon, and fire originates from the Supreme Lord Himself. The sun's radiance that illuminates the world, the moon's gentle glow, and fire's transforming power—all are expansions of Krishna's energy. This is not merely a poetic metaphor but a tangible truth: the very light that sustains life and enables perception is an expression of the Divine. From Sāṅkhya perspective, this demonstrates how the puruṣa (consciousness) pervades and energizes prakṛti (material nature). The Lord doesn't merely observe creation from afar; He enters into it (āviśya), sustains it (dhārayāmi), and becomes its very essence (somo bhūtvā). Similarly, the splendor of celestial bodies is not an inherent property but a reflection of the divine consciousness that illuminates them. The nakṣatra Magha (until 12:10 PM), ruled by the Pitṛs (ancestors), emphasizes honoring our spiritual lineage and the divine source of our sustenance. After 12:10 PM, Purva Phalguni nakṣatra begins, symbolizing creativity and benevolent flow—ideal for devotional activities. Subha yoga (until 5:21 PM) represents virtue and auspiciousness, supporting the day's spiritual focus. The Moon's placement in Simha (Leo) after noon adds a regal, illuminating quality to the day's energies, reminding us that the same divine light that sustains the planets also shines within our hearts. The special observance of Pradosh Vrat (worship of Shiva) further highlights the auspiciousness of this day for spiritual practices, especially in the evening when the moon is visible. The verse’s teaching—that the splendor of the sun, moon, and fire comes from the Lord—finds direct resonance in today’s panchāṅga. By recognizing the divine source in these luminous objects, we cultivate discernment (viveka) and devotion (bhakti), turning ordinary perception into remembrance of the Supreme Lord who is the essence of all splendor.

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS I.36: viśokā vā jyotiṣmati — "Or [the mind is stabilized] by a luminous, sorrowless state." BG 15.12 describes how the splendor of the sun, moon, and fire originates from the Lord. YS I.36 complements this by showing that focusing on luminous objects (jyotiṣmati) can stabilize the mind and lead to a sorrowless state (viśokā). Both texts affirm that recognizing the divine source in luminous phenomena—whether celestial bodies or inner light—leads to mental tranquility and spiritual clarity. The verse and sutra together map the journey: from perceiving the Lord's splendor in the external world to cultivating an inner state of luminous peace through devotion and discernment.

Sanskrit Terms

  • āditya-gataṁ (आदित्यगतम्) — in the sunshine; like solar-powered code — drawing energy directly from the divine source.
  • tejo (तेजः) — splendor; like the brightness of a well-tuned system — a sign of optimal operation and divine radiance.
  • candramasi (चन्द्रमसि) — in the moon; like a subsystem reflecting core light — a vital interface that translates divine consciousness into gentle illumination.
  • agnau (अग्नौ) — in fire; like the CPU's heat output — a sign of activity and transformation that originates from the Divine.
  • viddhi (विद्धि) — understand; like a debugger's insight into the code — recognizing the divine origin in all phenomena.
  • māmakam (मामकम्) — from Me; like the origin point of a process — the fundamental source that gives meaning and energy to all operations.
