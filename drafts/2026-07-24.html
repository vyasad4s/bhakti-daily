Bhagavad Gita Chapter 10, Verse 20

अहमात्मा गुडाकेश सर्वभूताशयस्थित: । अहमादिश्च मध्यं च भूतानामन्त एव च ॥ २० ॥

 

aham ātmā guḍākeśa
sarva-bhūtāśaya-sthitaḥ
aham ādiś ca madhyaṁ ca
bhūtānām anta eva ca

 

I am the Supersoul, O Arjuna, seated in the hearts of all living entities. I am the beginning, the middle and the end of all beings.

 

Today is Shayani Ekadashi — the day Lord Vishnu enters Yoga Nidra (divine sleep) on the cosmic ocean, marking the beginning of Chaturmasya (four months of divine rest). The purport to BG 10.20 directly references this: "Yaḥ kāraṇārṇava-jale bhajati sma yoga-nidrām: the Supreme Lord, Kṛṣṇa, the cause of all causes, lies down in the cosmic ocean as Mahā-viṣṇu." Arjuna is addressed as Guḍākeśa — "one who has conquered the darkness of sleep." This creates a profound reciprocal dynamic: while Vishnu sleeps in Yoga Nidra to sustain the universe, the devotee (symbolized by Arjuna) conquers ordinary sleep through devotional vigilance. On Ekadashi, we fast and stay awake not to mimic the Lord's sleep, but to honor His sustaining presence by remaining conscious of Him as the Supersoul within. Today's panchanga amplifies this: - **Tithi:** Śukla Ekādaśī (Shayani Ekadashi) — the holiest day for spiritual practice, fasting, and heightened consciousness. Ekadashi is intrinsically linked to sense control (indriya-saṁyama) — withdrawing the senses from external objects to turn inward toward the Supersoul dwelling in the heart. - **Nakṣatra:** Anurādhā (until 7:06 PM), then Jyeṣṭhā — Anurādhā means "following Rādhā" or "following the divine friend," ruled by Mitra (solar deity of friendship, contracts, divine alliance). This perfectly matches BG 10.20's revelation of the Lord as the intimate friend (Supersoul) seated in the heart of every being. - **Yoga:** Śukla (auspicious, pure) until 10:41 AM, then Brahma (creator/preserver energy) — Brahma yoga carries the energy of creation and preservation, reflecting Vishnu's role as maintainer even while in Yoga Nidra. - **Special:** Ekadashi's presiding deity is Vishnu Himself — the very Lord who rests in Yoga Nidra yet remains the indwelling Supersoul (Paramātmā) in all hearts. The sāṅkhya insight is precise: Prakṛti (material nature, including the body and its need for sleep) rests, but puruṣa (consciousness, the Supersoul) remains awake as the witness. As the Purport states: "The Supreme Personality of Godhead is existing as the Supersoul in all manifested universes." Even in Yoga Nidra, Vishnu maintains the universe through His plenary expansion as the Supersoul — the very consciousness that illuminates every heart. This is the ultimate non-duality of devotion: the Lord who sleeps to sustain creation is the same Lord who awakens as consciousness in every devotee's heart. Ekadashi practice — fasting, staying awake, remembering Vishnu — is not mortification but alignment: we stabilize the body (prakṛti) to make the puruṣa (Supersoul) more palpable as our true identity. For the bhakti-yogī, this is lived reciprocity: we honor the Lord's sustaining rest by remembering Him as the Supersoul within; He reciprocates by revealing Himself more clearly as the inner guide. The practice becomes constant remembrance: wherever there is heartbeat and breath, there is Kṛṣṇa as the Supersoul giving permission to be.

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Ekadashi Supersoul Remembrance: Before each meal (or before breaking fast), place your hand on your heart and silently offer: "O Supersoul, dweller in my heart, thank You for sustaining this body and breath. Help me remember You as my true identity." Let the physical sensation of heartbeat become a direct perception of the Divine as your inner witness.

Anurādhā Alignment: Since the nakṣatra is Anurādhā (following the divine friend) until 7:06 PM, before each action, ask: "Is this action in accordance with following the divine friend within?" Let every deed become an offering to the Supersoul who guides from within.

Shayani Ekadashi Vigil: If observing Ekadashi fast, spend the usual sleep hours in japa, kirtan, or reading Bhagavad-gītā — transforming the body's rest period into heightened consciousness of the Lord who never sleeps as the Supersoul.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

aham (pronoun) — I; the Supreme Personality of Godhead

ātmā (noun) — the Supersoul; the eternal consciousness dwelling in all hearts

guḍākeśa (vocative) — O conqueror of sleep; Arjuna's epithet indicating victory over tamas

sarva-bhūtāśaya-sthitaḥ (compound) — seated in the hearts of all beings; the indwelling witness

ādiś (noun) — the beginning; the origin or source

madhyaṁ (noun) — the middle; the sustaining principle

antaḥ (noun) — the end; the final goal or dissolution

eva (adverb) — certainly; without doubt

ca (particle) — and; also; inclusive connector

bhūtānām (genitive plural) — of all beings; of all living entities
