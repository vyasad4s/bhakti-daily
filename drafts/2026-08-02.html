Bhagavad Gita Chapter 11, Verse 55

मत्कर्मकृत्‍त्‍म-paramo mad-bhaktaḥ saṅga-varjitaḥ । निरवैरः सर्वभूतेषु यः स मामेति पाण्डव ॥ ५५ ॥५५॥

 

mat-karma-kṛt mat-paramo mad-bhaktaḥ saṅga-varjitaḥ
nirvairaḥ sarva-bhūteṣu yaḥ sa mām eti pāṇḍava

 

One who engages in My pure devotional service, free from the contaminations of previous activities and who is devoted to Me, who is devoid of envy towards all living entities, who is non-envious and who is friendly to all living entities comes to Me.

 

Today is Sunday — Ravivāra, ruled by Surya (Sun), the illuminator of the path, the source of clarity that burns away illusion. The tithi is Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa Caturthī, moving into Pañcamī at 1:45 PM — a juncture of obstacle-removal and stabilization. The nakṣatra this morning is Pūrva Bhādrapadā (until 12:07 PM), presided over by Aja Ekapāda, whose symbol is the funeral pyre — the fire of purification. From 12:07 PM, the nakṣatra shifts to Uttara Bhādrapadā, the “latter foot of the fortunate one,” whose deity is Ahir Budhnya, the serpent of the depths, governing stability, deep waters, and the unshakable foundation. The yoga transitions from Atiganda (excessive conflict/struggle) to Sukarman (well-doing, auspicious action) at 12:59 PM — from friction into aligned action. The Moon is in Meena (Pisces), the mutable-water sign of dissolution, compassion, and the return to the source, rising at 10:02 PM.

 

BG 11.55 is the culminating statement of Chapter 11 — the Viśvarūpa Darśana Yoga. After revealing His universal form with thousands of mouths, eyes, and forms of divine majesty, Kṛṣṇa now discloses the actual method to approach Him: not through awe. This Divine Person. The verse begins with mat-karma-kṛt — “one who engages in My activities” — not karma as bondage, but karma as bhakti, every action offered as service. Then mat-paramaḥ — “whose supreme goal is Me” — the single-pointed devotion that makes all other pursuits secondary. Then mad-bhaktaḥ — “My devotee” — one whose heart is fixed on Kṛṣṇa as the object of love. Then saṅga-varjitaḥ — “free from association” — not isolation, but freedom from contaminating motives: desire for sense gratification, speculation, or fruitive results. Finally, nirvairaḥ sarva-bhūteṣu — “devoid of envy towards all living beings” — the natural fruit of seeing Kṛṣṇa everywhere, eliminating jealousy and competition. This is not a description of a perfected soul; it is the process. Each qualifier is a step on the path: engage in His service → make Him your highest goal → cultivate bhakti → purify your associations → see all beings as His parts. The verse ends with sa mām eti pāṇḍava — “such a person comes to Me.” Not merges, not becomes — comes. The personal relationship remains.

 

Uttara Bhādrapadā’s Ahir Budhnya (serpent of the depths) provides the perfect backdrop for this verse. The serpent does not flash; it holds deep, steady, unshakable. It represents the stability that comes when devotion matures beyond flashes of emotion into a fixed orientation. The Caturthī→Pañcamī tithi transition mirrors the verse’s arc: Caturthī removes obstacles (saṅkaṣṭhī — the remover of impediments), Pañcamī stabilizes the gain. The Moon in Meena (Pisces) adds the flavor of dissolution into the unified whole — the vision that all beings are parts of Kṛṣṇa, making nirvairaḥ (freedom from envy) not an effort but a natural perception. Today’s Ravivāra (Sunday) illuminates the path: Surya’s light does not create the vision; it reveals what was always there. BG 11.55 is not a theoretical ideal; it is the operable method for the embodied soul seeking to approach the Supreme Person in a way that is actual, realizable, and non-speculative.

 

The Sāṅkhya lens clarifies why this works. In the dualistic framework of puruṣa/prakṛti, bhakti is not a modification of prakṛti — it is the engagement of puruṣa (the conscious principle) with its source. The verse’s qualifiers are not ethical injunctions; they are descriptions of consciousness aligned with puruṣa. Mat-karma-kṛt: action performed as puruṣa, not as prakṛti’s compulsions. Mat-paramaḥ: buddhi (intellect) fixed on puruṣa as the highest reference. Mad-bhaktaḥ: ahaṅkāra (ego-sense) redirected from its petty identifiers to its true identity as a part of puruṣa. Saṅga-varjitaḥ: citta (mind-stuff) free from prakṛti’s distracting vṛttis (modifications). Nirvairaḥ sarva-bhūteṣu: the vision of puruṣa in all bodies eliminates prakṛti’s inherent competition (rāga-dveṣa). This is not morality imposed from outside; it is the spontaneous functioning of consciousness when it is no longer misidentified. The verse is not a checklist; it is a description of the soul’s natural trajectory when it turns toward its source. The obstacles removed by Caturthī are precisely the prakṛti-based identifications that block the puruṣa’s direct approach.

 

YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION

YS 1.23: Īśvara praṇidhānād vā — “Or, [this previously mentioned state is attainable] from devotion to the Lord.” Patanjali explicitly states that the goal of yoga (citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ) can be attained through īśvara-praṇidhāna — devotion to God. BG 11.55 is the Gītā’s elaboration of this very sutra: mat-karma-kṛt mat-paramo mad-bhaktaḥ saṅga-varjitaḥ nirvairaḥ sarva-bhūteṣu is the detailed description of what īśvara-praṇidhāna looks like in practice. It is not vague “love”; it is specific, actionable, and measurable: engage in His service, make Him your goal, cultivate bhakti, purify associations, see all beings as His parts.

YS 2.1: tapas svādhyāya īśvara-praṇidhānā kriyā-yogaḥ — The threefold practice of Kriya Yoga includes īśvara-praṇidhāna as its third component. BG 11.55 shows the fruition: when īśvara-praṇidhāna is mature, it naturally produces the other two — tapas (discipline in service) and svādhyāya (self-study through His teachings). The verse’s mat-karma-kṛt is tapas in action; its mat-paramaḥ mad-bhaktaḥ implies svādhyāya through constant remembrance.

YS I.12: abhyāsa vairāgyābhyāṁ tannirodhaḥ — citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ is attained through abhyāsa (consistent practice) and vairāgya (dispassion). BG 11.55’s saṅga-varjitaḥ nirvairaḥ sarva-bhūteṣu is vairāgya in action: freedom from association with what distracts, and freedom from envy — the two faces of detachment (from the undesired and from the desired when seen as binding).

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Īśvara-praṇidhāna Inventory (Uttara Bhādrapadā Morning): Until 12:07 PM, Uttara Bhādrapadā is active — the stability nakshatra. Before your japa, review your activities from the last 24 hours: How many were offered as mat-karma-kṛt? How many were done for personal gain, sense gratification, or mere habit? Not to condemn, but to see. The serpent’s steadiness reveals where your energy is leaking. Then, for the next hour, consciously offer one routine activity as service — washing dishes, answering emails, driving — with the sankalpa: “This is for You.” Let the stability of Ahir Budhnya anchor your intention.

Saṅga-varjitaḥ Adjustment (Caturthī-Pañcamī Juncture): At 1:45 PM, the tithi shifts from Caturthī to Pañcamī — obstacle-removal to stabilization. Use this juncture to identify one association (saṅga) that subtly pulls your consciousness away from mat-paramaḥ — not necessarily “bad,” but not serving your highest goal. It could be a mental habit, a relationship dynamic, or a media pattern. From 1:45 PM onward, reduce engagement with that association by 25%. Not by force, but by recognizing: “This does not serve my goal of making Kṛṣṇa my paramaḥ.” Let the Pañcamī tithi stabilize the gain.

Nirvairaḥ Cultivation (Meena Moonrise): Tonight, the Moon rises in Meena (Pisces) at 10:02 PM — the sign of unity, compassion, and the dissolution of boundaries. During moonrise, practice seeing the divine in three beings you typically overlook or feel neutral toward: the cashier, the stranger on the street, a coworker you don’t interact with much. Silently acknowledge: “This is a part of Kṛṣṇa.” Not as imagination, but as recognition: if Kṛṣṇa is sarva-bhūteṣu (in all beings), then the vision is already there — you are merely uncovering it. Let the Meena Moon’s dissolving quality reveal the unity that envy obscures.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

mat-karma-kṛt (compound) — “one who engages in My activities”; mat (My) + karma (activities, work) + kṛt (doer/engager). Not karma as bondage, but every action offered as service — cooking, cleaning, working, speaking — done with the consciousness: “This is for You.”

mat-paramaḥ (compound) — “whose supreme goal is Me”; mat (My) + paramaḥ (highest, supreme goal). The intellect’s highest reference point is Kṛṣṇa; all other pursuits are secondary. Not abandonment of duties, but reorientation of purpose.

mad-bhaktaḥ (compound) — “My devotee”; mat (My) + bhaktaḥ (devotee, one who possesses bhakti). Bhakti is not emotion; it is the sustained orientation of the heart toward the Supreme Person as the object of love and service.

saṅga-varjitaḥ (compound) — “free from association”; saṅga (association, connection) + varjitaḥ (given up, avoided). Not isolation, but freedom from contaminating motives: association with sense gratification, speculative philosophy, or fruitive desires that distract from bhakti.

nirvairaḥ (adjective) — “devoid of envy, free from hostility”; nir (without) + vairaḥ (envy, hostility, rivalry). The natural fruit of seeing Kṛṣṇa everywhere: no jealousy toward others’ blessings, no competition for limited resources, no ill-will — because all beings are seen as parts of the same whole.

uttara bhadrapada (noun, nakṣatra) — “the latter good foot.” Ruled by Ahir Budhnya, the serpent of the depths. Governs stability, deep waters, patience, and the unshakable foundation. The foot that remains steady while the world moves.

ahir budhnya (proper noun, deity) — “the serpent of the deep.” The deity of Uttara Bhādrapadā nakṣatra, representing the primal, stabilizing force in the cosmic waters — not flashy, but deep, steady, and unshakable.

saṅkaṣṭhī (adjective) — “obstacle-removing”; from saṅkaṣṭa (obstacle, impediment) + hī (remover). Caturthī (the fourth tithi) is traditionally the day for removing obstacles through the grace of Gaṇeśa. In the context of bhakti, the obstacle is the false identification that blocks the soul’s direct approach to Kṛṣṇa.

pañcamī (noun, tithi) — “the fifth.” The tithi following Caturthī, representing stabilization, consolidation, and the grounding of what was removed or revealed on the fourth.

meena (noun, rāśi) — “Pisces.” The mutable-water sign of dissolution, compassion, and the return to the source. Governs the vision of unity, the breaking down of barriers between self and other.

ravivāra (noun, vāra) — “Sunday.” Ruled by Surya (Sun), the illuminator, the source of clarity and vitality. Not the creator of vision, but the revealer of what is already there.
