Bhagavad-gītā 14.2

इदं ज्ञानमुपाश्रित्य मम साधर्म्यमागता: । सर्गेऽपि नोपजायन्ते प्रलये न व्यथन्ति च ॥ २ ॥
idaṁ jñānam upāśritya
mama sādharmyam āgatāḥ
sarge ’pi nopajāyante
pralaye na vyathanti ca
By becoming fixed in this knowledge, one can attain to the transcendental nature like My own. Thus established, one is not born at the time of creation or disturbed at the time of dissolution.
After acquiring perfect transcendental knowledge, one acquires qualitative equality with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, becoming free from the repetition of birth and death. One does not, however, lose his identity as an individual soul. It is understood from Vedic literature that the liberated souls who have reached the transcendental planets of the spiritual sky always look to the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord, being engaged in His transcendental loving service. So, even after liberation, the devotees do not lose their individual identities.

Verse Selection Rationale

Today’s pañcāṅga highlights Dhruva yoga — the fixed, steady, immovable conjunction. This aligns perfectly with BG 14.2, where taking shelter of supreme knowledge establishes one in the transcendental nature of the Supreme, untouched by the cycles of creation (sarga) and dissolution (pralaya). The Sunday (Ravivāra) emphasizes the solar principle, the ātman, while the Revati nakṣatra (nourisher Pushan) suggests the sustaining, protective quality of such knowledge. The Sun in Virgo points to discernment and purity, complementing the sattvic quality of knowledge that frees from sinful reactions.

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS 1.3: tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe ‘vasthānam — “When that is accomplished, the seer abides in its own true nature.”

Just as BG 14.2 describes attaining the transcendental nature by being fixed in knowledge, YS 1.3 describes the state where the mind’s modifications cease and the puruṣa rests in its own form. Both point to the realization of the unchanging self beyond the fluctuations of prakṛti.

Sanskrit Terms

  • jñānam (ज्ञानम्) — knowledge, transcendental insight; like root access to the source code of reality.
  • upāśritya (उपाश्रित्य) — taking shelter, relying upon; akin to mounting a secure volume for protection.
  • sādharmyam (साधर्म्यम्) — same nature, similarity; comparable to inheritance of divine qualities.
  • sarge (सर्गे) — creation, manifestation; like booting up the cosmic system.
  • pralaye (प्रलये) — dissolution, annihilation; akin to graceful shutdown or power-off.
  • vyathanti (व्यथन्ति) — disturbed, agitated; similar to segmentation fault or kernel panic.

Morning Application

Meditate on the idea of being “fixed in knowledge” (jñānam upāśritya). Contemplate how the unchanging reality behind all change is accessed through sustained remembrance of the Supreme. Let this awareness stabilize your mind amidst the day’s activities, like a system running on a rock-solid kernel.
