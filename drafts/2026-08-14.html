Bhagavad-gītā 11.25

daṁṣṭrā-karālāni ca te mukhāni
dṛṣṭvaiva kālānala-sannibhāni
diśo na jāne na labhe ca śarma
prasīda deveśa jagan-nivāsa

daṁṣṭrā-karālāni ca te mukhāni dṛṣṭvaiva kālānala-sannibhāni diśo na jāne na labhe ca śarma prasīda deveśa jagan-nivāsa

O Lord of lords, O refuge of the worlds, please be gracious to me. I cannot keep my balance seeing thus Your blazing deathlike faces and awful teeth. In all directions I am bewildered.

Cosmic Context — Panchāṅga Analysis (August 14, 2026, Philadelphia)

Tithi: Śukla Pakṣa Tṛtīyā (transitions from Dwitiya at 9:17 AM). Tṛtīyā — the third lunar day, consolidating what Pratipada initiated and Dwitiya energized.

Nakṣatra: Pūrva Phālgunī → Uttara Phālguni (6:12 PM). Pūrva Phālgunī, ruled by Bhaga, governs pleasure and creative abundance. The transition to Uttara Phālguni, ruled by Aryaman, shifts the energy into nobility, generosity, and enduring bonds — from enjoyment refined into principle.

Yoga: Śiva → Siddha (9:38 PM). Śiva — dissolution, the breaking open of rigid structures. Not destruction for its own sake but the precise removal of what has hardened beyond usefulness. Siddha follows — the accomplished state that becomes possible only after dissolution makes space.

Vara: Śukravāra (Friday). The day of devotion, aesthetic refinement, and softness of heart. Friday’s energy makes dissolution bearable — even sacred — by inviting the soul to respond with prayer rather than panic.

Special Configuration: Friday + Śiva Yoga = the Lord’s fierce form arriving not to punish but to dissolve false security. The Pūrva → Uttara Phālgunī arc mirrors exactly what the verse does: composure breaks, and from the wreckage arises pure surrender — "prasīda."

Verse Selection Rationale

Three cosmic signals converge today on one teaching: Śiva Yoga is dissolution; Pūrva → Uttara Phālguni is pleasure yielding to nobility; Friday is devotional surrender. Together they map perfectly onto BG 11.25 — the verse where Arjuna’s composure fractures before the universal form and his only remaining resource is prayer.

Yoga Sūtra Connection: YS 1.23 — īśvara-praṇidhānād vā

YS 1.23 states: īśvara-praṇidhānād vā — "Or through devotion to the Lord." Patanjali offers īśvara-praṇidhāna as an alternative path: when the rigors of self-effort fail, when the citta remains turbulent, the yogi may turn to complete surrender to Īśvara. This is not Plan B — it is the teaching the entire aṣṭāṅga was building toward.

Arjuna’s cry in BG 11.25 is the living expression of this sutra. After a lifetime of discipline — as a warrior, as Kṛṣṇa’s student, as a practitioner of dharma — faced with the cosmic form, his first response is not philosophical argument. It is vulnerability: "dhṛtiṁ na vindāmi — I cannot maintain steadiness." He admits defeat at the level of his own resources and asks for the one thing that cannot be earned: grace.

YS 1.29 clarifies the fruit of such surrender: from devotion to Īśvara comes inner realization and freedom from all antarāyas — the disturbances that fragment the mind. Arjuna’s cit is unmistakably vikṣipta (scattered) in this moment. YS 1.30 names the antarāya of anavasthitatva — the inability to maintain any state of concentration — which is exactly what "diśo na jāne" describes: not knowing in which direction to orient.

The parallel is demanding. When your practice has been faithful and the mind still runs wild, when meditation brings only agitation — recognise this as your kālānala moment. It is not a failure of practice. It is the practice leading you to the threshold where surrender is the only remaining genuine response. Īśvara-praṇidhāna is not weakness; it is the highest tapas.

Practical Application for the Day

The cosmic pattern today asks what happens when what you thought was solid begins to dissolve. Śiva Yoga is sometimes dramatic — sometimes it is quieter: the slow erosion of a relationship you relied on, the collapse of a project you invested years in, the recognition that your identity was built on conditions that no longer hold. The natural response is grasping. BG 11.25 offers a different response: stop. Admit the dissolution. Cry out for grace. That is not defeat; it is yoga in its most distilled form.

Friday’s devotional softness makes this possible. Śukravāra does not demand composure. It demands honesty. Arjuna’s admission — "I cannot keep my balance" — is more spiritually advanced than any philosophical argument he could have offered. Let it be yours today if that is your experience.

The Pūrva → Uttara Phālguni teaching for dissolution: if a phase of pleasure or success is fading, do not fight the transition. Bhaga’s enjoyment was real. Aryaman’s nobility is more real still. Ask what your current dissolution is refining you toward.

Sanskrit Terms

kālānala (n.) — "the fire of time / death"; the impartial process by which time consumes all forms and identities. Not random destruction but the operating system of the material world — every constructed thing is subject to it. Recognizing this is not morbid; it is the beginning of realism.

śarma (n.) — "grace, divine shelter"; what Arjuna cannot obtain in this moment — not the general happiness of worldly success but the specific refuge that only surrender to the Lord provides. The term distinguishes divine grace from everything else a practitioner might reach for.

prasīda (v. imperative) — "be gracious, show favor"; the central devotional cry of the verse. Not "give me what I want" but "restore what I have lost through my own confusion." It is the recognised helplessness that precedes every genuine spiritual breakthrough.

jagan-nivāsa (n.) — "refuge of the worlds"; the Lord as the final dwelling place of all existence. All temporary refuges — army, skills, status, relationships — are part of the jagat, the moving world that passes. Kṛṣṇa alone is its nivāsa, its abiding home.

īśvara-praṇidhāna (YS 1.23) — "devotion to the Lord"; the complete surrender of will and effort to Īśvara. Not passive resignation but an active, cultivated orientation that develops when the mind is steady so it is available when the mind is not.

Summary

BG 11.25 is surrender at its most honest. Arjuna has reached the edge of his resources. The universal form has shattered his composure. Every arrow he can loose, every strategy he has rehearsed, every certainty he has held — all rendered irrelevant by kālānala faces that reduce everything to ash. And from that emptiness, one cry: "prasīda."

YS 1.23 teaches that īśvara-praṇidhāna is a complete path. The entire Yoga Sūtra system was building to this moment — not because discipline is insufficient, but because its ultimate fruit is the recognition that the Lord\'s grace cannot be manufactured. The discipline prepares the ground; the surrender is the harvest.

Today\'s practice: when you feel the ground dissolving — when something you relied on collapses, when your practice feels like it has failed you — do not grasp. Cry out. Build the muscle of prasīda now, when you are steady, so it is available when Śiva Yoga arrives.
