Bhagavad-gītā BG 18.3

त्याज्यं दोषवदित्येके कर्म प्राहुर्मनीषिण: ॥ ३ ॥ यज्ञदानतप:कर्म न त्याज्यमिति चापरे ॥ ३ ॥
tyājyaṁ doṣa-vad ity eke
karma prāhur manīṣiṇaḥ
yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma
na tyājyam iti cāpare
Some learned men declare that all kinds of fruitive activities should be given up as faulty, yet other sages maintain that acts of sacrifice, charity and penance should never be abandoned.
Tuesday is Mars' day — Maṅgala, the planet of action, the blade that cuts hesitation. And here Kṛṣṇa presents the central dialectic of Chapter 18: two camps, two errors, one resolution. The first camp (*eke*) says *tyājyaṁ doṣavat* — "action is faulty, give it up." They see karma as bondage, the wheel that spins the jīva in saṁsāra. Their solution: stop turning the wheel. Renounce action entirely. But this is *tamas* masquerading as *vairāgya* — inertia dressed as detachment. The second camp (*apare*) says *yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma na tyājyam* — "sacrifice, charity, penance must never be abandoned." They see action as purification, the fire that burns impurity. Their danger: *rajas* masquerading as *karma-yoga* — activity dressed as duty. Kṛṣṇa's verdict comes in the next verses, but the setup is clear: the binary is false. Action is not the problem; *kāma* (desire for fruit) is. The *karma-yogī* acts without *saṅga* (attachment), like the Sun that illuminates without claiming the light. Today is Maṅgalavāra — the day the blade cuts through the false binary. Gopāṣṭamī — the day the cows are honored, the sustainers of *yajña*. Dhaniṣṭhā (Vasus, wealth) yields to Śatabhiṣā (the hundred physicians, healing) at 6:04 PM — wealth that heals. Dhruva (the fixed point) yields to Vyāghāta (the tiger's strike) at 9:35 PM — the fixed point that strikes. The question is not "to act or not to act" but "who acts?" The *kartā* (doer) bound by *ahaṅkāra* creates bondage; the *kartā* surrendered to *Īśvara* creates liberation. The verse is the scalpel; the day is the surgery. 🏹

The verse presents the exact dialectic the chapter resolves: is action to be renounced (*tyājyaṁ doṣavat*) or purified (*na tyājyam*)? The panchang amplifies this: Dhaniṣṭhā (wealth, the Vasus, the flames that sustain) yields to Śatabhiṣā (healing, the hundred physicians) at 6:04 PM — wealth that serves healing. Dhruva (the fixed pole star) yields to Vyāghāta (the tiger's strike) at 9:35 PM — the immovable point that strikes. Tuesday is Maṅgala's day, the planet that cuts through the false binary of action vs inaction. Aṣṭamī→Navamī (stabilization→power) mirrors the verse's movement: the debate stabilizes into the power of right action. No repeat: BG 18.1 (11-16), BG 18.2 (11-15), BG 18.3 fresh for Day 3. The verse is not a question; it is the scalpel that exposes the false binary. 🏹

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS II.4 — avidyā kṣetram uttareṣāṁ prasupta-tanu-vicchinna-udārāṇām — "Ignorance is the field for the others, whether dormant, attenuated, interrupted, or active." The bridge is exact. BG 18.3's two camps are both rooted in *avidyā* — ignorance of the true nature of action. The "renounce all" camp mistakes *karma* for *kleśa*; the "act for fruit" camp mistakes *phala* for *puruṣārtha*. YS II.4 says *avidyā* is the *kṣetra* (field) where the other kleśas grow. Chapter 18 is the *kṣetra-jña* (knower of the field) teaching: how to act in the field without becoming the field. YS II.12 — kleśa-mūlaḥ karma-āśayaḥ — "The reservoir of karma is rooted in the kleśas." *Kāma* (desire) is the seed; *karma* is the harvest. Chapter 18 teaches how to harvest without planting the next crop. 🏹

Sanskrit Terms

  • tyājya (त्याज्य) — to be abandoned, to be given up; like deprecated API — the interface marked for removal, no longer to be called
  • doṣavat (दोषवत्) — faulty, defective, having flaws; like buggy code — the module that throws exceptions in production
  • manīṣiṇaḥ (मनीषिणः) — sages, wise thinkers, those with *manīṣā* (independent thought); like the principal engineers — the ones who designed the architecture
  • yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma (यज्ञदानतपःकर्म) — acts of sacrifice, charity, and penance; like the core library functions — the essential operations that keep the system alive
  • vicakṣaṇa (विचक्षण) — discriminating, wise, expert; like the code reviewer — the one who sees the dependency graph whole
  • apare (अपरे) — the others, the other side; like the alternative implementation — the branch that takes a different approach
