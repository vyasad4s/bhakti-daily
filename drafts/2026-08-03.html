Bhagavad Gita Chapter 11, Verse 43

पितासि लोकस्य चराचरस्य त्वमस्य पूज्यश्च गुरुर्गरीयान् । न त्वत्समोऽस्त्यभ्यधिक: कुतोऽन्यो लोकत्रयेऽप्यप्रतिमप्रभाव ॥ ४३ ॥

 

pitāsi lokasya carācarasya
tvam asya pūjyaś ca gurur garīyān
na tvat-samo ’sty abhyadhikaḥ kuto ’nyo
loka-traye ’py apratima-prabhāva

 

You are the father of this complete cosmic manifestation, of the moving and the nonmoving. You are its worshipable chief, the supreme spiritual master. No one is greater than You, nor can anyone be one with You. How then could there be anyone greater than You within the three worlds, O Lord of immeasurable power?

 

Today is Somavāra (Monday), ruled by the Moon — the presiding deity of the mind, emotions, and reflection. The Moon currently resides in Meena (Pisces), the mutable-water sign of compassion, dissolution, and unity. The tithi is Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa Pañcamī (stabilization) transitioning to Ṣaṣṭhī (effort) at 1:24 PM — a juncture where the gains of obstacle-removal (Caturthī) are consolidated and directed toward purposeful action. The nakṣatra is Uttara Bhādrapadā (until 12:30 PM), governed by Ahir Budhnya (serpent of depths — stability, deep waters), shifting to Revatī (after 12:30 PM), governed by Pūṣan (the nourisher, protector, and guide of souls on their journey). The yoga is Sukarman (auspicious action, well-doing) until 11:43 AM, transitioning to Dhṛti (steadiness, resolve, holding firm) thereafter. These energies align precisely with BG 11.43: the Moon (mind) contemplating the Divine Father (pitāsi lokasya carācarasya), with unwavering resolve (Dhṛti yoga), guided by the nourishing grace of Pūṣan/Revatī, recognizing the Supreme as the worshipable chief and spiritual master without equal (apratima-prabhāva — immeasurable power).

 

BG 11.43 is Arjuna’s prayer of realization after beholding the Viśvarūpa (universal form). He recognizes Kṛṣṇa not merely as a mighty form but as the primordial father (pitā) of all existence — both moving (cara) and non-moving (acara). This establishes Kṛṣṇa as the source and sustainer of the entire cosmos. Furthermore, He is the worshipable chief (pūjyaś) and the supreme spiritual master (guru garīyān), the original source of all spiritual knowledge. Most profoundly, Arjuna declares that none is equal to or greater than the Divine (na tvat-samo ’sty abhyadhikaḥ kuto ’nyo loka-traye ’py apratima-prabhāva) — a statement of absolute monotheism within the personalist tradition. Today’s planetary alignments reinforce this revelation: Somavāra (Monday) directs the mind (Chandra) toward paternal reflection; Meena (Pisces) dissolves the illusion of separateness into the oceanic unity of the Divine Father; Karka (Cancer) adds the nurturing, protective quality of fatherhood; Revatī, governed by Pūṣan, provides guidance and nourishment on the path to recognizing the Supreme Father; and Dhṛti yoga grants the steadfastness to hold this realization without wavering. This is not mere intellectual assent but a lived recognition: the mind fixed on the Divine as father, guide, and ultimate refuge.

 

From a Sāṅkhya perspective, BG 11.43 describes the state where puruṣa (conscious principle) recognizes its true source and refuge in the personal Divine. To see the Divine as “father of the moving and nonmoving” is to realize that all of prakṛti (the material cosmos, both sentient and insentient) emanates from and depends upon the puruṣa-prakṛti ground — the Personal who transcends yet pervades both. This is viveka (discrimination) in its highest form: distinguishing the eternal puruṣa (the Father) from its ever-changing modifications (prakṛti). The proclamation that “no one is greater than You, nor can anyone be one with You” refutes both impersonal merger (which would imply equality) and the notion of any rival power — establishing the Divine as the absolute reference point. Today’s Dhṛti yoga (steadfastness) supports the unwavering conviction of this truth; the Moon in Meena facilitates the dissolution of false ego-identifications that would claim independence or equality with the Divine; and Revatī/Pūṣan provides the nourishing guidance that leads the devotee to recognize and surrender to the Divine Father. The result is not a distant theology but an intimate relationship: the soul, though distinct, rests securely in the arms of the cosmic Father who nourishes, guides, and holds all existence within His infinite power.

 

YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION

YS 1.23: īśvara praṇidhānād vā — “Or, [this state is attainable] from devotion to the Lord.” BG 11.43 details what īśvara-praṇidhāna looks like: recognizing the Lord as the father of all creation, the worshipable chief, the supreme spiritual master, and the one without equal. It is not vague devotion but precise recognition of the Lord’s unique position as the source and sustainer of everything.

YS 2.1: tapas svādhyāya īśvara-praṇidhānā kriyā-yogaḥ — The threefold practice of Kriya Yoga. BG 11.43 contains all three: tapas (the discipline of maintaining this recognition despite distractions), svādhyāya (the study of the Lord’s nature through continuous reflection on His fatherhood, mastery, and incomparability), and īśvara-praṇidhāna (fixing the mind on the Lord as the ultimate refuge). The verse shows that when these three are practiced, the result is a consciousness anchored in the Divine as the sole refuge.

YS I.12: abhyāsa vairāgyābhyāṁ tannirodhaḥ — “The cessation of mental modifications is achieved through consistent practice and detachment.” BG 11.43’s implicit call to recognize the Divine as father and refuge is abhyāsa (consistent practice of returning the mind to this truth). Its implied detachment is vairāgya — freedom from the false identifications that would seek refuge elsewhere. The Moon in Meena (dissolution, unity) supports this vairāgya: the sense of separate existence dissolves when the mind recognizes the Divine Father as the sole source.

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Recognizing the Divine Father (Uttara Bhādrapadā Morning): Until 12:30 PM, Uttara Bhādrapadā is active — the stability nakṣatra governed by Ahir Budhnya (serpent of depths). Before japa, sit quietly and reflect: what do you consider the ultimate source and refuge of your existence? Is it career, relationships, achievements, or the Divine Father who is the father of the moving and nonmoving? Let the steadiness of Ahir Budhnya support this recognition: like the serpent holding deep in the waters, let your awareness rest in the Divine Father as the unchanging ground.

Receiving Nourishment and Guidance (Revatī Afternoon): After 12:30 PM, Revatī governs — Pūṣan, the nourisher and guide. During this period, consciously cultivate gratitude for the Divine Father’s nourishment and guidance. Recall moments when you felt sustained, protected, or directed by a higher presence — not as metaphor, but as recognition of the Father’s active care. Let this recollection strengthen your conviction: “He is my Father. He nourishes me. He guides me.” This is not emotional excitement; it is the steady nourishment of the intellect, like Pūṣan providing sustenance to the traveler on the path.

Steadfast Recognition (Dhṛti Yoga & Soma Vāra): Dhṛti yoga (steadiness, resolve) is active after 11:43 AM, and Somavāra (Monday) emphasizes the mind’s connection to the Moon. Use this combination to practice unwavering recognition of the Divine Father. Select one routine activity (washing dishes, answering emails, driving) and perform it with the sankalpa: “I am a child of the Divine Father.” Not as occasional remembrance, but as a steady stream: let each action be an expression of mat-karma-kṛt (action done as an offering to the Father). Let the steadiness of Dhṛti and the lunar influence of Somavāra keep your recognition unbroken, like a steady flame protected from wind.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

pitāsi lokasya carācarasya (compound) — “You are the father of the moving and nonmoving”; pitā (father) + asi (You are) + lokasya (of the world) + cara (moving) + acarasya (and nonmoving). Not metaphor, but ontological recognition: the Divine is the source and sustainer of all existence, both sentient (cara) and insentient (acara).

pūjyaś ca gurur garīyān (compound) — “worshipable chief and supreme spiritual master”; pūjyaḥ (worthy of worship) + ca (also) + guruḥ (spiritual master) + garīyān (supreme, glorious). The Divine is not only to be worshiped but is the original guru, the source of all true spiritual knowledge.

na tvat-samo ’sty abhyadhikaḥ kuto ’nyo (compound) — “no one is equal to or greater than You”; na (not) + tvat-samaḥ (equal to You) + asti (there is) + abhyadhikaḥ (greater) + kutaḥ (how) + anyaḥ (other). A categorical denial of any rival or equal to the Divine — establishing absolute monotheism within the personalist tradition.

apratima-prabhāva (compound) — “of immeasurable power”; apratima (immeasurable, boundless) + prabhāva (power, influence, glory). The Divine’s power cannot be measured by any standard; it is infinite and transcends all quantification.

somavāra (noun, vāra) — “Monday.” Ruled by the Moon (Chandra), presiding deity of the mind, emotions, reflection, and the inner world.

meena (noun, rāśi) — “Pisces.” The mutable-water sign of dissolution, compassion, and the return to the source. Governs the vision of unity, the breaking down of barriers between self and other.

karka (noun, rāśi) — “Cancer.” The cardinal-water sign of nurturing, protection, and the maternal principle. Governs the home, the heart, and the foundation of emotional security.

uttara bhadrapada (noun, nakṣatra) — “the latter good foot.” Ruled by Ahir Budhnya, the serpent of the depths. Governs stability, deep waters, patience, and the unshakable foundation.

ahir budhnya (proper noun, deity) — “the serpent of the deep.” The deity of Uttara Bhādrapadā nakṣatra, representing the primal, stabilizing force in the cosmic waters.

revati (noun, nakṣatra) — “the wealthy one.” Ruled by Pūṣan, the nourisher, protector, and guide. Governs nourishment, protection, guidance, and the successful completion of journeys.

pūṣan (proper noun, deity) — “the nourisher.” The deity of Revatī nakṣatra, who guides and protects souls on their journey, ensuring they reach their destination.

dhṛti (noun, yoga) — “steadiness, resolve, holding firm.” One of the 27 yogas, representing the power to maintain focus, remain steady under pressure, and hold to one’s purpose.

sukarman (noun, yoga) — “well-doing, auspicious action.” One of the 27 yogas, representing actions that are naturally aligned with dharma and produce positive results.

pañcamī (noun, tithi) — “the fifth.” The tithi following Caturthī, representing stabilization, consolidation, and grounding.

ṣaṣṭhī (noun, tithi) — “the sixth.” The tithi following Pañcamī, representing effort, endeavor, and directed action toward a goal.

mat-karma-kṛt (compound) — “one who engages in My activities”; mat (My) + karma (activities, work) + kṛt (doer/engager). Not karma as bondage, but every action offered as service — cooking, cleaning, working, speaking — done with the consciousness: “This is for You.”

mat-paramaḥ (compound) — “whose supreme goal is Me”; mat (My) + paramaḥ (highest, supreme goal). The intellect’s highest reference point is Kṛṣṇa; all other pursuits are secondary.

mad-bhaktaḥ (compound) — “My devotee”; mat (My) + bhaktaḥ (devotee, one who possesses bhakti). Bhakti is not emotion; it is the sustained orientation of the heart toward the Supreme Person as the object of love and service.

saṅga-varjitaḥ (compound) — “free from association”; saṅga (association, connection) + varjitaḥ (given up, avoided). Not isolation, but freedom from contaminating motives: association with sense gratification, speculative philosophy, or fruitive desires that distract from bhakti.

nirvairaḥ (adjective) — “devoid of envy, free from hostility”; nir (without) + vairaḥ (envy, hostility, rivalry). The natural fruit of seeing Kṛṣṇa everywhere: no jealousy toward others’ blessings, no competition for limited resources, no ill-will — because all beings are seen as parts of the same whole.

citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ (compound) — “cessation of the modifications of the mind”; citta (mind-stuff) + vṛtti (modifications) + nirodhaḥ (cessation). The definition of yoga in YS 1.2: not suppression, but the natural stilling of mental modifications when the mind is absorbed in a higher object.

abhyāsa (noun) — “consistent practice, repetition.” The steady, repeated effort to fix the mind and engage in service.

vairāgya (noun) — “detachment, dispassion.” Freedom from the modifying tendencies of prakṛti that would pull the mind away from the personal Divine.

kriyā-yogaḥ (compound) — “the yoga of action”; kriyā (action) + yoga (union). The threefold practice: tapas (discipline), svādhyāya (self-study), īśvara-praṇidhāna (devotion to the Lord).
