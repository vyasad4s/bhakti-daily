 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

Āgama as Pramāṇa — The Infrastructure of Transmission: In Sāṅkhya-Yoga epistemology, right knowledge (pramāṇa) has three sources: direct perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), and authoritative testimony (āgama). Of these, āgama — specifically śruti, "that which is heard" — is the highest, because it transmits knowledge that transcends the limits of individual perception and inference. Vyāsadeva is the supreme āgama-transport: the node where the infinite stream of Vedic knowledge was formatted into human-accessible form. Without Vyāsa, śruti remains an undifferentiated mass; with Vyāsa, it becomes a parseable system.

The Guru-Paramparā as Living Śruti: YS 1.26 declares Īśvara as "the guru of even the ancients, because He is not limited by time." The paramparā — the unbroken disciplic succession — is the mechanism by which śruti travels across time without corruption. Vyāsa is the compilation node; the paramparā is the distribution network. When Kṛṣṇa says "I am Vyāsa," He is identifying as the source of both: the knowledge AND its transmission infrastructure. The full moon reflecting the sun's complete light is the prakṛtic analogue: Vyāsa reflects 100% of Kṛṣṇa's knowledge into compilable form, and the paramparā reflects that knowledge still. The Moon in Makara (Capricorn) grounds this in Saturnian discipline — knowledge is not merely received; it must be structured, practiced, and endured.

Buddhi as the Receptor: In the Sāṅkhya chain — mahat → ahaṅkāra → manas → indriyas — buddhi (intellect) is the first evolute of prakṛti and the faculty that receives śruti. Śravaṇa (hearing) is the act of routing sacred sound past manas (which doubts) and ahaṅkāra (which appropriates) directly into buddhi (which discerns). This is why the Śravaṇa nakṣatra is so potent for spiritual practice: it opens the ear-gate to the intellect, bypassing the ego's filters.
