 >>> COSMIC INTERPRETATION <<<

Shukravāra (Friday), ruled by Venus (Shukra), emphasizes refinement, beauty, and the pursuit of pleasure—finding its perfection in the Supreme Person. The nakṣatra Uttara Phalguni (until 11:49 AM), ruled by Aryaman (deity of patronage) and the Sun, transitions to Hasta (ruled by Savita, the Sun) after 11:49 AM, bringing dexterity and skill ideal for discerning the transcendental reality. The yoga Brahma (until 1:50 PM) represents purity and auspiciousness, transitioning to Indra yoga (the yoga of strength and vitality) after 1:50 PM—ideal for upholding the resolve to recognize the Lord beyond dualities. The special observance of the last day of Pitru Paksha calls for honoring ancestors while remembering our true identity in the eternal Supreme Person. As the Amavasya tithi begins at 12:06 PM EDT, the moon's disappearance symbolizes transcendence beyond the mind's waxing-waning cycles and the duality of conditioned and liberated states. Rāhu Kāla (11:18 AM – 12:43 PM) warns against initiating new endeavors during this window; instead, use it for japa and reflection on the verse's teaching.

 >>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

[BG 15.18] Alignment: The selected verse BG 15.18 — "Because I am transcendental, beyond both the fallible and the infallible, and because I am the greatest, I am celebrated both in the world and in the Vedas as that Supreme Person" — is profoundly appropriate for this cosmic juncture. The Amavasya tithi, when the moon is not visible, symbolizes the transcendence beyond the mind’s waxing-waning cycles and the duality of conditioned and liberated states. The Supreme Person’s description as transcendental beyond both the fallible (kṣara) and infallible (akṣara) directly aligns with the Amavasya energy of going beyond dualities. Venus-ruled Friday emphasizes refinement, which finds its perfection in the Supreme Person. Hasta nakṣatra (after 11:49 AM) brings dexterity and skill, aiding the discernment of the transcendental reality. Indra yoga (after 1:50 PM) provides strength and vitality to uphold this understanding. The verse’s declaration of the Lord as uttamaḥ (the greatest) and puruṣottamaḥ (Supreme Personality) resonates with the day’s call to recognize the eternal Supreme Person beyond temporary material relationships honored during Pitru Paksha. Whether reflecting on ancestors or preparing for the new moon, the devotee remembers that the Supreme Person is the ultimate reality beyond all dualities, celebrated in both the world and the Vedas.

 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

[Consciousness Beyond Duality]: This verse declares the Supreme Personality of Godhead as transcendental (atītaḥ) beyond both the fallible (kṣara) and infallible (akṣara) living entities. He is uttamaḥ — the greatest — celebrated in the world and in the Vedas as puruṣottamaḥ.

[Inner mechanism]: From Sāṅkhya perspective, this demonstrates the eternal distinction between puruṣa (consciousness) and prakṛti (material nature). The Supreme Purusha (Puruṣottama) is the conscious principle that pervades and sustains prakṛti without being transformed by it. The Amavasya tithi, representing the absence of the moon (symbolizing the mind), invites contemplation of the transcendental consciousness that underlies and transcends mental fluctuations. The Lord’s entering the three worlds (loka-trayam āviśya) and maintaining them (bibharti) inexhaustibly (avyaya) shows His role as the conscious source that sustains material creation while remaining aloof from its transformations—just as the sun illuminates the world without being affected by terrestrial events.

[Optional terminal metaphor]: Like a system administrator who maintains the infrastructure while remaining transcendentally distinct from the processes they oversee, the Supreme Person sustains all worlds while being beyond both the fallible and infallible aspects of individual consciousness.
