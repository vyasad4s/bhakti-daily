 >>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

[BG 10.8] Alignment: BG 10.8 reveals Kṛṣṇa as the very source (prabhava) of all existence — the ultimate foundation from which everything emanates. This aligns perfectly with Mūla nakshatra's energy of "the root" and its deity Nirṛti, who governs the destructive aspect necessary for new growth. Just as a tree's unseen roots sustain its visible form, Kṛṣṇa is the invisible ground of all manifestation. Nirṛti's destructive energy removes old forms so life can regenerate from its root, mirroring the Gita's teaching that recognizing Kṛṣṇa as the source leads to buddhi (intelligence) fixed in devotion — the wise (budhāḥ) who know this truth engage in bhakti with their whole being.

The purport emphasizes that learned scholars who understand Kṛṣṇa as the origin of Brahmā, Śiva, and all demigods become fixed in devotional service, unshakable by nonsensical commentaries. All Vedic literature agrees: Kṛṣṇa is the source. From Nārāyaṇa (an expansion of Kṛṣṇa) come Brahmā, the patriarchs, Indra, the Vasus, the Rudras, and the Ādityas — the entire cosmic hierarchy flows from Him as the root.

 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

[Prakṛti-Puruṣa Relationship]: Mūla nakshatra's energy reveals prakṛti's dual nature as both binding and liberating force. As the root nakshatra, Mūla symbolizes the fundamental substrate of prakṛti from which all manifestations arise and to which they return. Nirṛti, its deity, governs the destructive aspect (tamoguṇa dominance) necessary for the cyclical renewal of creation — a key aspect of prakṛti's three guṇas (sattva, rajas, tamas) in perpetual dance. The soul (puruṣa) realizes its true nature by recognizing itself as distinct from yet the witness of prakṛti's modifications.

[Inner mechanism]: The process of self-realization involves withdrawing identification with prakṛti's ever-changing forms (body, mind, ego) and resting in pure consciousness (puruṣa). Mūla's energy facilitates this by creating circumstances that strip away superficial identifications — what the Yoga Sutras describe as vairāgya (detachment) and viveka (discrimination). As the soul disengages from prakṛti's modifications, it establishes itself in its true nature as pure consciousness, witness, and bliss.

[Optional terminal metaphor]: Like deleting temporary files and clearing cache to reveal the stable, unchanging operating system beneath, Mūla's energy helps clear the accumulated samskaras (mental impressions) to reveal the ever-present, unchanging self (ātmān) — the puruṣa that is ever-free, ever-blissful.
