 >>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

BG 11.32–33 Alignment: "Kālo 'smi — Time I am, the great destroyer of the worlds." These verses are the first doctrinal teaching after the Viśvarūpa form is revealed. They describe the operating principle of prakṛti at cosmic scale: the gunas (sattva, rajas, tamas) are already cycling; the universe is already being consumed. The Battle of Kurukṣetra was decided before weapons were drawn because Kāla had already drawn them. "Mayaivaite nihatāḥ pūrvam eva" — "By Me they are already killed." This is not fatalism. This is the removal of the illusion that human maneuvering can override Divine timing.

Why on Śatabhiṣa? The "star of a thousand physicians" is presided over by Varuṇa, whose waters dissolve all forms back into the unmanifest source. Varuṇa is the cosmic auditor: His pāśa (noose) binds the untruthful and liberates the truthful. The verse that reveals Kāla as the destroyer and instructs the warrior to "become an instrument" is the precise teaching for a day whose deity is dissolution itself. Śatabhiṣa heals by unmaking. BG 11.32 heals by unmaking the illusion of agency. The resonance is exact.

Why BG 11, not continuing BG 10? The chapter rotation protocol mandates a new chapter after Pūrṇimā. Pūrṇimā (Jul 29) ended BG 10. Today opens BG 11. The verse selection is driven by the day's panchāṅga field, not by verse number sequence — and today's field demands Kāla as the entry point to the Viśvarūpa teaching.

Yoga: Sobhana: "Splendid, luminous, auspicious." Sobhana yoga amplifies the possibility of transforming through dissolution — not merely suffering loss, but becoming radiant through it.  >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

Kāla as Prakṛti's Self-Cleaning Function: In Sāṅkhya-Yoga, prakṛti's purpose is to provide experience (bhoga) and liberation (apavarga) to puruṣa (YS 2.18). The gunas operate automatically. When formations become too rigid — when ahaṅkāra appropriates too much of buddhi's discriminative capacity, when citta accumulates too many vāsanās (memory-traces) — prakṛti initiates a purge cycle. This purge is what BG 11.32 calls Kāla. It is not an external destroyer. It is prakṛti cleaning her own house. The materialist reads dissolution as tragedy; the yogi reads it as prakṛti doing what she was always designed to do: resetting the conditions for puruṣa recognition.

Nimitta-Mātraṁ Bhava — Citta as Pure Instrument: YS 4.24 describes the final transformation of citta: when samskaras and sankalpas have been worn thin through abhyāsa (dedicated practice) and vairāgya (dispassion), citta ceases to act for its own purposes and begins to function for the sake of puruṣa's liberation. This is "become an instrument." BG 11.33 gives the battlefield version: Arjuna is not the doer, Kṛṣṇa is. The arrows fly by Kṛṣṇa's arrangement, the victory is Kṛṣṇa's, the kingdom is Kṛṣṇa's. The warrior's only function is to remember he is the instrument and not the agent. The same applies to every action that flows from a purified sankalpa. The dissolution of I-agency is not the result of effort. It is the natural byproduct of Time eating the structures that I-agency was clinging to.

Śatabhiṣa and the Void-Pointer: The empty-circle symbol of Śatabhiṣa points directly to prakṛti in her unmanifest state (avyakta). When all forms have been drawn into the void, what remains is puruṣa — bare, luminous, witnessing consciousness. Śatabhiṣa is not destruction for destruction's sake. It is the pointer toward the substrate that underlies all structure. Varuṇa's waters (ap) are the fluidic medium that carries dissolved forms back into avyakta. The Moon in Kumbha rāśi — the sign of the water-bearer, the one who holds and releases — mirrors this cosmic process at the level of emotional and mental waters.
