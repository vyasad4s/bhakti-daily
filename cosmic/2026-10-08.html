Verse Selection Rationale

Today is Guruwar (Thursday) — ruled by Jupiter, representing wisdom, expansion, and divine guidance — and the tithi is Krishna Paksha Trayodashi, transitioning to Chaturdashi (Masik Shivaratri). The selected verse BG 15.17 is profoundly appropriate: "Besides these two, there is the greatest living personality, the Supreme Soul, the imperishable Lord Himself, who has entered the three worlds and is maintaining them." This verse directly addresses the Uttamaḥ Puruṣaḥ — the Supreme Person beyond all other puruṣas — which aligns perfectly with Shivaratri, when Lord Shiva (the greatest Vaiṣṇava) is worshipped as the ideal devotee of this very Supreme Personality. Jupiter’s influence as the divine guru emphasizes the importance of receiving this knowledge from a bona fide spiritual master. The Moon’s placement in Simha (Leo) until evening adds a regal, illuminating quality to the day’s energies, reminding us that the same divine light that sustains the planets also shines within our hearts as Paramātmā. Whether engaging in guru-guided study or preparing for Shivaratri worship, the devotee recognizes that the Lord who maintains the three worlds is the same Supreme Person who resides as the innermost self of all beings. The tithi observation of Trayodashi/Chaturdashi until 12:46 PM EDT aligns with the verse’s teaching — recognizing the Lord as the maintainer of all worlds. Purva Phalguni nakṣatra (until 11:50 AM), ruled by Bhaga (deity of prosperity), emphasizes benevolent flow and divine grace — ideal for studying the Lord’s sustaining power. After 11:50 AM, Uttara Phalguni nakṣatra begins, symbolizing patronage and nobility — reflecting the Lord’s role as the supreme patron of all worlds. Sukla yoga (until 3:28 PM) represents purity and auspiciousness, supporting the day’s spiritual focus. Brahma yoga (after 3:28 PM) represents the yoga of creation — perfect for contemplating the Lord as the source of all emanations. Rāhu Kāla (2:08–3:32 PM) warns against initiating new endeavors during this window; instead, use it for japa and reflection on the verse’s teaching.
