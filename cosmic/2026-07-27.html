 >>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

[BG 10.29] Alignment: BG 10.29 positions Kṛṣṇa as the supreme among cosmic administrators: Ananta among serpents, Varuṇa among aquatics, Aryamā among ancestors, and Yama among lawgivers. This aligns perfectly with Pūrva Aṣāḍā's ruling deity Apah (Waters) and its energy of "early victory." Varuṇa, who features in this verse, is the Vedic demigod who governs the waters and enforces cosmic order (ṛta) — the all-seeing administrator of moral law. Just as waters flow everywhere and nothing is hidden from their gaze, Varuṇa sees all actions and maintains cosmic order through the principle of ṛta (cosmic law and truth). The purport emphasizes that Ananta is the greatest of the Nāgas, Varuṇa the greatest of aquatics, Aryamā the presiding ancestor, and Yama the chief of law-keepers — they all represent Kṛṣṇa because they administer His divine order. The placement of Yama (lord of death) in this verse reminds us that every soul faces the cosmic lawgiver's judgment: the materialist after death, the yogī through discipline, and the bhakta through surrender.

Viṣkambha yoga (beginning 1:24 PM) means "supported" or "prop" — it is the energy of foundation, of being upheld. To surrender to Kṛṣṇa as the supreme administrator is to be viṣkambha — supported by the very structure of reality itself. The one who fights cosmic law struggles against the current; the one who aligns with it — who recognizes Varuṇa's waters, Yama's discipline, Ananta's patience — moves with the current toward the full moon of Liberation.

 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

[Ṛta - Cosmic Order]: Pūrva Aṣāḍā's ruling deity Apah (Waters) connects directly to Varuṇa's role in BG 10.29 as the enforcer of ṛta (cosmic order). In Sāṅkhya philosophy, ṛta represents the inherent intelligence and orderliness of prakṛti — the precise, lawful manner in which the three guṇas (sattva, rajas, tamas) interact to produce the manifest universe. This is not arbitrary rule but the intrinsic nature of existence itself, much like the laws of physics in modern science. Varuṇa's all-seeing nature reflects sattva-guṇa's illuminating quality, which reveals truth and maintains cosmic harmony through awareness rather than force.

[Inner mechanism]: The soul's journey toward liberation involves aligning with ṛta (cosmic order) rather than resisting it. When we fight against dharma (righteous action in accordance with cosmic law), we create suffering through friction with the fundamental nature of existence. When we surrender to divine law — not as blind obedience but as intelligent cooperation with the inherent intelligence of the universe — we flow with the current rather than against it. This is the essence of karma yoga: performing action in harmony with cosmic order, unattached to fruits, thereby neutralizing karmic bondage.

[Optional terminal metaphor]: Like a well-tuned engine that runs smoothly when all parts work in harmony according to their design specifications, the soul functions optimally when aligned with ṛta — the natural law governing consciousness and matter. Resistance causes wear and tear; alignment brings smooth, efficient operation.
