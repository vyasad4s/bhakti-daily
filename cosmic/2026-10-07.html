 >>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

[BG 15.12] Alignment: The selected verse BG 15.12 is profoundly appropriate: "The splendor of the sun, which dissipates the darkness of this whole world, comes from Me. And the splendor of the moon and the splendor of fire are also from Me." This verse directly addresses the divine source of splendor, which is especially meaningful on a day dedicated to discernment (Buddhwar) and spiritual observance (Pradosh Vrat). The Moon's role as a reflector of divine light connects beautifully with Pradosh Vrat observances. Whether engaging in spiritual practices or cultivating intellect, the devotee remembers that even the simplest light carries the Lord's essence. The tithi observation of Dwadashi/Trayodashi until 1:46 PM EDT aligns with the verse's teaching — recognizing the Lord as the very source of splendor in celestial phenomena.

 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

[Consciousness as Source of Splendor]: This verse reveals that the divine splendor we perceive in the sun, moon, and fire originates from the Supreme Lord Himself. The sun's radiance that illuminates the world, the moon's gentle glow, and fire's transforming power—all are expansions of Krishna's energy. This is not merely a poetic metaphor but a tangible truth: the very light that sustains life and enables perception is an expression of the Divine.

[Inner mechanism]: From Sāṅkhya perspective, this demonstrates how the puruṣa (consciousness) pervades and energizes prakṛti (material nature). The Lord doesn't merely observe creation from afar; He enters into it (āviśya), sustains it (dhārayāmi), and becomes its very essence (somo bhūtvā). Similarly, the splendor of celestial bodies is not an inherent property but a reflection of the divine consciousness that illuminates them.

[Optional terminal metaphor]: Like a well-designed system where all components draw power from a central source, the sun, moon, and fire derive their splendor from the divine source—Krishna consciousness illuminating all of prakṛti.
