 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

[The Lord as sustaining consciousness]: From Sāṅkhya first principles, puruṣa (consciousness) is the witnessing light by which all contents are manifest, while prakṛti (material nature) is jaḍa — insentient, inert. The verse’s gām āviśya ca bhūtāni dhārayāmy aham ojasā ("I enter into each planet, and by My energy they stay in orbit") reveals that puruṣa does not merely observe creation but enters and sustains it — a direct parallel to consciousness pervading and energizing the material world. The Lord’s becoming the moon (somo bhūtvā) to nourish vegetation illustrates how consciousness infuses vitality into what would otherwise be inert matter.

[Essence-bearing nourishment]: The word rasātmakaḥ signifies that true nourishment comes not from chemical compounds alone but from the divine spark within all living things. In Sāṅkhya terms, this is the reflection of puruṣa’s chaitanya (consciousness) in prakṛti’s evolutes — the very juice of life that transforms the insentient into the sentient. When we eat vegetables nourished by the moon’s essence, we are consuming a manifestation of the Lord’s sustaining consciousness.

[Sanātanaḥ — the eternal basis]: Though not explicit in this verse, the Lord’s sustaining presence is eternal and unchanging (sanātanaḥ). The planets and vegetation exist only by His continuous energy — a reminder that all dependence on material nourishment ultimately points back to the conscious source that makes it possible.
