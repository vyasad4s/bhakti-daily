 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

[The eternal fragmental nature]: From Sāṅkhya first principles, puruṣa (consciousness) is eternal and distinct — each soul is an individual unit of consciousness that never ceases to exist. The verse’s mamaivāṁśaḥ (My eternal fragmental part) affirms this ontological truth: the living entity is not a temporary modification but an everlasting part of the Divine. What changes is condition — not essence. In conditioned life, the soul identifies with prakṛti’s evolutes — the body, senses, and mistaking itself for the enjoyer. This misidentification is the root of struggle (karṣati).

[The struggle of the six senses]: The six senses (mind, ego, intelligence, sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch — traditionally grouped as mind + five senses) are evolutes of prakṛti, situated in material nature. Their function is to perceive and engage with sense objects. When engaged in selfish gratification, they bind the soul to prakṛti. When engaged in devotion — hearing the Lord’s name, seeing the deity, touching sacred objects — they become instruments of liberation. The verse does not condemn the senses but highlights their misdirection in conditioned life.

[Sanātanaḥ — the eternal basis]: The word sanātanaḥ is crucial. The living entity’s essential nature is unchanging and everlasting. What fluctuates is the soul’s identification: with matter (leading to bondage) or with spirit (leading to liberation). The struggle mentioned is not a flaw but the very mechanism of choice. By redirecting the six senses from sense enjoyment to divine service, the soul uses its God-given independence correctly — turning struggle into yoga.
