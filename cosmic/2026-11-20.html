 >>> COSMIC INTERPRETATION <<<

Chapter 18 (Mokṣa/Sannyāsa Yoga) Day 10. Friday is Śukravār — Venus' day, the planet of kāma (desire). The verse exposes the most dangerous tyāga: tāmasika-tyāga — renunciation born of moha (illusion, delusion, ignorance). Niyatasya tu sannyāsaḥ karmaṇo nopapadyate — "Prescribed duties should never be renounced." The verb upapadyate means "is proper, is fitting, is appropriate." To renounce one's prescribed duty (niyata-karma) is not appropriate; it is tāmasaḥ — born of ignorance. Why? Mohāt — from delusion. The person thinks: "This action is binding, I should stop." But the binding is not in the action; it is in the moha. The kartṛ (doer) thinks "I am the doer, I am the enjoyer, I am the sufferer" — that is moha. To renounce action from that place is tāmasaḥ — it compounds the ignorance. Today is Prabodhinī Ekādaśī — the awakening Ekādaśī, the day the Lord awakens from His four-month sleep (Cāturmāsya ends). The sleeper awakens; the illusion ends. Śukravār (Friday) is Venus' day — the planet of kāma (desire), the planet that tests whether desire runs you or you run desire. The panchang amplifies: Uttarā Bhādrapadā (the latter auspicious feet, the water that heals) yields to Revatī (the final nakṣatra, the journey's end, the safe harbor) at 8:20 PM — the journey ends in the safe harbor. Vajra (the thunderbolt, the diamond) yields to Siddhi (accomplishment, perfection) at 6:25 PM — the diamond that becomes the accomplished deed. The Ekādaśī fast breaks the kāma loop; the fast is not tyāga of food — it is upavāsa (dwelling near the Supreme). The verse is the alarm clock: mohāt — from illusion, not from wisdom. Renounce the illusion, not the duty. 🏹

Friday as Śukra-Day: Śukravār is Venus' day, the planet of kāma (desire), the planet that tests whether desire runs you or you run desire. The Sāṅkhya lens: kāma is rajas in its fruitive mode — the rajas that says "I want the fruit." The verse exposes tāmasika-tyāga — renunciation born of moha (illusion). Mohāt (from illusion) is avidyā in action. The Sāṅkhya lens: avidyā is the root kleśa (YS II.3); moha is avidyā manifesting as false renunciation. The verse's nopapadyate (does not fit) is the type error: the kartṛ (doer) tries to cast sannyāsa onto niyata-karma, but the types don't match. The Ekādaśī fast is the viveka-khyāti (YS II.26) — the discriminative discernment that sees the duty as niyata, not as bandhana. The fast breaks the moha loop. 🏹
