 >>> COSMIC INTERPRETATION <<<

Today's celestial alignment marks the beginning of the waning moon cycle under the auspices of Sankashti Chaturthi, dedicated to Lord Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. The Moon's transit through Revati (nourished by Pushan, the celestial guardian) until late afternoon, then shifting to Ashwini (the star of healing and new beginnings), mirrors the devotee's journey: first receiving divine nourishment and completion, then embarking on fresh endeavors with renewed vitality.

The yoga transition from Ganda (the knot, representing challenges and entanglements) to Vridhi (growth and increase) signifies how obstacles, when approached with devotion, transform into opportunities for spiritual expansion. Similarly, the karaṇa shift from Bava (associated with prosperity) to Balava (sacredness) and then Kaulava (social enjoyment) reflects the day's progression from material success to sacred celebration and community harmony.

 >>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

BG 13 Alignment: The knowable Brahman as beginningless (anādi) and the source of immortal nectar (amṛta) aligns with the essence of Sankashti Chaturthi, where Lord Ganesha removes obstacles to reveal the eternal path. The Supersoul's omnipresence (BG 13.14) echoes Ganesha's pervasive presence as the Lord of beginnings and obstacles.

 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

Puruṣa as the Beginningless Knower: BG 13.13's "anādi mat‑paraṁ brahma" describes the eternal consciousness (puruṣa) that is beginningless and subordinate to the Lord, untouched by the changes of prakṛti.

Obstacles as Prakṛtic Knots: Ganda (knot) represents the entanglements of prakṛti—desire, hatred, and the three modes—that bind the puruṣa to the cycle of birth and death.

Ganesha as the Remover of Prakṛtic Bondage: Just as Ganesha clears the path for new undertakings, the knowledge of the eternal Self clears the path of ignorance (avidyā), allowing the puruṣa to realize its true nature.
