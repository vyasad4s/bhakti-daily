 >>> COSMIC INTERPRETATION <<<

Today's cosmic field is structured for breakthrough vision. Caturthī tithi marks the fourth phase of the waning moon — the day of resolution and determination to break through the veils obscuring the true nature of reality. In the Gītā's arc, this corresponds exactly to the moment Arjuna's ordinary sight is shattered and replaced by the divine eye (divya-cakṣus).

Rohini → Purva Bhadrapada nakṣatra transition creates the perfect crucible. Rohini, ruled by the Moon in its exaltation, provides the sensory richness and embodied stability for the vision to manifest not as abstraction but as lived experience. Then Purva Bhadrapada — the "first good foot" — activates transformative fire. Its deity Aja Ekapāda, the single-footed goat, symbolizes the sacrificial nature of spiritual progress: one foot planted in the known, one stepping into the unknown. The moment Rohini hands over to Purva Bhadrapada is the moment Arjuna's eyes become divya-cakṣus.

Dhruva Yoga — the fixed star — holds throughout the day. This is the anchor: even as infinite forms unfold, there is one unwavering point. Kṛṣṇa. The paradox of BG 11.13 is that infinite multiplicity exists within the single, fixed reality. The Moon exalted in Taurus adds embodied richness — the vision is not intellectual but sensory, tangible, felt. This is cosmic architecture engineered for direct perception.

 >>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

BG 11.13 Alignment: The verse is selected precisely for today's breakthrough architecture. Caturthī's energy of resolution and determination matches the verse's climax: Arjuna no longer doubts, no longer analyzes — he sees. The Rohini → Purva Bhadrapada transition provides both the sensory grounding (Rohini, Moon exalted) and the transformative fire (Purva Bhadrapada). Dhruva Yoga provides the fixed point around which the cosmic vision organizes.

The verse describes the universal form as infinite multiplicity within a single point — tatraika-stham: "in one place." This is Dhruva Yoga made visible: countless forms orbiting the one fixed reality. The Moon in Taurus ensures this vision is embodied, not abstract — Arjuna doesn't just understand the universal form, he experiences it with sensory fullness. This is the Caturthī gift: resolution into direct perception.

 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

Citta as Universal Screen: BG 11.13 reveals the citta's true capacity when purified of tamas and rajas. The universal form appears not because Kṛṣṇa has become a spectacle but because Arjuna's citta has become transparent — a flawless mirror reflecting the infinite. In Sāṅkhya terms, this is the moment when sattva predominates completely: the mind's natural translucence is no longer obstructed by rajasic turbulence or tamasic inertia.

Prakṛti's Multiplicity Display: The countless forms within the universal form are prakṛti's full display — the five great elements, the senses, the mind, the intellect, all manifest simultaneously. Yet they appear "in one place" because puruṣa's witnessing consciousness (the Dhruva point) organizes them. The verse is not describing separate objects but the unified field of prakṛti as seen by purified puruṣa-consciousness.

Inner Mechanism: The process from ordinary to divine vision: citta-sattva increases through bhakti → vṛttis become transparent → the mind reflects puruṣa's light → prakṛti's forms appear as they are: infinite, radiant, unified. This is not acquisition of a new power; it is removal of the veil. The divine eye was always present; Caturthī simply wipes the dust away.
