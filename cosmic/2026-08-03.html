>>> COSMIC INTERPRETATION <<<

Uttara Bhādrapadā’s Ahir Budhnya (serpent of depths) provides the perfect backdrop for this verse. The serpent does not flash; it holds deep, steady, unshakable. It represents the stability that comes when devotion matures beyond flashes of emotion into a fixed orientation. The Caturthī→Pañcamī tithi transition mirrors the verse’s arc: Caturthī removes obstacles (saṅkaṣṭhī — the remover of impediments), Pañcamī stabilizes the gain. The Moon in Meena (Pisces) adds the flavor of dissolution into the unified whole — the vision that all beings are parts of Kṛṣṇa, making nirvairaḥ (freedom from envy) not an effort but a natural perception. Today’s Ravivāra (Sunday) illuminates the path: Surya’s light does not create the vision; it reveals what was always there. BG 11.43 is not a theoretical ideal; it is the operable method for the embodied soul seeking to approach the Supreme Person in a way that is actual, realizable, and non-speculative.

>>> VERSE SELECTION RATIONALE <<<

[BG 11.43] Alignment: This verse describes the Divine as the father of all creation, the worshipable chief, the supreme spiritual master, and the one without equal. It establishes Kṛṣṇa as the source and sustainer of the entire cosmos, the object of worship, and the ultimate spiritual guide. The proclamation that none is equal to or greater than the Divine establishes absolute monotheism within the personalist tradition.

Today’s planetary alignments reinforce this revelation: Somavāra (Monday) directs the mind (Chandra) toward paternal reflection; Meena (Pisces) dissolves the illusion of separateness into the oceanic unity of the Divine Father; Karka (Cancer) adds the nurturing, protective quality of fatherhood; Revatī, governed by Pūṣan, provides guidance and nourishment on the path to recognizing the Supreme Father; and Dhṛti yoga grants the steadfastness to hold this realization without wavering. This is not mere intellectual assent but a lived recognition: the mind fixed on the Divine as father, guide, and ultimate refuge.

>>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

Puruṣa-Prakṛti Disidentification: BG 11.43 describes the state where puruṣa (conscious principle) recognizes its true source and refuge in the personal Divine. To see the Divine as “father of the moving and nonmoving” is to realize that all of prakṛti (the material cosmos, both sentient and insentient) emanates from and depends upon the puruṣa-prakṛti ground — the Personal who transcends yet pervades both. This is viveka (discrimination) in its highest form: distinguishing the eternal puruṣa (the Father) from its ever-changing modifications (prakṛti). The proclamation that “no one is greater than You, nor can anyone be one with You” refutes both impersonal merger (which would imply equality) and the notion of any rival power — establishing the Divine as the absolute reference point.

Inner Mechanism: Today’s Dhṛti yoga (steadfastness) supports the unwavering conviction of this truth; the Moon in Meena facilitates the dissolution of false ego-identifications that would claim independence or equality with the Divine; and Revatī/Pūṣan provides the nourishing guidance that leads the devotee to recognize and surrender to the Divine Father. The result is not a distant theology but an intimate relationship: the soul, though distinct, rests securely in the arms of the cosmic Father who nourishes, guides, and holds all existence within His infinite power.
