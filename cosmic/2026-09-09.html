The divine paradox of immanence and transcendence is embodied in Lord Shiva, who as Ardhanarishvara represents the unity of seemingly opposites. Today's solar-lunar alignment in Siṃha (Leo) amplifies the regal, fearless aspect of divine consciousness, while Magha nakṣatra connects us to ancestral wisdom and Siva yoga provides the auspicious energy for spiritual insight.

 >>> SĀṄKHYA LENS <<<

Absolute Truth as Immanent and Transcendent: In Sankhya-Yoga, the Absolute Truth (brahman) is both the unchanging substrate (acaraṁ) and the dynamic expression (caram) of all existence. It is not merely "out there" or "in here" but the very ground of being that permeates all levels of reality while remaining distinct from them.

The Threefold Reality: The verse reveals three principles: the field (kṣetra - prakṛti manifest as body-mind), the knower of the field (kṣetra-jña - puruṣa as pure consciousness), and the Supreme Truth that pervades both. This aligns with the Sankhya understanding of puruṣa-prakṛti dualism with the addition of īśvara as the transcendental regulator.

Subtle Nature of Divine: The reason the Absolute remains avijñeyam (unknowable) to ordinary perception is sūkṣmatvāt – on account of being subtle (sūkṣma). Just as radio waves are present but undetectable without a receiver tuned to their frequency, the Supreme Truth is present in all things but imperceptible to the material senses clouded by rajas and tamas. Only when the buddhi becomes sattvik and purified through practice can it tune into this subtle frequency.
