🏹 BG 15.7 | 2026-10-05

 

ममैवांशो जीवलोके जीवभूत: सनातन: । मन:षष्ठानीन्द्रियाणि प्रकृतिस्थानि कर्षति ॥ ७ ॥

 

mamaivāṁśo jīva-loke
jīva-bhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ
manaḥ-ṣaṣṭhānīndriyāṇi
prakṛti-sthāni karṣati

 

The living entities in this conditioned world are My eternal fragmental parts. Due to conditioned life, they are struggling very hard with the six senses, which include the mind.

 

This verse establishes the eternal nature of the living entity as a fragmental part of the Supreme Lord. The living entity is not created or destroyed; it is eternally existing as a part of God. However, in conditioned life, the soul struggles with the material senses and mind due to identification with the body. The six senses (mind, ego, intelligence, and the five sensory organs) are situated in material nature and constantly seek engagement with sense objects. This struggle is not a flaw but the very mechanism through which the soul can either fall deeper into material bondage or, by proper use of independence, achieve liberation. From Sāṅkhya perspective, puruṣa (consciousness) is eternally distinct and fragmental — each soul is an individual unit of consciousness. Prakṛti (material nature) provides the vehicles (bodies) and instruments (senses) for experience. The struggle (karṣati) arises when puruṣa identifies with prakṛti's evolutes — the body, senses, and mind. This misidentification is the root of bondage. The verse does not condemn the senses themselves but points to their misdirection. When the senses are engaged in sense gratification for selfish enjoyment, they bind the soul. When the same senses are engaged in devotional service (hearing the Lord's name, seeing the deity, touching holy objects, etc.), they become tools for liberation. The word sanātanaḥ (eternal) is crucial. The living entity's essential nature is unchanging and everlasting. What changes is the condition — whether the soul is under the influence of material modes (guṇas) or situated in pure goodness (sattva) engaged in transcendental loving service. The struggle mentioned is not hopeless; it is the field of opportunity. By redirecting the struggle from material enjoyment to spiritual realization, the soul uses its God-given independence correctly. This verse complements BG 15.6 from yesterday: yesterday we learned that the supreme abode needs no external illumination because it is self-luminous. Today we learn who seeks that abode — the eternal fragmental parts of the Lord, currently struggling in conditioned existence. The two verses together describe the journey: the struggle of the fragmental part seeking return to its source, the self-luminous abode where no external light is needed because the source of all light resides within.

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: YS II.17: heyam duḥkham anāgatam — "The pain that is yet to come can be avoided."
YS II.18: prakṛtiśca kriyā guṇasthāna vṛttivit pravṛttiḥ ca śeṣa ṣaḍ-vikāraḥ bhoge apavargaḥ ca — Explains the nature of prakṛti, its modes, and the dual states of bhoge (worldly enjoyment) and apavarga (liberation).
The living entity's struggle with the six senses described in BG 15.7 finds direct correlation in Yoga Sūtra's analysis of prakṛti and the mind. YS II.18 details how prakṛti operates through the three guṇas (sattva, rajas, tamas) and their transformations (vikāras), leading to either bhoge (identification with matter) or apavarga (liberation through discrimination). The six senses (manaḥ-ṣaṣṭhānīndriyāṇi) are evolutes of prakṛti, and their engagement determines whether the soul experiences bondage or liberation. YS II.17's promise that future pain can be avoided aligns with the verse's implication: by understanding our eternal nature (sanātanaḥ) and properly engaging the senses in devotion, we can avoid the suffering of continued transmigration. The struggle (karṣati) is not to be eliminated but redirected — just as Yoga Sūtra teaches nirodha (stilling of vṛttis) not as destruction of the mind but as channeling its fluctuations toward the true self. Both texts affirm that the senses and mind are neutral instruments; their effect depends on the consciousness that employs them. When identified with prakṛti, they bind; when engaged in service to puruṣa, they liberate.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

mamaivāṁśaḥ (ममैवांशः) (compound noun) — My eternal fragmental part; like a process spawned from the main thread — shares the same essence but operates as an individual unit.

jīva-loke (जीवलोके) (compound noun) — in the world of conditional life; comparable to user space — where processes run with limited privileges and are subject to system constraints.

jīva-bhūtaḥ (जीवभूतः) (compound noun) — the conditioned living entity; akin to a process running in user mode — active but not possessing full system access.

sanātanaḥ (सनातनः) (adjective) — eternal; the permanent segment of memory that persists across reboots — never deallocated, always accessible.

manaḥ (मनः) (noun) — the mind; the central scheduler that allocates resources and determines which processes receive attention.

ṣaṣṭhāni (षष्ठाः) (adjective) — six; referring to the mind plus five senses — like six I/O channels through which data flows.

indriyāṇi (इन्द्रियाणि) (noun) — the senses; comparable to keyboard, mouse, microphone, camera, and touchscreen — input devices for experiencing the external world.

prakṛti-sthāni (प्रकृतिस्थानि) (compound noun) — situated in material nature; like processes running in kernel space — operating at a fundamental level of system operation.

karṣati (कर्षति) (verb) — is struggling hard, exerts effort; akin to a process consuming 100% CPU — working intensely but potentially in the wrong direction.

Morning Application

Today, observe the struggle of the six senses. Throughout the day, pause periodically to ask: “Which of my six senses (mind, eye, ear, nose, tongue, touch) is currently most active, and what is it seeking?” Use the awareness of BG 15.7 to redirect sensory engagement toward Krishna’s service: hearing the holy name (śravaṇam), seeing the deity (darśanam), smelling incense (ghrāṇam), tasting prasāda (rasanam), touching sacred objects (sparśanam), and remembering the Lord (manaḥ smaran). When the mind struggles with anxiety or distraction, gently bring it back to the mantra Hare Krishna. This transforms the struggle (karṣati) from bondage into yoga — the very effort that, when properly directed, leads to liberation. Remember: the struggle itself is not the problem; misdirection is. By engaging the senses in devotion, we use our God-given independence to return to our eternal nature as fragmental parts of the Supreme Lord.
