🏹 BG 17.1 | 2026-11-06

 

अर्जुन उवाच ये शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य यजन्ते श्रद्धयान्विता: । तेषां निष्ठा तु का कृष्ण सत्त्वमाहो रजस्तम: ॥ १ ॥

 

arjuna uvāca
ye śāstra-vidhim utsṛjya
yajante śraddhayānvitāḥ
teṣāṁ niṣṭhā tu kā kṛṣṇa
sattvam āho rajas tamaḥ

 

Arjuna inquired: O Kṛṣṇa, what is the situation of those who do not follow the principles of scripture but worship according to their own imagination? Are they in goodness, in passion or in ignorance?

 

Ṭhākur repeated: threefold faith. And why three? Because the entire material creation is built on the interaction of sattva, rajas, and tamas — the guṇa‑tri‑kāṇḍa. Every being inherits a unique proportion, and that proportion decides everything: what we crave, how we think, what we reject, what we feed. On Chaturdashi the moon wanes toward disappearance; its light fades according to that same internal ratio. The question BG 17 answers is not "why is the moon fading" — that's physics — but "who is the witness of the fading?" The faith that answers is not a belief-system but a discriminative knowing: the self is not the faith, the self is the field in which faith arises. And since the field is tri‑guṇīya, the faith within it is necessarily tri‑varīya. Prabhupāda's purport is plain: some are in the mode of goodness, some of passion, some of ignorance — and the nature of one's faith matches that mode. The person in sattva has clear, pure faith; the rajas person has fruitive, result‑oriented faith; the tamas person has faith that is misguided, obstructive, or absent. This is not judgment; it is observation. And the observation has a purpose: it tells the sādhaka where to apply the sādhanā. If one's faith is tamas‑bound, no amount of scriptural hearing will elevate; the vehicle is broken. If one's faith is rajas‑bound, hearing may spark curiosity but will not sustain without transformation. If one's faith is sattva‑bound, the path is open. Friday's Venus‑energy naturally amplifies sattva‑guṇa — the taste for beauty, harmony, relationship — and the panchanga's Vishkambha ("support stake") turning into Pṛthī ("earth") by late afternoon is exactly the stabilisation of rajas into sattva. The verse is not moralistic; it is diagnostic. Know your mode, and work accordingly. 🏹

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: YS I.20 — abhāve svarūpe śuddhiḥ — "In the absence of the false, the true nature reveals itself." The bridge is structural. BG 17.1 names three modes of faith; YS I.20 names the absence of the false. The mechanism is the same: when the false identification (ahaṅkāra as the sole driver) is removed, the true nature reveals itself. And YS II.32's niyama īśvara‑praṇidhāna — surrender to the Supreme — is the natural culmination of BG 17's knowledge: knowing one's guṇa‑proportion is not the end; it is the pivot for surrender. If faith is sattvic, surrender deepens it; if rājasic, it transforms it; if tāmasic, it elevates it. The verse provides the diagnosis; the sūtra provides the method. Together they form the complete sādhana‑circle: know → surrender → transform. 🏹
