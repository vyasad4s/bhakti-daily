Bhagavad Gita Chapter 13, Verse 16

बहिरन्तश्च भूतानामचरं चरमेव च । सूक्ष्मत्वात्तदविज्ञेयं दूरस्थं चान्तिके च तत् ॥ १६ ॥
bahir antaś ca bhūtānām acaraṁ caram eva ca sūkṣmatvāt tad avijñeyaṁ dūra-sthaṁ cāntike ca tat
The Supreme Truth exists outside and inside of all living beings, the moving and the nonmoving. Because He is subtle, He is beyond the power of the material senses to see or to know. Although far, far away, He is also near to all.
From a first-principles Sankhya-Yoga perspective, this verse reveals the paradoxical nature of the Absolute Truth – simultaneously immanent and transcendent, near and far, knowable yet beyond ordinary perception. The field (kṣetra) is prakṛti manifest as the body-mind complex, governed by the three gunas and their modifications. The knower of the field (kṣetra-jña) is puruṣa, pure consciousness that illuminates but does not act. Yet here we encounter a third principle: the Supreme Truth that pervades all levels of existence. It exists outside (bahiḥ) and inside (antaḥ) all living beings – not merely as an observer but as the very substratum of their existence. It is both acaraṁ (non-moving, the unchanging Absolute) and caram eva ca (moving, manifesting as all movement in the universe). This is not a spatial relationship but an ontological one: the Absolute is the ground of both stillness and motion. The reason it remains avijñeyam (unknowable) to ordinary perception is sūkṣmatvāt – on account of being subtle (sūkṣma). Just as radio waves are present but undetectable without a receiver tuned to their frequency, the Supreme Truth is present in all things but imperceptible to the material senses clouded by rajas and tamas. Only when the buddhi becomes sattvik and purified through practice can it tune into this subtle frequency. Though dūra-stham (far away) in the sense of being beyond the gross material realm, it is simultaneously cāntike ca tat (near and here). This is the paradox of the Divine: infinitely distant yet intimately close. As the Kaṭha Upaniṣad declares (1.2.21): "Āsīno dūraṁ vrajati śayāno yāti sarvataḥ" – "The sitting one travels far; the lying one goes everywhere." The Absolute transcends spatial limitations while permeating all space. This understanding aligns perfectly with Masik Shivaratri, the monthly observance of Lord Shiva, who embodies this very paradox – the destroyer who is also the regenerator, the formless who assumes innumerable forms, the distant ascetic of Kailash who dwells in every atom. Shiva as Ardhanarishvara represents the unity of seemingly opposites, just as this verse unites the outer and inner, the moving and unmoving, the near and far.

Today, honor Lord Shiva by recognizing the Divine in both extremes:

  • Meditation on the Immanent: Sit quietly and feel the presence of the Divine within your own body – as the warmth in your heart, the consciousness behind your thoughts, the life in your breath.

  • Meditation on the Transcendent: Expand your awareness outward to feel the Divine pervading the entire universe – in the stars, the galaxies, the empty space between atoms, and the unmanifested potential behind all creation.

  • Unity Practice: Recognize that these are not two separate experiences but one reality – the Divine is simultaneously closer than your breath and farther than the farthest star.

Perform this not as visualization but as direct perception, allowing the buddhi to discern what the senses cannot grasp.

 

sūkṣma (सूक्ष्म) - subtle, fine, delicate; hacker metaphor: quantum field – undetectable by classical instruments yet underlying all observable phenomena, requiring specialized equipment (sattvic buddhi) to perceive its influence.

avijñeya (अविज्ञेय) - unknowable, incomprehensible; hacker metaphor: like the BIOS/UEFI firmware – present and essential but inaccessible from the operating system without specialized tools; only visible through its effects on system behavior.

dūra-stham (दूरस्थ) - far away, distant; hacker metaphor: like a cloud server – physically distant yet functionally present; its distance doesn’t diminish its influence, only changes the mode of access.

antīka (अंतिक) - near, close at hand; hacker metaphor: like a CPU cache – physically close yet functionally distinct; its proximity enables immediate access without changing its fundamental nature.

magha (मघा) - the bountiful, magnificent; hacker metaphor: like root access granted by ancestral credentials – provides foundational privileges and blessings that enable higher-level operations.

siva (शिव) - auspicious, benevolent; hacker metaphor: like a kernel-level security patch – when applied, it removes vulnerabilities and enhances system stability while appearing transparent to user-space applications.
