Bhagavad-gītā BG 15.17

उत्तम: पुरुषस्त्वन्य: परमात्मेत्युदाहृत: । यो लोकत्रयमाविश्य बिभर्त्यव्यय ईश्वर: ॥ १७ ॥

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS I.24: kleśa-karma-vipākāśayair aparāmṛṣṭaḥ puruṣa-viśeṣa Īśvaraḥ — "The Lord is a special soul. He is untouched by the obstacles [to the practice of yoga], karma, the fructification [of karma], and subconscious predispositions." BG 15.17 describes the uttamaḥ puruṣaḥ — the greatest living personality, the Supreme Soul, the imperishable Lord Himself, who has entered the three worlds and is maintaining them. YS I.24 complements this by defining Īśvara as a special puruṣa (puruṣa-viśeṣa) who is untouched by the kleshas, karma, and samskaras that bind ordinary souls. Both texts affirm the existence of a transcendental consciousness beyond the ordinary puruṣa-prakṛti interplay — one that sustains creation while remaining eternally distinct and unaffected by material conditions. The verse and sutra together map the journey: from recognizing the Lord’s supreme position in cosmology (BG 15.17) to understanding His nature as the untouched, special puruṣa who is the object of yoga (YS I.24). This connection is especially potent today on Masik Shivaratri, when Lord Shiva — the foremost yogi and devotee — exemplifies the ideal relationship with this supreme puruṣa-viśeṣa.

Sanskrit Terms

  • uttamaḥ (उत्तम:) — the best, greatest; like the root user in a system — the highest level of access and authority that underlies all operations.
  • puruṣaḥ (पुरुष:) — personality, conscious being; like the core process — the fundamental conscious entity that gives identity and purpose to a system.
  • tv anyaḥ (त्वन्य:) — but another; like a distinct process type — emphasizing that this puruṣa is categorically different from ordinary conscious beings.
  • paramātmā (परमात्मा) — the Supreme Self; like the kernel — the essential, all-pervading conscious principle that underlies and sustains all other processes.
  • udāhṛtaḥ (उदाहृत:) — is said, is stated; like documentation — an authoritative declaration that defines reality.
  • loka-trayam (लोकत्रयम्) — the three worlds; like the full stack — encompassing all levels of manifestation from material to celestial to spiritual realms.
  • āviśya (आवीश्य) — entering; like deploying to production — the act of making oneself present and active in a domain while maintaining one’s identity.
  • bibharti (बिभर्ति) — is maintaining; like the scheduler — the process that sustains and coordinates all other functions to keep the system running.
  • avyayaḥ (अव्यय:) — inexhaustible, imperishable; like a non-depleting resource — a source of energy that never diminishes regardless of how much is used.
  • īśvaraḥ (ईश्वर:) — the Lord; like the system administrator — the conscious being with supreme authority and responsibility for the entire system’s functioning.

Morning Application

Today, on Guruwar (Thursday), contemplate the Supreme Personality as uttamaḥ puruṣaḥ as BG 15.17 teaches: "Besides these two, there is the greatest living personality, the Supreme Soul, the imperishable Lord Himself, who has entered the three worlds and is maintaining them." Throughout the day, remember that the Lord who sustains all worlds is the same Paramātmā residing in your heart. If observing Masik Shivaratri tonight, prepare for worship by studying the life of Lord Shiva as the foremost Vaiṣṇava, whose meditation on the Supreme Person exemplifies the teachings of this verse. Throughout the day, periodically ask: "How can I recognize the Lord’s sustaining presence in the worlds He maintains?" Let this awareness transform your perception — seeing the divine hand in the maintenance of your body, the environment, and the cosmic order — while remembering that the Lord remains transcendently distinct as uttamaḥ puruṣaḥ.
