🏹 BG 12.2 | 2026-08-28

 

श्रीभगवानुवाच मय्यावेश्य मनो ये मां नित्ययुक्ता उपासते । श्रद्धया परयोपेतास्ते मे युक्ततमा मता: ॥ २ ॥
śrī-bhagavān uvāca
mayy āveśya mano ye māṁ
nitya-yuktā upāsate
śraddhayā parayopetās
te me yukta-tamā matāḥ
Translation (Prabhupada):
The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Those who fix their minds on My personal form and are always engaged in worshiping Me with great and transcendental faith are considered by Me to be most perfect.

 

BG 12.2 answers Arjuna's question from 12.1 — "who is more perfect?" — with surgical clarity. It is not about methodology debates. It is about where you fix your attention. The personal form of the Lord is not a concession to sentiment; it is the direct object of meditation that delivers perfection. Fix the mind on the Form, keep it there, and everything becomes devotional activity.

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: BG 12.2's description of fixing the mind on the Divine (mayy āveśya manaḥ) finds its parallel in Yoga Sutra 1.1: atha yoga-anuśāsanam — "Now, therefore, the instruction of yoga." Patañjali opens not with a definition but with a now. The sūtra assumes the student is already here, already ready. Similarly, BG 12.2 assumes the practitioner is already engaged — nitya-yuktā, always engaged. This is not beginner's yoga; this is the mature sadhaka who has already committed. The sutra's "now" is the cosmic moment of Ashwini — new, immediate, demanding response. The cessation of mental fluctuations (citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ, YS 1.2) that follows is not a technique; it is the natural result of fixing attention on the Form so completely that nothing else can take hold. From a Sankhya perspective: buddhi (discriminating intelligence) is the faculty that performs mayy āveśya. When buddhi is directed toward puruṣa (the Lord as the supreme knower), it performs its proper function. When it is misdirected toward prakṛti (the body, the world of objects), it produces confusion. BG 12.2 gives buddhi its correct object — the personal form — and in doing so, establishes the foundation for samādhi.

 

Practice for Today:

Choose one daily activity — cooking, walking, bathing, or any routine task — and perform it with full, uninterrupted attention to Kṛṣṇa's presence. Do not add extra rounds of japa or reading. Simply take what you already do and make it continuous. This is mayy āveśya manaḥ in action: fix the mind on the Form and stay there. When the mind drifts (and it will), notice the drift and return to the task. That return is the practice. Ashwini's speed will test your steadiness; Bharani's fertility will reward your patience. No dramatic sadhana required — just ordinary actions made sacred by unbroken attention.

 

Sanskrit Terms:

Mayy āveśya (मय्यावेश्य) — fixing within Me; The mind's intentional relocation from the world of objects to the form of the Lord. Ā-veśa is not possession; it is attention raised to the level of absorption.

Śraddhayā parayā (श्रद्धया परया) — with supreme faith; Not blind belief but a quality of certainty that arises from direct experience of the sacred. Para means beyond — this faith points toward the transcendent.

Yukta-tamāḥ (युक्ततमा) — most perfect in yoga; The superlative of yukta, meaning "united" or "engaged." These practitioners are not partially united — they are most perfectly so.
