🏹 BG 17.3 | 2026-11-05

 

सत्त्वानुरूपा सर्वस्य श्रद्धा भवति भारत ॥ ३ ॥ श्रद्धामयोऽयं पुरुषो यो यच्छ्रद्ध: स एव स: ॥ ३ ॥

 

sattvānurūpā sarvasya
śraddhā bhavati bhārata
śraddhā-mayo ’yaṁ puruṣo
yo yac-chraddhaḥ sa eva saḥ

 

O Bhārata, according to the existence of each living being, his faith is formed. The person who has faith in this way is truly situated.

 

Ṭhākur repeated: threefold faith. And why three? Because the entire material creation is built on the interaction of sattva, rajas, and tamas — the guṇa‑tri‑kāṇḍa. Every being inherits a unique proportion, and that proportion decides everything: what we crave, how we think, what we reject, what we feed. On Chaturdashi the moon wanes toward disappearance; its light fades according to that same internal ratio. The question BG 17 answers is not "why is the moon fading" — that's physics — but "who is the witness of the fading?" The faith that answers is not a belief-system but a discriminative knowing: the self is not the faith, the self is the field in which faith arises. And since the field is tri‑guṇīya, the faith within it is necessarily tri‑varīya. Prabhupāda's purport is plain: some are in the mode of goodness, some of passion, some of ignorance — and the nature of one's faith matches that mode. The person in sattva has clear, pure faith; the rajas person has fruitive, result‑oriented faith; the tamas person has faith that is misguided, obstructive, or absent. This is not judgment; it is observation. And the observation has a purpose: it tells the sādhaka where to apply the sādhanā. If one's faith is tamas‑bound, no amount of scriptural hearing will elevate; the vehicle is broken. If one's faith is rajas‑bound, hearing may spark curiosity but will not sustain without transformation. If one's faith is sattva‑bound, the path is open. And today, on Govatsa Dwadashi, the day when the cow is worshipped as the embodiment of prakṛti and the living entity's sustenance, the verse takes on a deeper layer: every living entity's faith is formed according to its existence. The cow's faith is sattvic by nature — it offers, it gives, it does not hoard. The human being's faith, however, is tri‑guṇīya, and the task of the sādhaka is to recognise one's own proportion and work accordingly. The verse is not moralistic; it is descriptive. And the observation, as always, has a purpose: it tells the sādhaka where to apply the sādhanā. 🏹

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: YS I.20 — abhāve svarūpe śuddhiḥ — "In the absence of the false, the true nature reveals itself." The bridge is structural. BG 17.3 names three modes of faith situated by existence; YS I.20 names the absence of the false. The mechanism is the same: when the false identification (faith as arbitrary preference) is removed, the true nature (faith as guṇa‑proportion) reveals itself. And YS II.32's niyama īśvara‑praṇidhāna — surrender to the Supreme — is the natural culmination of BG 17's knowledge: knowing one's guṇa‑proportion is not the end; it is the pivot for surrender. If faith is sattvic, surrender deepens it; if rājasic, it transforms it; if tāmasic, it elevates it. The verse provides the diagnosis; the sūtra provides the method. Together they form the complete sādhana‑circle: know → surrender → transform. 🏹
