🏹 BG 11.29 | 2026-08-11

 

yathā pradīptaṁ jvalanaṁ pataṅgā

viśanti nāśāya samṛddha-vegāḥ

tathaiva nāśāya viśanti lokās

tavāpi vaktrāṇi samṛddha-vegāḥ

 

I see all people rushing full speed into Your mouths, as moths dash to destruction in a blazing fire.

 

Caturdashi Threshold — Moths into Flame — This verse captures the inexorable pull of dissolution. Vyatipāta Yoga, "the falling away," is active today: the severing of bonds, the cosmic scissors cutting the last threads of ego-identification. Caturdashi is the final step before Amāvasyā's void, and the verse describes exactly what happens at that threshold beings do not hesitate they rush full speed into the Divine's flaming mouths.

The moth metaphor is not about foolishness — it is about nature. Moths are drawn to flame by design. So too is the jīva drawn to dissolve what is not eternal. The blazing fire of the cosmic form is not cruelty; it is the instrument of kleśa-dissolution. What burns is not the soul but the covering — the identifications, the attachments, the karmic encrustations that prevent the return to the beautiful form.

 

🔥 YS 2.2 Connection: samādhi-bhāvanārthaḥ kleśa-tanū-karaṇārthaś ca — "For cultivating samādhi and diminishing the kleśas." The rushing into the mouths is the very mechanism of kleśa-tanū-karaṇa: the thinning of impurities until they can no longer obstruct the light. As the kleśas fall away, what remains is puruṣa — the pure witness abiding in its own nature (YS 1.3). The dissolution is not annihilation but preparation.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

samṛddha-vegāḥ (compound) — with full speed; complete momentum, maximum velocity. The soul's rush toward dissolution is not gradual — it is decisive, like water breaking through a dam.

vaktrāṇi (noun plural) — mouths; the openings of the cosmic form, points of dissolution and absorption. Like input ports of a system that consumes and transforms.

Vyatipāta (noun) — "the falling away," "the severed"; a yoga potent for spiritual dissolution, breaking bonds that must fall before renewal.

kleśa (noun) — impurity, affliction; the five obstacles (avidyā, asmitā, rāga, dveṣa, abhiniveśa) that obscure puruṣa's true nature.

samādhi-bhāvanārthaḥ (Yoga Sūtra 2.2) — "for the purpose of cultivating samādhi"; the first of yoga's dual aims — union with the object of meditation.

kleśa-tanū-karaṇārthaś ca (Yoga Sūtra 2.2) — "and for diminishing the kleśas"; the second aim — thinning the impurities that obstruct the light.
