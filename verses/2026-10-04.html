Bhagavad-gītā 15.6

न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः । यद्गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते तद्धाम परमं मम ॥ ६ ॥

Yoga Sūtra Connection

YS I.3: tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam — "Then the Seer abides in its own form."

YS IV.34: puruṣārtha-śūnyānāṁ guṇānāṁ pratiprasavaḥ kaivalyaṁ svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā vā citi-śaktir iti — "Kaivalya is the counter-flow of the guṇas, empty of purpose for puruṣa; it is the establishment of the power of consciousness in its own form."

The dhāma paramaṁ of BG 15.6 and the svarūpa of YS I.3 are two descriptions of one thing: the self-luminous, which no lamp can light because it is what lamps are lit FOR. Patañjali's entire technology — nirodha, the stilling of vṛttis — is not the manufacture of light but the removal of flicker: every fluctuation is a borrowed light wobbling across the screen of awareness. When the flicker stills, the Seer is found to have been shining the entire time. And YS IV.34 names the mechanism of Punarvasu's "return" with precision: pratiprasava — the counter-flow. The guṇas reverse and return to their source; the soul does not "go" anywhere — it is established (pratiṣṭhā) in its own citi-śakti, its own power of consciousness. Gītā and Sūtra meet at the same point from opposite directions: 15.6 says the abode needs no sun; IV.34 says the establishment IS the citi-śakti itself. No distance, no journey, no return — only the recognition of what was never absent.

Sanskrit Terms

  • bhāsayate (भासयते) (causative verb) — illumines, causes to shine; like rendering an object onto the display — but this abode is not in the scene graph; it is what the display runs on.
  • sūryaḥ (सूर्यः) (noun) — the sun; the system's primary external light source — itself a rendered object with a fuel gauge.
  • dhāma (धाम) (noun) — abode, domain, seat; the root directory of reality — except this one was never a subdirectory, and every path resolves into it.
  • paramam (परमम्) (superlative adjective) — supreme, beyond which nothing exists; the top of the stack — nothing further to pop.
  • nivartante (निवर्तन्ते) (verb) — they return, come back; a function with no return path — terminal call, the stack frame is never restored because there is nothing to restore it into.
  • gatvā (गत्वा) (gerund) — having gone; the paradox of arriving at your own nature — less a journey than an unmount of everything you were never mounted on.

Morning Application

Today, run a borrowed-light audit. Sunrise is at 7:00 AM — if you can, watch it, and as the sun climbs, remember what this verse says: the greatest lamp in your sky is a lamp. All day, notice what you depend on for light, literal and figurative — the screen's glow, another's approval, the shine of a result. Each is sūrya, śaśāṅka, pāvaka: an object, exhaustible, downstream. Then, for ten minutes, attempt the impossible on purpose: try to see the seer. You will fail — the eye cannot see itself — and that failure is the confirmation of 15.6, the fingerprint of the self-luminous. Rest in that which cannot be looked at. And at 2:43 PM, when Ravi Pushya and Sarvartha Siddhi open, make one single act a complete offering — work, meal, or word — performed as if the doer were light itself. That is how a lamp practices for the abode that needs none.
