🏹 BG 11.25 | 2026-08-14

 

daṁṣṭrā-karālāni ca te mukhāni

dṛṣṭvaiva kālānala-sannibhāni

diśo na jāne na labhe ca śarma

prasīda deveśa jagan-nivāsa

 

daṁṣṭrā-karālāni ca te mukhāni dṛṣṭvaiva kālānala-sannibhāni diśo na jāne na labhe ca śarma prasīda deveśa jagan-nivāsa

 

O Lord of lords, O refuge of the worlds, please be gracious to me. I cannot keep my balance seeing thus Your blazing deathlike faces and awful teeth. In all directions I am bewildered.

 

Arjuna has reached the edge of his composure and finds nothing left to stand on. The universal form's gaping mouths blaze like the fire of time; every certainty he carried into chapter 11 dissolves. "Dhṛtiṁ na vindāmi" — he finds no steadiness. The teaching here is severe: the cosmic form is not entertainment; it is the precise removal of the illusion that you are ever self-sufficient. When the intellect collapses and the senses are overwhelmed, the only authentic move is honest prayer. This is not a breakdown. It is yoga in its most distilled form.

 

🔥 YS 1.23 — īśvara-praṇidhānād vā — "Or through devotion to the Lord." This is not Plan B. It is the summit. When citta-vṛtti restlessness has been refined by every discipline and the mind still runs wild, īśvara-praṇidhāna is the final turn. Arjuna models it without argument, without trying to steady himself first, without waiting for the right moment. The Yoga Sūtras confirm the result: from complete surrender to Īśvara comes freedom from all antarāyas — the nine disturbances that fragment practice. YS 1.29 names the fruit directly: inner realization and cessation of all obstacles. In this verse, Arjuna's disturbance is unmistakable — anavasthitatva, the inability to maintain any state of concentration — which "diśo na jāne" describes exactly: disoriented, directionless, surrendered.

 

Practical Application: Śiva Yoga governs this day: structures that hardened beyond usefulness will crack today. The natural reflex is panic — to grasp, to argue, to find footing. BG 11.25 cuts through that reflex. Stop. Admit the ground has shifted. Cry out for grace. Friday's devotional softness makes the dissolution bearable. The Pūrva → Uttara Phālguni arc mirrors the inner movement: pleasure and worldly success yielding to noble surrender and principled endurance. Build the muscle of "prasīda" now, when you are steady, so it is available when the next dissolution arrives.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

daṁṣṭrā-karālāni (adj.) — "terrible teeth"; the gaping, dreadful teeth of the universal form. Like a debug trace exposing every hidden dependency, the cosmic form reveals what is truly permanent and what is only structural overlay.

kālānala (n.) — "the fire of time / death"; the impartial, automatic dissolution of all constructed forms. It is not random destruction; it is the operating system of the material world. Recognising this is realism, not morbidity.

śarma (n.) — "grace, divine shelter"; the specific refuge that only surrender to the Lord provides. Arjuna cannot obtain it while his citta is scattered, but the recognition of its absence is precisely the opening for its return.

prasīda (v. imperative) — "be gracious, show favor"; the central devotional cry of the verse. Not a demand for outcomes, but a request for restoration after confusion. Recognised helplessness is the precondition for every genuine spiritual breakthrough.

deveśa (n.) — "Lord of lords"; the title asserting Kṛṣṇa's supremacy over every other being. All demigods tremble before this form; Arjuna bypasses them and goes directly to the source.

jagan-nivāsa (n.) — "refuge of the worlds"; the abiding home of all existence. Every temporary refuge — army, skill, status, relationship — belongs to the jagat, the moving world that passes. Kṛṣṇa alone is its nivāsa.

īśvara-praṇidhāna (YS 1.23) — "devotion to the Lord"; complete surrender of will and effort to Īśvara. Not passive resignation but active dedication, cultivated when the mind is steady so it remains available when the mind is not.
