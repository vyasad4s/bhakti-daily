🏹 Bhagavad Gita 13.29 | 2026-09-11

 

समं पश्यन्हि सर्वत्र समवस्थितमीश्वरम् । न हिनस्त्यात्मनात्मानं ततो याति परां गतिम् ॥ २९ ॥

 

samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram na hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ tato yāti parāṁ gatim

 

One who sees the Supersoul equally present everywhere, in every living being, does not degrade himself by his mind. Thus he approaches the transcendental destination.

 

From a first-principles Sankhya-Yoga perspective, this verse reveals the egalitarian vision of the realized soul who perceives the one Supreme Lord dwelling equally in all beings. The field (kṣetra) is prakṛti manifest as the body-mind complex, governed by the three gunas and their modifications. The knower of the field (kṣetra-jña) is puruṣa, pure consciousness that illuminates but does not act. Yet here we encounter the transformative realization: samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram – "One who sees the Supersoul equally situated everywhere." This vision directly addresses the Chandra Darshan (first sighting of the moon) energy of today, where after the darkness of Amavasya, the slender crescent moon reappears, reminding us that the same divine light illuminates all creation. Just as the moon's light reflects the same sun whether seen on a dewdrop or the ocean, the Supersoul shines equally in the ant and the elephant, the saint and the sinner. The verse further states na hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ – "does not degrade himself by his mind." When one sees the divine equality in all beings, envy, hatred, and superiority complexes dissolve. The mind, which typically degrades itself through comparison and judgment, becomes purified when it rests in the perception of universal divinity. This mental purification is the prerequisite for tato yāti parāṁ gatim – "then he approaches the transcendental destination." This understanding aligns perfectly with Chandra Darshan, a special observance celebrating the first glimpse of the waxing moon after Amavasya. Just as the moon's reappearance signals the return of light, the realization of the Supersoul's equal presence signals the return of spiritual clarity. The darkness of comparison and distinction gives way to the light of unity – not a uniformity that erases individuality, but a recognition that the same divine consciousness animates all diverse forms. The Supersoul is further described as samavasthitam – "equally situated." This is not a claim that all beings are identical in their external circumstances, but that the divine essence within them is one and the same. Like electricity powering different appliances, the same consciousness powers different bodies, each functioning according to its unique design while sharing the same fundamental source.

 

🔥 Yoga Sutra Connection: BG 13.29’s description of seeing the divine equally in all beings finds its classical Yoga counterpart in Sutra 1.36: "viśokā vā jyotiṣmatī" – "Or from the inner light that is free from sorrow." This sutra describes one of the methods for stabilizing the mind: focusing on the inner light (jyotiṣmatī) that is free from sorrow (viśoka). BG 13.29’s "samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram" (one who sees the Supersoul equally situated everywhere) and "na hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ" (does not degrade himself by his mind) describe this same inner light – not as an object to be grasped by the intellect, but as the very subject that perceives without sorrow. Where the Bhagavad Gita presents this truth as seeing the divine equality in all beings, the Yoga Sutras offer the direct method to realize it: turn the mind inward to perceive the light that is free from sorrow, which naturally manifests as equal vision. In computational terms: BG 13.29 describes the Absolute as both the universal power supply (transcendent energy source) and the uniform voltage flowing through all circuits (impartial energy), while Yoga Sutra 1.36 provides the user interface for realizing that one’s inner perception (the observer) is non-different from this light – like a voltage meter confirming that all circuits receive the same pure, undistorted power regardless of their individual resistance.

 

Today's Practice: Today, honor the vision of divine equality by cultivating equal vision:

  • Equal Vision Meditation: Sit quietly and bring to mind three different beings – a loved one, a stranger, and someone you find challenging. Not as visualization, but as direct recognition of the same divine consciousness animating all three forms.
  • Moon Offering: If possible, glimpse the waxing moon tonight and offer a simple prayer: "Om samaṁ paśyan hi sarvatra samavasthitam īśvaram" – recognizing that the same light that shines on the moon shines equally in all beings.
  • Mindful Speech: Throughout the day, observe when your mind tends to degrade itself through comparison (hinasty ātmanātmānaṁ). When noticed, gently redirect your attention to the equal divine presence in yourself and others.

Perform this not as technique but as surrender, allowing the buddhi to discern what the senses grasp in duality.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

Samam (indeclinable) - Equal, same, even; like a universal hash function – produces the same output format regardless of input, ensuring fairness and consistency across all data types.

Paśyan (present participle) - Seeing, observing, perceiving; like a kernel-level system monitor – observes all processes with equal access and privilege, providing comprehensive visibility without bias.

Sarvatra (indeclinable) - Everywhere, in all places; like a global CDN (Content Delivery Network) – delivers content with uniform performance and availability regardless of geographic location.

Samavasthitam (indeclinable) - Equally situated, similarly positioned; like a load balancer – distributes requests evenly across all servers, ensuring no single unit is overwhelmed or neglected.

Īśvaram (masculine noun) - The Supreme Lord, the divine ruler; like the root DNS server – the ultimate authority that resolves all queries fairly and consistently, regardless of the domain’s popularity or size.

Ātmanā (instrumental singular) - By the mind, through mental processes; like speculative execution – mental processes that can either enhance performance or create vulnerabilities depending on their nature and control.

Ātmānam (accusative singular) - The self, the individual soul; like a sandboxed process – operates with defined permissions and resources, isolated yet part of the larger system.

Hinasti (3rd person singular present) - Degrades, harms, diminishes; like a memory leak – gradually degrades system performance over time, often unnoticed until significant damage accumulates.

Parām (indeclinable) - Supreme, transcendental, highest; like the hardware abstraction layer – operates at the most fundamental level, enabling all higher-level functions without being visible in day-to-day operations.

Gatim (feminine noun) - Destination, goal, end point; like the system’s bootloader – the initial code that determines the ultimate operating state and enables all subsequent operations.

Chandra Darshan (compound noun) - Moon sighting, first glimpse of the waxing moon; like the power-on self-test (POST) – the initial diagnostic that confirms basic functionality before full system boot.
