🏹 BG 11.12 | 2026-08-07

 

divi sūrya-sahasrasya

bhavet yugapat utthitā

yadi bhāḥ sadṛśī sā syāt

bhāsas tasya mahātmanaḥ

 

divi sūrya-sahasrasya bhavet yugapat utthitā / yadi bhāḥ sadṛśī sā syāt bhāsas tasya mahātmanaḥ

 

If hundreds of thousands of suns were to rise at once into the sky, their radiance might resemble the effulgence of the Supreme Person in that universal form.

 

What Arjuna saw was indescribable, yet Sañjaya is trying to give a mental picture of that great revelation to Dhṛtarāṣṭra by comparing it to thousands of rising suns. The universal form is not an ordinary vision; it is the cosmic manifestation concentrated into a single darśana—all planets, all beings, all time, all space simultaneously present in the body of the Supreme. Material suns are but fragmented reflections of that one original light. When Kṛṣṇa reveals His viśva-rūpa, the conditioned soul receives a direct transmission of that supreme effulgence through divya-cakṣu, divine vision granted by grace.

 

🔥 YS 1.41 — samāpatti as transparent jewel: kṣīṇa-vṛtter abhijātasyeva maṇer grahītṛ-grahāṇa-grahyeṣu tat-stha-tad-añjanatā samāpattiḥ — "When the mental modifications are attenuated, the mind becomes like a transparent jewel, taking on the color of whatever is placed near it—the perceiver, the perception, or the perceived—remaining in that state. This is absorption (samāpatti)." The parallel to BG 11.12 is exact. When Arjuna's citta was purified and made ready through Kṛṣṇa's grace, his mind became like a perfectly transparent crystal. The viśva-rūpa's effulgence entered without resistance. For the practitioner: the universal form is not something to chase; it is something to become transparent enough to receive.

TERM DEFINITIONS:

divi sūrya-sahasrasya bhavet (phrase) — "if hundreds of thousands of suns were present in the sky"; the hypothetical magnitude that would still fall short. Sanskrit uses the conditional mood (syāt) deliberately: the comparison is an approximation offered by language's limitations. Like describing total system memory in a 32-bit address space.

bhāsaḥ mahātmanaḥ (phrase) — "the effulgence of the great soul"; bhāsaḥ denotes active, self-luminous radiance intrinsic to the Lord's being.

Rohini (proper noun) — Aldebaran, α Tauri, the red giant 44× the Sun's diameter. Prajāpati's star, the creative generative nakṣatra.

Dhruva (n./adj.) — "fixed, immovable"; the pole star yoga. Maps onto puruṣa in Sāṅkhya: never moves, never changes, the fixed reference for all movement.

Navamī (n.) — "the ninth"; Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa Navamī marks the final threshold before Amāvāsyā. Completion energy: finish what needs finishing.

samāpatti (YS 1.41, n.) — "absorption, coalescence"; the mind becomes transparent and takes on the color of its object. Not fantasy-visualization but direct perceptual transformation.
