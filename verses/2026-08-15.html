🏹 BG 11.33 | 2026-08-15

 

tasmāt tvam uttiṣṭha yaśo labhasva

jitvā śatrūn bhuṅkṣva rājyaṁ samṛddham

mayāivaite nihatāḥ pūrvam eva

nimitta-mātraṁ bhava savya-sācin

 

tasmāt tvam uttiṣṭha yaśo labhasva jitvā śatrūn bhuṅkṣva rājyaṁ samṛddham mayāivaite nihatāḥ pūrvam eva nimitta-mātraṁ bhava savya-sācin

 

Therefore get up. Prepare to fight and win glory. Conquer your enemies and enjoy a flourishing kingdom. They are already put to death by My arrangement, and you, O Savyasācī, can be but an instrument in the fight.

 

This is the moment the cosmic form becomes intensely practical. Kṛṣṇa has shown Arjuna the universal form — terrifying, magnificent, all-consuming — and now from within that vision comes the instruction: uttiṣṭha — get up. The revelation was not given to paralyze. It was given to liberate. The entire viśva-rūpa darśana has been leading here: you are not the author of the universe. You are not the creative cause. You are nimitta-mātra — the instrumental cause, the farmer who breaks the wall, the hand that executes what the divine has already arranged.

 

The Sanskrit is exact. mayāivaite nihatāḥ pūrvam eva — "they are already put to death by My arrangement." The enemies are not potential victories; they are accomplished facts in the cosmic plan. Arjuna's hesitation is the last barrier between what is already established in transcendence and what has not yet manifested in the field of time. His arrows do not create victory. They remove the final obstruction. The chisel does not create the sculpture. The vision does. The chisel is nimitta-mātra — a precise instrument that removes what is unnecessary.

 

🔥 YS IV.3 — nimittam aprayojakaṁ prakṛtīnāṁ varaṇa-bhedas tu tataḥ kṣetrikavat — "The instrumental cause does not itself create, but it pierces the covering of creation like a farmer [who breaks the barrier between his fields and lets water flow]." Patanjali uses the same word Kṛṣṇa uses: nimittam. The correspondence is surgical. In Yoga Sūtra's causal framework, the nimitta is not the creative cause — prakṛti is — but it removes the last barrier between latent potential and manifestation. The farmer does not create the water. He breaks the earthen wall and lets it flow. This is the metaphysical backbone of Kṛṣṇa's instruction. nimitta-mātraṁ bhava does not mean "be a passive tool." It means: be the precise, skilled instrument that removes the last barrier between divine arrangement and its visible fulfillment.

 

Vyāsa's commentary on YS IV.3 adds another layer: Īśvara is the ultimate instrumental cause who works through all other instrumental causes — time, karma, dharma. The whole universe is a cascade of nimittams channeling prakṛti toward its unfoldment. When Arjuna rises as nimitta-mātra, he steps into his precise role within that cascade. He does not become less. He becomes exactly what he is — a point of convergence through which divine will becomes visible action.

 

Kriyā-yoga (YS II.1) — the practice of tapas, svādhyāya, and īśvara-praṇidhāna — is the training ground for becoming nimitta. You practice discipline to still the ahaṅkāra that insists on claiming authorship. You practice self-study to see through the illusion that you are the creative cause. You practice dedication to Īśvara to learn, breath by breath, what it feels like to be the instrument rather than the hand that wields it. The fruit of kriyā-yoga is precisely what Arjuna receives in BG 11.33: the freedom of acting without the burden of authorship.

 

Practical Application: Hastā nakṣatra teaches that the hand is not the source of the work — it is the instrument through which work is accomplished. Today, your work is to be the chisel, not the sculptor. Act with skill and precision, and let go of the fantasy that you are the source. Caturthī's grace clears the obstacles — Gaṇeśa removes the internal barriers: the ahaṅkāra that claims the work as "mine," the anxiety that insists on being the creative cause. Śanivāra brings the discipline to sustain this — Saturn rewards those who show up, pick up the task, and execute. Identify one action you have been resisting because you cannot see the full path to completion. Rise into it as nimitta-mātra. Don't try to figure out the outcome — pūrvam eva, it is already in motion. Be the farmer who breaks the wall. Be the hand that executes. Be the instrument. Let the divine arrangement flow.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

nimitta-mātram (n.) — "merely the instrumental cause"; the precise metaphysical designation for the soul acting as conduit for divine will. In Sāṅkhya-Yoga's causal framework (YS IV.3), the nimitta is not the creative cause — prakṛti is — but it removes the last barrier between latent potential and manifestation. Like a farmer who breaks the wall between fields and lets water that was already there flow where it needs to go. Becoming nimitta-mātra is not passivity; it is the highest precision of aligned action.

savya-sācin (n.) — "the one who shoots with either hand"; Arjuna's epithet denoting his archer's mastery — the capacity to release arrows with equal skill from either side. The name itself encodes the teaching: the instrument is ambidextrous, adaptable, not bound to one approach. The instrument serves the archer's intention, not its own.

uttiṣṭha (v. imperative) — "arise, stand up"; one of the Gītā's most repeated commands across eighteen chapters. Not a suggestion but a call to embodied action. The imperative mood is essential — there is no philosophical contemplation of rising. There is only rising. The command cuts through the deliberation that prevents action.

siddha (adj./n.) — "accomplished, perfected"; from the root sidh, to accomplish. In yoga, a siddha is one who has perfected practice. As a yoga (today's panchāṅga yoga), it names a cosmic energy of fulfillment — the work already brought to completion. mayāivaite nihatāḥ pūrvam eva — they are already killed by Me. The siddha of cosmic arrangement precedes the sādhya of your remaining work.

kriyā-yoga (n.) — "the yoga of action"; YS II.1 defines it as tapas (discipline) + svādhyāya (self-study) + īśvara-praṇidhāna (dedication to the Lord). The training ground for becoming nimitta-mātra: you practice disciplined action until you discover that disciplined action performed with the right orientation is not different from surrender.
