Bhagavad-gītā 15.13

गामाविश्य च भूतानि धारयाम्यहमोजसा । पुष्णामि चौषधी: सर्वा: सोमो भूत्वा रसात्मक: ॥ १३ ॥
gām āviśya ca bhūtāni
dhārayāmy aham ojasā
puṣṇāmi cauṣadhīḥ sarvāḥ
somo bhūtvā rasātmakaḥ
I enter into each planet, and by My energy they stay in orbit. I become the moon and thereby supply the juice of life to all vegetables.
This verse reveals the intimate presence of the Supreme Lord in all aspects of creation. Not only does He reside within every atom and planet, sustaining their very existence, but He specifically becomes the moon to nourish all vegetation. This is not a distant, abstract concept but a tangible reality: the very food we eat lives by the divine essence of the Moon, which is an expansion of Krishna Himself. From Sāṅkhya perspective, this demonstrates how the puruṣa (consciousness) pervades and energizes prakṛti (material nature). The Lord doesn't merely observe creation from afar; He enters into it (āviśya), sustains it (dhārayāmi), and becomes its very essence (somo bhūtvā). The moon's influence on vegetation isn't a mechanical process but a direct manifestation of divine care. Today is Indira Ekadashi, a sacred day of fasting and increased devotion. Ekadashi observances often involve abstaining from grains and beans, focusing instead on fruits, vegetables, milk products, and non-grain foods. This verse provides the perfect theological foundation for such practice: when we eat vegetables on Ekadashi (or any day), we are consuming what has been nourished by the Lord's own essence as the moon. Our food is not inert matter but charged with divine vitality. The word rasātmakaḥ — "supplying the juice of life" or "essence-bearing" — is particularly significant. It reminds us that true nourishment comes not just from chemical compounds but from the divine spark within all living things. When we approach food with this awareness, even ordinary meals become acts of remembrance and connection to the Supreme Lord who sustains the planetary systems and makes the vegetables grow.
🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: YS I.23: īśvara-praṇidhānād vā — "Or [samādhi is attained] through devotion to and surrender to the Lord."
YS II.45: samādhi-siddhīśvara-praṇidhānāt — "Perfection in samādhi comes through devotion to the Lord."
BG 15.13 describes how the Lord sustains all planetary systems and nourishes all vegetation. YS I.23 and II.45 complement this by showing that devotion to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna) is a direct path to samādhi. Both texts affirm that recognizing the Lord's presence in all aspects of creation — from the orbits of planets to the juice in vegetables — leads naturally to surrender and devotion. The verse and sutras together map the journey: from perceiving the Lord's sustaining presence in the material world to cultivating exclusive devotion to Him as the highest perfection.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

gām āviśya (गामाविश्य) (compound verb) — entering the planets; like a daemon process initializing system services — establishing fundamental presence at the core level.

ojaṣā (ओजसा) (noun) — by energy, vigor; comparable to kernel-level privileges — the fundamental power that enables all system operations.

dhārayāmi (धारयामि) (verb) — I sustain, support; akin to the init system — maintaining essential services that keep everything running.

puṣṇāmi (पुष्णामि) (verb) — I nourish, strengthen; like a package manager updating repositories — providing the essential nutrients for growth and vitality.

cauṣadhīḥ (चौषधीः) (noun) — vegetables, herbs; comparable to user applications — the visible, tangible outputs that users interact with and benefit from.

somo (सोमः) (noun) — the moon; the hardware abstraction layer — a vital interface that translates cosmic energies into usable nourishment for terrestrial life.

bhūtvā (भूत्वा) (gerund) — having become; like successful container deployment — assuming a new role while maintaining essential identity.

rasātmakaḥ (रसात्मकः) (adjective) — essence-bearing, supplying the juice of life; akin to metadata that gives meaning to data — the vital essence that transforms mere substance into life-sustaining nourishment.

Morning Application

Today, on Indira Ekadashi, contemplate the divine essence in your sustenance. Before eating, pause to recognize that even the simplest vegetable carries the Lord's essence as BG 15.13 teaches: "I become the moon and thereby supply the juice of life to all vegetables." If observing Ekadashi fasting, use the hunger as a reminder to turn toward the Supreme Lord who is the true sustainer of all life. If taking special sattvic foods, offer them first in gratitude, remembering that their nourishing power comes from Krishna's expansion as the moon. Throughout the day, periodically ask: "How can I recognize the divine presence in what sustains me?" Let this awareness transform ordinary experiences into opportunities for remembrance and devotion.
