🏹 BG 11.14 | 2026-08-01

 

तत: स विस्मयाविष्टो हृष्टरोमा धनञ्जय: ।

प्रणम्य शिरसा देवं कृताञ्जलिरभाषत ॥ १४ ॥

 

tataḥ sa vismayāviṣṭo hṛṣṭa-romā dhanañ-jayaḥ
praṇamya śirasā devaṁ kṛtāñjalir abhāṣata

 

Then, bewildered and astonished, his hair standing on end, Arjuna bowed his head to offer obeisances and with folded hands began to pray to the Supreme Lord.

 

Today is Saturday — Śanivarā, ruled by Śani (Saturn), the planet of limitation, delay, and the hard-won vision that comes only after all shortcuts fail. The tithi is Kṛṣṇa Pakṣa Tritiya, moving into Chaturthi at 1:37 PM. The nakṣatra this morning is Śatabhiṣa — the "star of a hundred physicians," presided over by Varuṇa, whose cosmic waters dissolve all forms back into the unmanifest. From 11:15 AM, the nakṣatra shifts to Purva Bhadrapada, the "first foot of the fortunate one," whose deity is Aja Ekapāda and whose symbol is the funeral pyre — the flame that consumes the old form to reveal what remains. The yoga transitions from Saubhagya (radiant fulfillment) to Atiganda (excessive conflict/struggle) at 1:52 PM — from auspiciousness into the friction that precedes breakthrough. The Moon is in Kumbha (Aquarius), the fixed-air sign of collective liberation, staying here until August 2 at 5:56 AM.

 

BG 11.14 captures the exact moment the Viśvarūpa teaching lands on the nervous system. Arjuna is not afraid — he is vismayāviṣṭaḥ, "overwhelmed with wonder." His bodily hairs stand on end (hṛṣṭa-romā) — the classic Sign of intense darśana, the somatic response to a truth that exceeds conceptual processing. He bows his head (praṇamya śirasā) and offers folded hands (kṛtāñjaliḥ) — the posture shifts from friendly equality to reverential surrender. The relationship with Kṛṣṇa is not destroyed; it is transubstantiated. Friendship becomes vismaya — awe. And awe is not a lesser emotion; it is the only appropriate response to the direct perception of puruṣottama.

 

The Sāṅkhya lens is critical here. In the internal cosmos of the embodied being, the sequence is: darśana → vismaya → praṇāma → bhāṣaṇa (speech/prayer). Buddhi, having been purified through abhyāsa and vairāgya, finally recognizes its source. The citta-vṛttis that previously occupied the mind with strategy, doubt, and relationship-negotiation are now stilled by the force of direct perception. This is not passivity; it is the most active moment in Arjuna's spiritual biography. He has moved from "Should I fight?" (Chapter 1) to "I cannot fight" (Chapter 2-9 refusal) to "I want to see You" (11.3) to "I am overwhelmed by what I see" (11.14). The journey from confusion to commitment passes through awe.

 

Śatabhiṣa's Varuṇa and Purva Bhadrapada's Aja Ekapāda together frame this verse anatomically. Śatabhiṣa is the dissolution phase — Varuṇa's waters wash away the mental categories that made Kṛṣṇa "just my friend." Purva Bhadrapada is the fire that consumes the residue — the funeral pyre of the old Arjuna. Between them, the actual transformation happens: the human being is "overwhelmed with wonder" because the structures that defined him have been dissolved and then burned. He is reborn in that instant as dhanañ-jaya — the conqueror of wealth who now recognizes that the only wealth worth conquering is the vision of the Viśvarūpa.

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION

YS 1.17: vitarka-vicāra-nanda-smita-ānanda-asmitā-pratyayānugama-saṃprajñāta yogaḥ — the state of samprajñāta samādhi is characterized by deliberation, reflection, bliss, and the sense of "I-am-ness" that remains as the final object of meditation. Arjuna's response in BG 11.14 shows the transition from vitarka/vicāra (conceptual knowing) into ānanda and asmitā — the bliss and self-sense that arise when puruṣa is directly contacted. The hair-standing-on-end (hṛṣṭa-romā) is the somatic correlate of ānanda flooding the embodied citta. It is not psychological enthusiasm; it is the nervous system registering puruṣa's proximity.

YS 3.36: saṃskāra-śeṣaḥ — Even after samādhi, samskaras (impressions) remain. Arjuna's awe is not the final state; it is the gateway. The Yoga Sūtras teach that these residual impressions must be burned through continued abhyāsa. The Viśvarūpa vision is the seed; the subsequent practice is the watering. BG 11.14 shows the seed sprouting. The returned state to normal perception after the universal form disappears will test whether that seed took root or was merely a peak experience. The saints warn: it is easy to be overwhelmed by darśana. It is far harder to remain overwhelmed in the marketplace.

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Vismaya Cultivation (Śatabhiṣa Morning): Until 11:15 AM, Śatabhiṣa is active — the dissolution nakshatra. The morning practice today is not concentration. It is awe-induction. Before your japa, sit for 5 minutes and recall a moment when you were genuinely overwhelmed by truth — a sacred sight, a verse that shattered your mental models, a moment of grace. Don't analyze it. Let the hair-on-end sensation return. This is hṛṣṭa-roma as practice. The citta learns that it can be moved by something larger than the ego's imagination.

Praṇāma with Full Body: Today, before your rounds or your āsana, perform one daṇḍavat (full prostration) with the explicit sankalpa: "I am not the doer of this body; I am the witness before whom this body bows." The body knows what the mind resists. Physical surrender recalibrates the internal axis from ahaṅkāra to puruṣottama. Arjuna didn't just think about surrender — his head literally bent, his hands folded, his whole being reoriented.

Saṅkaṣṭhī Caturthi Juncture: Today is Saṅkaṣṭhī Caturthi — the fast day that removes obstacles (saṅkaṣṭa) through the grace of Gaṇeśa. The cosmic form dissolved the obstacles of Arjuna's confusion. Today, identify the specific obstacle that has been blocking your practice — not the generic "I'm lazy" but the precise vāsanā that keeps surfacing. Fast from that pattern for 24 hours. Not by suppression, but by recognition: "This pattern is already dead in the Viśvarūpa view; I'm just the last one to notice." Let the Caturthi tithi burn it.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

vismayāviṣṭaḥ (past participle) — "overwhelmed with wonder, amazement"; vismaya (wonder/astonishment) + āviṣṭa (pervaded/overwhelmed). Not fear — fear contracts; vismaya expands. It is the citta's response when it encounters a reality too large for its existing models.

hṛṣṭa-romā (compound) — "with hair standing on end"; hṛṣṭa (erect/raised) + romā (bodily hairs). The somatic hallmark of darśana. In the Upaniṣads, this is described as the body's reaction to brahman-contact. It is involuntary; it cannot be faked; it is the autonomic nervous system registering the presence of puruṣa.

praṇamya śirasā — "bowing the head"; praṇamya (bowing/offering obeisances) + śirasā (with the head). The head is the locus of ahaṅkāra ("I am the doer"). Bowing the head is the first physical cessation of I-agency.

kṛtāñjaliḥ — "with folded hands"; kṛta (made/done) + añjali (the palm-fold offering). Anjali mudra is the physical synthesis of left/right brain, solar/lunar, ha/pingala nadi. The folded hands create a neutral circuit — the vacuum that receives grace.

śatabhiṣa (noun, nakṣatra) — "the hundredth division" / "star of a thousand physicians." Ruled by Varuṇa. Its shakti is healing through total dissolution. Today it frames Arjuna's awe as medicinal — the universe's "doctor" shattering his conceptual immune system so the real vision can be received.

purva bhadrapada (noun, nakṣatra) — "the first good foot." The dual-footed nakshatra of transformation; one foot on the funeral pyre, one foot in the cosmic waters. It governs the death of the old identity and the simultaneous promise of the new. Perfect backdrop for Arjuna at the threshold between friend and devotee.

atiganda (noun, yoga) — "excessive conflict/struggle." Not a "bad" yoga; it is the friction that precedes breakthrough. The Viśvarūpa experience is not smooth; it is overwhelming, disorienting, paradigm-destroying. Atiganda yoga makes this verse literal: Arjuna is in the conflict zone between his old identity and the new vision.

vairagya (n.) — dispassion; the guṇa of sattva-perfected discernment. Arjuna's vismaya is the first spike of vairagya — not yet stabilized, but already detectable. The hair stands on end because citta is beginning to detach from rāga-dveṣa (likes/dislikes) and register puruṣa directly.
