🏹 BG 11.13 | 2026-08-09

 

tatraika-sthaṁ jagat kṛtsnaṁ pravibhaktam anekadhā

apaśyad deva-devasya śarīre pāṇḍavas tadā

 

tatra — there; eka-stham — in one place; jagat — the universe; kṛtsnam — complete; pravibhaktam — divided; anekadhā — into many; apaśyat — could see; deva-devasya — of the Supreme Personality of Godhead; śarīre — in the universal form; pāṇḍavaḥ — Arjuna; tadā — at that time.

 

At that time Arjuna could see in the universal form of the Lord the unlimited expansions of the universe situated in one place although divided into many, many thousands.

 

Cosmic Thunder — Divine Vision Unleashed — Arjuna's perception breaks open on Chaturthī. The verse describes not a single cosmic image but a boundless spectrum of forms: the Lord's body as a living mandala of infinite shapes, colors, and radiant opulences, each a distinct dhātu of the material cosmos. The "thousand-headed serpent" of time, the "wheel of fire" of solar systems, the "glittering stars" of distant galaxies — all appear as fleeting expressions of the Divine's līlā.

The key shift: Arjuna is no longer analyzing. He is perceiving. The finite mind, limited to linear cognition, is suddenly gifted with direct apprehension of the infinite. This is not philosophy — this is vision. The divine eye (divya-cakṣus) has opened.

 

🔥 YS 3.38 Connection: saṁyoga-śaktir itareṣām — "By saṁyoga on the relationship between the perceiver and the perceived, the yogi attains the power to become as small as an atom or to reach any desired place." The same reality Arjuna now experiences: the ability to perceive all forms simultaneously. The universal form is not an external spectacle — it is the inner recognition that knower, known, and knowing are one. This is divya-cakṣus: not a supernatural siddhi reserved for elites, but the natural consequence of purifying the citta until it reflects the Divine exactly as it is. Total bhakti → total vision.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

divya-cakṣus (noun) — divine eye; the perfected faculty of transcendental perception. When citta becomes a transparent medium, it receives all forms without distortion. Not a supernatural power but the natural result of total surrender.

pravibhaktam anekadhā (compound) — divided into many; the universe manifesting as countless forms from a single source. The paradox of unity-in-multiplicity: infinite forms, one body.

deva-devasya (compound noun) — of the Personality of Godhead; the God of all gods, the source beyond all deities. Leans into the absolute supremacy of the witnessed form.

Dhruva Yoga (noun) — the fixed star yoga; steady, unchanging, the axis around which all variation pivots. Even amid cosmic dissolution, there is one point that does not move: Kṛṣṇa.

aṇimā (noun) — the power to become as small as an atom; one of the eight siddhis. Applied here to perception: to see the infinite within the finite, the universal within the particular.
