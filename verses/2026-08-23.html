Bhagavad Gita Chapter 12, Verse 12

श्रेयो हि ज्ञानमभ्यासाज्ज्ञानाद्ध्यानं विशिष्यते । ध्यानात्कर्मफलत्यागस्त्यागācchāntir anantaram ॥ १२ ॥
śreyo hi jñānam abhyāsāj
jñānād dhyānaṁ viśiṣyate
dhyānāt karma-phala-tyāgas
tyāgāc chāntir anantaram
If you cannot take to this practice, then engage yourself in the cultivation of knowledge. Better than knowledge, however, is meditation, and better than meditation is renunciation of the fruits of action, for by such renunciation one can attain peace of mind.
This verse outlines a graduated approach to spiritual realization for those who find direct devotional service challenging. It presents a ladder: practice → knowledge → meditation → renunciation of fruits of action → peace of mind. The ultimate recommendation remains direct bhakti-yoga, but acknowledges that souls at different levels of spiritual development may need to approach the goal gradually. The renunciation of karma-phala (fruits of action) is highlighted as superior to both knowledge and meditation because it directly addresses the binding force of karma—desire for enjoyment of results. By offering the results of one's activities to a higher cause, the mind becomes purified and liberated from the cycle of expectation and disappointment. This aligns perfectly with Ekadashi observance, where the renunciation of food, sense gratification, and material pursuits serves as an external expression of internal detachment from the fruits of action. The peace of mind (śānti) mentioned here is the natural state of the soul when freed from karmic bondage.
