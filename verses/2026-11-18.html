यज्ञदानतप:कर्म न त्याज्यं कार्यमेव तत् ॥ ५ ॥ यज्ञो दानं तपश्चैव पावनानि मनीषिणाम् ॥ ५ ॥

 

yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma
na tyājyaṁ kāryam eva tat
yajño dānaṁ tapaś caiva
pāvanāni manīṣiṇām

 

Acts of sacrifice, charity and penance are not to be given up; they must be performed. Indeed, sacrifice, charity and penance purify even the great souls.

 

Wednesday is Mercury's day — Budha, the planet of discrimination, the intellect that distinguishes essence from appearance. And here Kṛṣṇa delivers the verdict that cuts through Chapter 18's dialectic: the activities of the previous verse's second camp — yajña, dāna, tapaḥ — are not to be given up (na tyājyam); they must be performed (kāryam eva tat). The Lord's verdict is final: yajña, dāna, tapaḥ are not optional; they are kāryam — obligatory, the very structure of dharma. And their fruit? Pāvanāni manīṣiṇām — they purify even the manīṣiṇām, the wise, the discriminating ones. Even the manīṣiṇaḥ (the discriminating sages of the previous verse) require purification. There is no graduation beyond yajña, dāna, tapaḥ; they are the very architecture of sattva-guṇa. Today is Budhavār — Mercury's day, the day of buddhi (intellect, discrimination). The planet of buddhi presides over the verse that declares: purification is not optional; it is the kāryam (duty) of every seeker. The panchang amplifies: Śatabhiṣā (the hundred physicians, the star of healing) yields to Pūrva Bhādrapadā (the former auspicious feet, the fire that burns impurity) at 7:40 PM — healing that prepares the ground for the fire. Vyāghāta (the tiger's strike) yields to Harṣaṇa (joy) at 9:12 PM — the strike that becomes joy. The Navamī (Akṣaya Navamī, the inexhaustible ninth) is the day the inexhaustible ākṣaya is revealed: the inexhaustible nature of yajña, dāna, tapaḥ. They are akṣaya — never diminishing, always purifying. Budhavār is the day the intellect bows to the verdict: yajña, dāna, tapaḥ are not negotiable. They are the compiler flags that must be set for the code to compile. 🏹

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: YS II.1 — tapaḥ-svādhyāya-īśvara-praṇidhānāni kriyā-yogaḥ — "Austerity, self-study, and surrender to the Supreme constitute kriyā-yoga." The bridge is exact. BG 18.5 names tapaḥ (penance/austerity) as one of the three non-negotiable purifiers; Patañjali names it as the first limb of kriyā-yoga. The bridge is exact: tapas is not optional self-mortification; it is the kāryam (duty) that generates the heat of purification. BG 18.5's yajña, dāna, tapaḥ map onto the threefold kriyā-yoga: tapas (austerity), svādhyāya (self-study, the dāna of knowledge), īśvara-praṇidhāna (surrender, the yajña of the self). The manīṣiṇām (the wise) of BG 18.5 are the yogin of YS II.1 who practice kriyā-yoga. The pāvanāni (purifiers) of BG 18.5 are the kriyā-yoga that burns the kleśas (YS II.3). The Sāṅkhya lens: tapas is the tejas (fire) that separates puruṣa from prakṛti; it is the buddhi (Mercury/Budha) functioning as the discriminating knife. 🏹
