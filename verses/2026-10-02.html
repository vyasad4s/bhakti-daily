🏹 BG 15.9 | 2026-10-02

 

श्रोत्रं चक्षु: स्पर्शनं च रसनं घ्राणमेव च ।

अधिष्ठाय मनश्चायं विषयानुपसेवते ॥ ९ ॥

 

śrotraṁ cakṣuḥ sparśanaṁ ca
rasanaṁ ghrāṇam eva ca
adhiṣṭhāya manaś cāyaṁ
viṣayān upasevate

 

The living entity, thus taking another gross body, obtains a certain type of ear, eye, tongue, nose and sense of touch, which are grouped about the mind. He thus enjoys a particular set of sense objects.

 

This verse describes the subtle mechanism by which the conditioned soul interacts with the material world through the senses and mind. The living entity, upon entering a new gross body, acquires specific sensory faculties—ears, eyes, tongue, nose, and touch—that are not randomly assigned but are calibrated to the consciousness it carries. These senses then become instruments through which the mind experiences corresponding sense objects: sound, form, taste, smell, and touch. From a first-principles perspective, this illustrates the Sankhya-Yoga framework: puruṣa (consciousness) remains unchanged, while prakṛti (material nature) provides the vehicles (bodies) and instruments (senses) for experience. The senses are evolutes of prakṛti, arising from the tanmatras (subtle elements) under the influence of the three guṇas. The mind (manas) acts as the central processor, receiving sensory data and determining which objects to pursue or avoid based on past impressions (saṁskāras) and current tendencies. The verse highlights that enjoyment is not inherent in the objects themselves but arises from the interaction between the senses, mind, and the consciousness that identifies with the experience. When consciousness becomes absorbed in sensory enjoyment, it strengthens identification with the material condition, perpetuating the cycle of birth and death. Conversely, when the mind is trained to withdraw the senses from their objects (pratyāhāra) and redirect toward the inner self, the same sensory apparatus can become a tool for liberation rather than bondage. Thus, the senses are neutral instruments; their effect depends on the state of the mind and the consciousness that uses them. A mind dominated by rajas and tamas seeks pleasure and avoids pain, leading to entanglement. A mind cultivated in sattva and directed toward self-realization can use the senses as offerings in devotional service, transforming perception into a path toward transcendence.

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: YS 2.54: svaviṣayāsaṁprayoge cittasya svarūpānukāra ivendriyāṇāṁ pratyāhāraḥ — “When the sense organs withdraw from their objects and assimilate with the nature of the mind, that is pratyāhāra.”
YS 2.55: tataḥ paramā vaśyatendriyāṇām — “Then follows supreme mastery over the sense organs.”
BG 15.9 describes how the senses are situated in the mind and enjoy sense objects. YS 2.54–55 complement this by showing the reverse process: when the senses withdraw from their objects and align with the mind’s true nature, mastery over the senses is attained. Both texts affirm that the mind is the central hub—either it becomes entangled in sensory pursuit (as in BG 15.9 when the senses enjoy objects) or it achieves control through pratyāhāra (YS 2.54). The verse and sutras together map the full spectrum: from sensory identification to sensory transcendence.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

manaḥ (noun) — the mind; the central CPU that processes input from sensory devices.

viṣayān (noun) — sense objects; comparable to data streams from keyboards, cameras, microphones, etc.

cakṣuḥ (noun) — eye; the video input device.

ghrāṇam (noun) — nose; the olfactory sensor.

Morning Application

Contemplate how your senses—ears, eyes, tongue, nose, and touch—are constantly feeding data to your mind. Throughout the day, pause briefly to ask: “What am I allowing my mind to enjoy through these senses?” Use the awareness of BG 15.9 to redirect sensory engagement toward uplifting inputs: sacred sound (kīrtan), beautiful forms (deity images), sweet offerings (prasādam), fragrant incense, and the touch of holy objects. By consciously choosing what the senses enjoy, you train the mind to associate pleasure with devotion, gradually transforming the senses from instruments of bondage into tools of remembrance.
