🏹 BG 13.23 | 2026-09-01

 

उपद्रष्टानुमन्ता च भर्ता भोक्ता महेश्वर: ।

परमात्मेति चाप्युक्तो देहेऽस्मिन्पुरुष: पर: ॥ २३ ॥

 

upadraṣṭānumantā ca
bhartā bhoktā maheśvaraḥ
paramātmeti cāpy ukto
dehe ’smin puruṣaḥ paraḥ

 

Yet in this body there is another, a transcendental enjoyer, who is the Lord, the supreme proprietor, who exists as the overseer and permitter, and who is known as the Supersoul.

 

From a first-principles Sankhya-Yoga perspective, this verse reveals the constitutional reality within every embodied being. The field (kṣetra) is prakṛti manifest as the body-mind complex—governed by the three gunas, senses, and their modifications. The knower of the field (kṣetra-jña) is puruṣa, the pure consciousness that illuminates but does not act, remaining witness to prakṛti's evolutes.

 

Yet here we discern a third principle: the Supersoul as transcendental enjoyer (parama-ātmā). This is not another individual puruṣa but the primordial consciousness that both permeates and transcends prakṛti. As overseer (upadraṣṭā), it corresponds to the discriminative buddhi (YS I.23) that witnesses prakṛti's modifications without identification—like a hypervisor monitoring system calls without claiming ownership. As permitter (anumantā), it represents the sanctioning intelligence that allows prakṛti to evolve according to the soul's karma, much like buddhi permits sense-object contact when vijñāna (discrimination) is present.

 

The master (bhartā) aspect reflects īśvara as the controller of prakṛti's evolutionary flow, while supreme enjoyer (bhoktā) indicates that all enjoyment ultimately returns to its source—not as possession but as the complete experience of unity. This is not theological speculation but an observable reality: when buddhi becomes sattvik and detached (YS I.12-16), it functions as the inner overseer that sees thoughts arise and pass without claiming them as "mine."

 

The mistaking of ātmā for Paramātmā occurs when buddhi remains clouded by rajas and tamas, causing the individual soul to identify with prakṛti's modifications. Liberation comes not by rejecting the body but by perfecting buddhi's discriminative function until it transparently reflects puruṣa—at which point the overseer, permitter, master, and enjoyer are recognized as one's own natural state. This aligns with YS II.26: "viveka-khyātir aviplavā hānopāyaḥ"—uninterrupted discriminative discernment is the means to liberation. When discernment becomes continuous, the false overlap between puruṣa and prakṛti dissolves, revealing the Supersoul not as a separate entity but as the true nature of consciousness itself.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

upadraṣṭā (noun) - overseer, witness; hypervisor monitoring system calls

anumantā (noun) - permitter, sanctioner; access controller granting/denying resources

bhartā (noun) - master, maintainer; root user with administrative privileges

bhoktā (noun) - enjoyer, consumer; process experiencing full output

parama-ātmā (noun) - supreme self, transcendental enjoyer; kernel-space consciousness
