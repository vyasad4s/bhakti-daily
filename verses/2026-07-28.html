Bhagavad Gita Chapter 10, Verse 21

आदित्यानामहं विष्णुर्ज्योतिषां रविरंश‍ुमान् । मरीचिर्मरुतामस्मि नक्षत्राणामहं शशी ॥ २१ ॥

 

ādityānām ahaṁ viṣṇur jyotiṣāṁ ravir aṁśumān marīciḥ marutām asmi nakṣatrāṇām ahaṁ śaśī

 

Of the Ādityas I am Viṣṇu, of lights I am the radiant sun, of the Maruts I am Marīci, and among the stars I am the moon.

 

Today is Śukla Caturdaśī (the fourteenth lunar day of the waxing moon) — the day preceding Pūrṇimā (full moon). The nakṣatra is Uttara Āṣāḍhā (until approximately 10:19 AM, after which the Moon enters Makara rāśi). The yoga transitions from Viṣkambha (support) to Prīti (nourishment) at 14:03 PM. This tithi, occurring just before the full moon, carries a sense of completion and illumination, as the Moon’s reflected light reaches its maximum. The planetary ruler of the day is Maṅgala (Mars), granting energy and drive, while the Moon’s presence in Uttara Āṣāḍhā conveys a victorious, invincible quality — the “undefeated” star. BG 10.21 declares Kṛṣṇa’s identity as the moon among the stars, the sun among lights, Viṣṇu among the Ādityas, and Marīci among the Maruts. This verse highlights the Lord’s all‑pervading presence within the celestial hierarchy: He is not merely a distant deity but the very essence of the luminaries that govern time and perception. The moon, which reflects the sun’s light, symbolizes the jīva’s reflective consciousness — dependent on the Supreme for its brilliance. As the Moon waxes to fullness, it mirrors the soul’s progression toward complete illumination in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. The presiding deity of Uttara Āṣāḍhā is the Viśvedevāḥ (the universal gods), aligning with the verse’s emphasis on Kṛṣṇa as the source of all divine categories. Just as the Moon derives its shine from the Sun, all divine radiant sun is itself an expansion of Kṛṣṇa’s viṣṇu‑tattva. Marīci, the leader of the Maruts (storm gods), represents the dynamic force of prāṇa that moves the cosmos; thus the verse subtly links the celestial bodies to the internal pranāyāmic currents that sustain the yogi. Recognizing the moon as Kṛṣṇa’s manifestation invites us to see every phase of our mind — waxing, waning, full — as an opportunity to turn toward the inner light of the Self.

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Chandra Darśana & Ārātrika: During the moonrise (approximately 20:02 EDT) or whenever the Moon is visible, sit facing east and gaze softly at the full moon. Silently recite the mantra “Om Chandraya Namaha” or the Gāyatrī, visualizing the moon’s cool nectar (soma) flowing into your crown chakra, purifying the mind and emotions. Offer water, white flowers, or a sweet rice dish (kheer) to the Moon as a gesture of gratitude for its reflective light, which reminds us that our true brilliance comes from the Sun of Consciousness within.

Marīci Prāṇāyāma: Since Marīci governs the Maruts (wind deities), practice five rounds of Nādi Śodhana (alternate nostril breathing) to balance the ida and pingala currents, invoking the steady, all‑pervading breeze that carries the Lord’s fragrance throughout the universe. Conclude with a brief meditation on the breath as the invisible thread linking the inner sun (ātman) and the outer moon (mind).

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

ādityānām (genitive plural) — of the Ādityas, the twelve solar deities representing the twelve months of the year; the Sun’s sovereign presences.

viṣṇuḥ (noun) — Viṣṇu, the all‑pervading sustainer; here, the supreme light among the Ādityas.

jyotiṣāṁ (genitive plural) — of lights, of all luminous bodies (sun, moon, stars, fire).

raviḥ (noun) — the Sun, the chief luminary; the source of daylight and solar energy.

aṁśumān (adjective) — radiant, bright‑shining; describing the Sun’s gentle, nourishing rays.

marīciḥ (noun) — Marīci, the leader of the Maruts (storm/wind deities); embodies the dynamic, life‑giving breath (prāṇa) that moves the cosmos.

marutām (genitive plural) — of the Maruts, the heavenly storm gods who govern atmospheric forces.

nakṣatrāṇām (genitive plural) — of the stars, the twenty‑seven lunar mansions that mark the Moon’s path.

śaśī (noun) — the Moon, the reflector of solar light; symbolizes the mind and the cooling, nurturing aspect of consciousness.
