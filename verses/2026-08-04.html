Bhagavad Gita Chapter 11, Verse 6

पश्यादित्यान्वसून्‍रुद्रानश्विनौ मरुतस्तथा । बहून्यदृष्टपूर्वाणि पश्याश्चर्याणि भारत ॥ ६ ॥

 

paśyādityān vasūn rudrān aśvinau marutas tathā bahūny adṛṣṭa-pūrvāṇi paśyāścaryāṇi bhārata

 

O best of the Bhāratas, see here the different manifestations of Ādityas, Vasus, Rudras, Aśvinī-kumāras and all the other demigods. Behold the many wonderful things which no one has ever seen or heard of before.

 

This verse is not about exotic divine spectacles - it's a perception upgrade patch. Arjuna isn't being shown rare gods for entertainment; he's getting diagnostic access to the source code of manifestation. The Ādityas (solar principles), Vasus (material substrates), Rudras (transformative forces), and Aśvinī-kumāras (healing duality) aren't separate deities to worship - they're background processes running in universal consciousness.

 

The key phrase is "adṛṣṭa-pūrvāṇi" (not seen before). This isn't about adding new beliefs to your mental model - it's about upgrading your perceptual firmware to see what was always running in the background. Just as a debugger reveals hidden processes in a system, divine vision reveals the ever-present modalities of prakṛti that the conditioned mind filters out as "ordinary."

 

Today's astronomical alignments mirror this perceptual shift: Revatī (Pūṣan, the navigator) yielding to Aśvinī (the divine physicians) represents the transition from guidance to healing - exactly what happens when you stop interpreting experience through conditioned labels and start perceiving underlying patterns. The Moon's transit from Meena (boundary-dissolving Pisces) to Mesha (initiatory Aries) moves you from oceanic unity to focused perception - not losing the wholeness, but gaining the ability to discern its parts.

 

YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION

YS II.10-11: te pratiprasavaheyaḥ sūkṣmāḥ - "These [kleśas] are to be resolved back into their primal cause; the active ones are to be overcome by meditation." The adṛṣṭa-pūrvāṇi (unseen before) perceptions in BG 11.6 happen precisely when the kleśas (particularly avidyā - mistaken identity) are temporarily suspended through focused attention.

YS I.2: yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ - Yoga is the cessation of mental modifications. The "wondrous things never seen before" appear not through added vision, but through subtracted distortion. When the mind stops interpreting, what was always visible becomes visible.

YS III.4-5: trayam ekatra saṃyamaḥ - The threefold practice of concentration, meditation, and samyama leads to prajñā (illumination). Today's practice isn't about gaining supernatural sight - it's about applying the standard yogic technology of attention to ordinary perception.

Not contemplating its divine nature - simply observing its actual characteristics without labeling. Train the mind to see what's there, not what you assume is there.

āścaryāṇi (adjective) — Wondrous, marvelous, astonishing

darśana (noun) — Seeing, perception, vision (especially of the divine)
