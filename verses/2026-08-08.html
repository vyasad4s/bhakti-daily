🏹 BG 11.36 | 2026-08-08

 

arjuna uvāca

sthāne hṛṣīkeśa tava prakīrtyā

jagat prahṛṣyaty anurajyate ca

rakṣāṁsi bhītāni diśo dravanti

sarve namasyanti ca siddha-saṅghāḥ

 

arjuna uvāca — Arjuna said; sthāne — rightly; hṛṣīkeśa — O master of all senses; tava — Your; prakīrtyā — by the glories; jagat — the world; prahṛṣyati — rejoices; anurajyate — becomes attached; ca — and; rakṣāṁsi — demons; bhītāni — frightened; diśaḥ — in all directions; dravanti — flee; sarve — all; namasyanti — offer respects; ca — also; siddha-saṅghāḥ — the gathered perfected beings

 

Arjuna said: O master of the senses, the world becomes joyful upon hearing Your name, and thus everyone becomes attached to You. Although the perfected beings offer You their respectful homage, the demons are afraid, and they flee here and there. All this is rightly done.

 

Arjuna describes here the two-fold response to the Lord's glories: perfected beings bow in recognition; demons flee in terror. The critical word is "sthāne" — rightly, fitly, appropriately. Arjuna is not describing a chaotic cosmic reaction; he is describing a precise, discriminative process. The Lord's glories do not convert everyone — they reveal what every being already is. The siddhas bow because they recognize what they have always known. The demons flee because their structure cannot tolerate what is genuinely real. Neither outcome is a new event; both are the honest disclosure of pre-existing conditions.

 

🔥 YS 2.47 — prayatna śaithilyānanta samāpatti: "Effort is relaxed when the effort to unite with the infinite is attained." Patanjali describes the moment when striving ends because union has been achieved — not by force but by yielding into something larger. The siddhas in BG 11.36 demonstrate this: they have already relaxed their effort into the infinite, so when they encounter the Lord's glories, they bow without friction. The demons demonstrate the opposite: their effort is entirely directed at maintaining a separate self, so when infinite reality appears, their tension collapses into flight. For the practitioner, the teaching is exact: your practice is working when it becomes easier, not harder — when the effort relaxes rather than intensifies. Vyāghāta yoga governs this day precisely because it attacks effort that is out of alignment: the fake effort crumbles; the real effort deepens.

 

Practical Application: Kamika Ekādaśī is the day to audit your own effort. Where are you striving against the current of your practice? Where are you forcing something that should unfold? The demons in this verse are the parts of your practice that are performative — the chants you do to appear devotional, the disciplines you maintain to look spiritual. Those will be revealed today and they will flee if you let them. The siddhas in this verse are the parts of your practice that are genuine — the moments of real connection, the spontaneous bhakti that arises without planning. Those will deepen today. Let Vyāghāta do its work. Do not rescue your demons. Let the Rohini creative energy flow into what is genuine. Let Śanivāra's slow processing finish what needs to finish.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

sthāne (adv.) — "rightly, fitly, appropriately"; Arjuna's assessment that the Lord's actions are correct in every instance. This is not passive acceptance; it is the culmination of discriminative knowledge that recognizes the Lord's justice is always precisely calibrated.

hṛṣīkeśa (n.) — "master of all senses"; acknowledges that the hṛṣīka — senses, mind — are not autonomous; they belong to Īśvara. When this is recognized, the senses stop running wild and become instruments of bhakti. Like a system administrator reclaiming control of a compromised process tree.

rakṣāṁsi (n.) — "demons, rākṣasas"; inner patterns of defending a false identity. When truth is spoken, they flee. They are not external beings to be destroyed — they are the structural overlay of ego that cannot tolerate reality.

siddha-saṅghāḥ (n.) — "the assembled perfected beings"; those whose citta is purified and who recognize the Lord immediately. Their bowing is not new devotion — it is the consummation of a lifelong orientation. Like code that finally compiles after years of debugging.

Vyāghāta (n.) — "the attacker, the obstacle-smasher"; today's active yoga. Vyāghāta smashes everything — valuable and worthless alike. It is not selective. This is why Ekādaśī paired with Vyāghāta is intense: the dissolution is non-negotiable.

prakīrtyā (n.) — "by the glories, by recounting"; the mechanism of transformation in this verse. The Lord's glories are active — they reorganize reality around themselves. Demons recoil; perfected beings advance. The glories do not need to convert anyone; they simply are what they are.
