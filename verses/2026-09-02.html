🏹 BG 13.30 | 2026-09-02

 

प्रकृत्यैव च कर्माणि क्रियमाणानि सर्वश: । य: पश्यति तथात्मानमकर्तारं स पश्यति ॥ ३० ॥

 

prakṛtyaiva ca karmāṇi
kriyamāṇāni sarvaśaḥ
yaḥ paśyati tathātmānam
akartāraṁ sa paśyati

 

One who can see that all activities are performed by the body, which is created of material nature, and sees that the self does nothing, actually sees.

 

From a first-principles Sankhya-Yoga perspective, this verse reveals the pinnacle of discriminative knowledge (viveka). The field (kṣetra) is prakṛti manifest as the body-mind complex—governed by the three gunas, senses, and their modifications, all operating as an automatic machine. The knower of the field (kṣetra-jña) is puruṣa, pure consciousness that illuminates but does not initiate action, remaining the silent witness to prakṛti's evolutes. The realization expressed here—"I am not the doer"—is not a philosophical idea but an experiential truth that dawns when buddhi becomes sattvik and detached (YS I.12-16). Like a kernel process that sees user-space applications run without claiming ownership, the purified buddhi observes the body-machine executing actions driven by prakṛti's gunas while remaining untouched by the notion of "I am acting." This is the state of akartāram (non-doer), where the false identification with the body's activities dissolves. The body is indeed a machine—designed by prakṛti under the sanction of the Supersoul—to fulfill past desires (sañcita karma). Hunger leads to eating, fatigue leads to resting, emotion leads to speaking—not because the self "decides" but because the biological apparatus responds to stimuli. When buddhi develops continuous discriminative discernment (YS II.26), it transparently reflects puruṣa, allowing one to see thoughts arise and pass, sensations occur and fade, and actions unfold—all without the superimposition of agency. This is not passivity but clarity: actions happen through the body, while the self remains the changeless witness.

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: I.3 (tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe ‘vasthānam) — Then the Seer abides in its own nature. This sutra describes the state of self-realization where pure consciousness (puruṣa) rests in its true nature, free from identification with the fluctuations of the mind-field (citta-vṛtti). BG 13.30 expresses this same realization: seeing that all actions belong to prakṛti while the self remains the changeless witness.

akartāram (noun) - non-doer; one who does not act; hacker metaphor: passive observer process with no write permissions
