Chapter 10, Verse 37

वृष्णीनां वासुदेवोऽस्मि पाण्डवानां धनञ्जय: । मुनीनामप्यहं व्यास: कवीनामुशना कवि: ॥ ३७ ॥

 

vṛṣṇīnāṁ vāsudevo ’smi pāṇḍavānāṁ dhanañ-jayaḥ munīnām apy ahaṁ vyāsaḥ kavīnām uśanā kaviḥ

 

Of the descendants of Vṛṣṇi I am Vāsudeva, and of the Pāṇḍavas I am Arjuna. Of the sages I am Vyāsa, and among great thinkers I am Uśanā.

 

Today is Pūrṇimā — the full moon — and not just any full moon. It is Vyāsa Pūjā, the day the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava tradition honors Śrīla Vyāsadeva, the compiler of all Vedic literature. The nakṣatra is Śravaṇa, whose deity is Viṣṇu and whose very name means "hearing" — the act of receiving knowledge through śabda, sacred sound. The Moon entered Makara rāśi (Capricorn) at dawn, grounding the full moon's luminosity in the Saturn-ruled sign of discipline, structure, and endurance. The yoga Prīti (nourishment) transitions to Ayuṣman (long-lived) at 2:27 PM — from the sweetness of devotion to the longevity that comes from living in alignment with dharma. On this day, Kṛṣṇa's declaration in BG 10.37 — "of the sages I am Vyāsa" — lands with the force of cosmic precision. It is not metaphor. It is not sentiment. It is the Supreme Lord identifying Himself as the very compiler of the knowledge that leads souls back to Him.

 

Vyāsadeva is not merely one sage among many. He is the sage who structured the Vedic corpus into four, who composed the Mahābhārata, the Purāṇas, and the Brahma-sūtras. Without Vyāsa, the śruti would remain an undifferentiated mass; the path of return would be unparsed, uncompiled, inaccessible to the Kali-yuga mind. When Kṛṣṇa says "munīnām apy ahaṁ vyāsaḥ," He is declaring that the systematic transmission of spiritual knowledge IS His vibhūti — His divine opulence. The act of compiling, organizing, and transmitting truth is not a human achievement; it is Kṛṣṇa acting through His empowered servant. This is why Vyāsa Pūjā is not merely a birthday celebration — it is the recognition that knowledge itself is a manifestation of the Divine.

 

The Śravaṇa nakṣatra deepens this connection. Śravaṇa means "the ear" — the organ of hearing, the primary instrument of śruti ("that which is heard"). The full moon of Pūrṇimā represents completeness: the Moon reflecting the Sun's full light, just as Vyāsa reflects the full light of Kṛṣṇa's knowledge into the world. Among the descendants of Vṛṣṇi, Kṛṣṇa is Vāsudeva — the all-pervading Supreme. Among the Pāṇḍavas, He is Arjuna — the warrior who received the Gītā itself. And among the sages, He is Vyāsa — the one who compiled the knowledge so that we could hear it. The paramparā is the living river of śruti, and Vyāsa is its delta — where the infinite stream was channeled into human-accessible form.

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Guru-paramparā Smaraṇa: Today is Vyāsa Pūjā — the celebration of the compiler of all knowledge. Take time to honor your guru-paramparā. Light incense and a ghee lamp before a picture of Śrīla Prabhupāda, your initiating/instructing guru, and the disciplic succession. Chant the praṇāma mantras of the paramparā, from Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Mahāprabhu down to your own spiritual master. This is not ritual for ritual's sake — it is the act of plugging into the current of śruti that Vyāsa compiled and the paramparā preserved.

Śravaṇa-śraddhā: The Śravaṇa nakṣatra governs hearing. Today, dedicate 30 minutes to śravaṇam — actively listening to a recorded class, kīrtana, or recitation of śāstra. Not passive consumption, but the yogic act of receiving sound through the ear-gate directly into Buddhi. Let the sound reshape the citta without the interference of analysis.

Pūrṇimā Contemplation: At moonrise (8:32 PM EDT), step outside and gaze at the full Moon in Makara rāśi. Consider: the Moon reflects 100% of the Sun's light today. Vyāsa reflected 100% of Kṛṣṇa's knowledge into compilable form. The paramparā reflects that knowledge still. Where are you in the reflection chain?

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

vṛṣṇīnām (genitive plural) — of the Vṛṣṇi dynasty, the Yadu lineage from which Kṛṣṇa descended; the royal house of Mathurā and Dvārakā.

vāsudevaḥ (noun) — Vāsudeva, Kṛṣṇa as the son of Vasudeva; also indicates Balarāma as the original expansion. Here, the Supreme among the Vṛṣṇis.

pāṇḍavānām (genitive plural) — of the Pāṇḍavas, the five sons of Pāṇḍu; the protagonists of the Mahābhārata.

dhanañ-jayaḥ (noun) — "Conqueror of Wealth," an epithet of Arjuna; he who amassed treasures through his bow and skill.

munīnām (genitive plural) — of the munis, the silent sages; those who have attained mastery over speech and mind through tapas.

vyāsaḥ (noun) — Vyāsadeva, "the compiler"; the avatar of Kṛṣṇa who divided the Veda into four, composed the Mahābhārata, Purāṇas, and Brahma-sūtras. The greatest muni because he structured transcendental knowledge for mass transmission.

kavīnām (genitive plural) — of the kavis, the great thinkers; poets and intellectuals capable of deep penetration into any subject.

uśanā (noun) — Uśanā, also known as Śukrācārya; the preceptor of the asuras, master of politics and strategy; a kavi of unparalleled intelligence.

śravaṇa (noun) — "hearing"; the 22nd nakṣatra, ruled by Viṣṇu, symbolized by three footprints. The power of listening as the gateway to knowledge.

āgama (noun) — authoritative verbal testimony; the third source of pramāṇa in Patañjali's system. Śruti is the highest āgama.

paramparā (noun) — disciplic succession; the unbroken chain of guru-to-disciple transmission preserving śruti across generations.
