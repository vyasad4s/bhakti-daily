🏹 BG 15.18 | 2026-10-09

 

यस्मात्क्षरमतीतोऽहमक्षरादपि चोत्तम: । अतोऽस्मि लोके वेदे च प्रथित: पुरुषोत्तम: ॥ १८ ॥

 

yasmāt kṣaram atīto ’ham akṣarād api cottamaḥ ato ’smi loke vede ca prathitaḥ puruṣottamaḥ

 

Because I am transcendental, beyond both the fallible and the infallible, and because I am the greatest, I am celebrated both in the world and in the Vedas as that Supreme Person.

 

This verse declares the Supreme Personality of Godhead as transcendental (atītaḥ) beyond both the fallible (kṣara) and infallible (akṣara) living entities. He is uttamaḥ — the greatest — celebrated in the world and in the Vedas as puruṣottamaḥ. Today’s panchāṅga powerfully illuminates this truth: we stand on the cusp of Amavasya (new moon), when the moon — symbolizing the mind (mana) and its waxing-waning cycles — disappears from view. The Amavasya tithi begins at 12:06 PM EDT, marking a moment of darkness and potential, wherein the dualities of conditioned and liberated states are transcended by the Supreme Person who is beyond both. The nakṣatra Hasta (ruled by Savita, the Sun) after 11:49 AM EDT brings dexterity and skill, ideal for discerning the transcendental reality beyond material dualities. The yoga Indra (after 1:50 PM EDT) embodies strength and vitality, supporting the resolve to recognize the Lord as the ultimate reality beyond all dualities. Friday (Shukravāra), ruled by Venus (Shukra), emphasizes refinement and the pursuit of beauty, which finds its perfection in the Supreme Person. As the last day of Pitru Paksha, today calls for honoring ancestors while remembering that our true identity lies in the eternal Supreme Person, beyond temporary material relationships. The verse’s teaching — that the Lord is beyond the fallible and infallible — invites us to transcend our identification with the body and mind, resting in the awareness of the Supreme Person who is the ultimate reality beyond all dualities.

 

🔥 YS I.24: kleśa-karma-vipākāśayair aparāmṛṣṭaḥ puruṣa-viśeṣa Īśvaraḥ — "The Lord is a special soul. He is untouched by the obstacles [to the practice of yoga], karma, the fructification [of karma], and subconscious predispositions." BG 15.18 describes the Supreme Person as transcendental beyond both the fallible and the infallible living entities. YS I.24 complements this by defining Īśvara as a special puruṣa (puruṣa-viśeṣa) who is untouched by the kleshas, karma, and samskaras that bind ordinary souls. Both texts affirm the existence of a transcendental consciousness beyond the ordinary puruṣa-prakṛti interplay — one that sustains creation while remaining eternally distinct and unaffected by material conditions. The verse and sutra together map the journey: from recognizing the Lord’s supreme position in cosmology (BG 15.18) to understanding His nature as the untouched, special puruṣa who is the object of yoga (YS I.24). This connection is especially potent today on the eve of Amavasya, when the moon’s absence invites contemplation of the transcendental reality beyond dualities.
