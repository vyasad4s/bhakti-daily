🏹 Bhagavad Gita 13.18 | 2026-09-10

 

ज्योतिषामपि तज्ज्योतिस्तमस: परमुच्यते । ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं ज्ञानगम्यं हृदि सर्वस्य विष्ठितम् ॥ १८ ॥

 

jyotiṣām api taj jyotis tamasaḥ param ucyate jñānaṁ jñeyaṁ jñāna-gamyaṁ hṛdi sarvasya viṣṭhitam

 

He is the source of light in all luminous objects. He is beyond the darkness of matter and is unmanifested. He is knowledge, He is the object of knowledge, and He is the goal of knowledge. He is situated in everyone’s heart.

 

From a first-principles Sankhya-Yoga perspective, this verse reveals the nature of the Absolute Truth as the inner light that transcends all darkness – a perfect reflection of Amavasya (new moon) energy. The field (kṣetra) is prakṛti manifest as the body-mind complex, governed by the three gunas and their modifications. The knower of the field (kṣetra-jña) is purua, pure consciousness that illuminates but does not act. Yet here we encounter a third principle: the Supreme Truth that is jyotiṣām api taj jyotis – the source of light in all luminous objects. This light is not merely external but the very essence that illuminates everything from within. The verse declares tamasaḥ param ucyate – "It is said to be beyond the darkness" – directly addressing the Amavasya condition where the moon is not visible, symbolizing the darkness of material ignorance. This is not a physical darkness but the darkness of avidyā (ignorance) that veils the true nature of reality. The Supreme Truth is further described as jñānam (knowledge), jñeyam (the object of knowledge), and jñāna-gamyam (approachable through knowledge). This triad reveals the self-sufficient nature of the Absolute: it is both the subject and object of knowledge, and the means to attain it. Most significantly, hṛdi sarvasya viṣṭhitam – "situated in everyone’s heart" – reveals that this inner light is not distant or external but the very core of our being. On Amavasya, when the external moon is hidden, we are reminded to turn inward to perceive this eternal light that never wanes. This understanding aligns perfectly with Pithori Amavasya, a special new moon observance focused on ancestral healing and dissolution of karmic patterns. Just as the moon disappears to allow for inner reflection, the Absolute Truth remains ever-present as the inner light awaiting discovery. The darkness of Amavasya is not an absence of light but an opportunity to recognize that the true source of illumination resides within – not as a reflected glow but as the self-luminous reality of consciousness itself.

 

🔥 Yoga Sutra Connection: BG 13.18’s description of the inner light beyond darkness finds its classical Yoga counterpart in Sutra 1.36: "viśokā vā jyotiṣmatī" – "Or from the inner light that is free from sorrow." This sutra describes one of the methods for stabilizing the mind: focusing on the inner light (jyotiṣmatī) that is free from sorrow (viśoka). BG 13.18’s "tamasaḥ param ucyate" (beyond the darkness is said) and "hṛdi sarvasya viṣṭhitam" (situated in everyone’s heart) describe this same inner light – not as an object to be grasped by the intellect, but as the very subject that perceives. Where the Bhagavad Gita presents this truth as the source of light in all luminous objects, the Yoga Sutras offer the direct method to realize it: turn the mind inward to perceive the light that is free from sorrow.

 

TERM DEFINITIONS:

jyotiṣām (locative plural) - in all luminous objects; like the photon field – the fundamental quantum field that underlies all electromagnetic phenomena

taj jyotis (nominative singular) - that light; like the coherent laser beam – photons in phase, producing a focused, powerful stream

tamasaḥ (ablative singular) - of darkness, ignorance; like signal noise in a communication channel – random fluctuations that obscure the true message

param (indeclinable) - beyond, supreme, highest; like the root user in a Unix system – possesses ultimate access and control

jñāna-gamyam (nom. sing. neuter) - to be approached by knowledge, attainable through wisdom; like the API gateway – the controlled entry point through which external systems can access core functionality

viṣṭhitam (nom. sing. neuter) - situated, placed, established; like the system’s root directory – the foundational location from which all paths originate

pithori (masculine noun) - the sesame seed Amavasya; like the boot sector – small but essential, containing the initial instructions that enable the entire system to start up
