🏹 BG 18.7 | 2026-11-20

 

नियतस्य तु सन्न्यास: कर्मणो नोपपद्यते ॥ ७ ॥ मोहात्तस्य परित्यागस्तामस: परिकीर्तित: ॥ ७ ॥

 

niyatasya tu sannyāsaḥ
karmaṇo nopapadyate
mohāt tasya parityāgas
tāmasaḥ parikīrtitaḥ

 

Prescribed duties should never be renounced. If one gives up his prescribed duties because of illusion, such renunciation is said to be in the mode of ignorance.

 

Friday is Venus' day — Śukra, the planet of desire, beauty, relationship. And here Kṛṣṇa exposes the most dangerous trap: tāmasika-tyāga. Not the renunciation of desire (which is rājasika), not the renunciation of action (which is sāttvika), but the renunciation born of moha (illusion, delusion, ignorance). Niyatasya tu sannyāsaḥ karmaṇo nopapadyate — "Prescribed duties should never be renounced." The verb upapadyate means "is proper, is fitting, is appropriate." To renounce one's prescribed duty (niyata-karma) is not appropriate; it is tāmasaḥ — born of ignorance. Why? Mohāt — from delusion. The person thinks: "This action is binding, I should stop." But the binding is not in the action; it is in the moha. The kartṛ (doer) thinks "I am the doer, I am the enjoyer, I am the sufferer" — that is moha. To renounce action from that place is tāmasaḥ — it compounds the ignorance. Today is Prabodhinī Ekādaśī — the awakening Ekādaśī, the day the Lord awakens from His four-month sleep (Cāturmāsya ends). The sleeper awakens; the illusion ends. Śukravār (Friday) is Venus' day — the planet of kāma (desire), the planet that tests whether desire runs you or you run desire. The panchang amplifies: Uttarā Bhādrapadā (the latter auspicious feet, the water that heals) yields to Revatī (the final nakṣatra, the journey's end, the safe harbor) at 8:20 PM — the journey ends in the safe harbor. Vajra (the thunderbolt, the diamond) yields to Siddhi (accomplishment, perfection) at 6:25 PM — the diamond that becomes the accomplished deed. The Ekādaśī fast breaks the kāma loop; the fast is not tyāga of food — it is upavāsa (dwelling near the Supreme). The verse is the alarm clock: mohāt — from illusion, not from wisdom. Renounce the illusion, not the duty. 🏹

 

🔥 YOGA SUTRA CONNECTION: YS II.5 — anityāśuci-duḥkhānātmasu nitya-śuci-sukhātmakhyātir avidyā — "Ignorance is regarding the impermanent as permanent, the impure as pure, the painful as pleasant, the non-Self as Self." The bridge is exact. BG 18.7's mohāt (from illusion) is YS II.5's avidyā — the root misidentification. To renounce niyata-karma (prescribed duty) because it seems painful or binding is avidyā — mistaking the kleśa (binding) for the karma (action). The binding is not in the action; it is in the moha (YS II.3: avidyāsmitārāgadveṣābhiniveśāḥ kleśāḥ). The renunciation born of moha is tāmasika — it is avidyā in action. YS II.26 — viveka-khyātir aviplavā — "unwavering discriminative discernment" is the antidote: see the duty as niyata (prescribed), not as bandhana (binding). The Ekādaśī fast is the viveka-khyāti practice: the hunger is real, the kāma is real, but the kartṛ (doer) is not the body. The fast breaks the moha loop. 🏹

niyata (नियत) — prescribed, fixed, ordained; like the required function signature — the interface that must be implemented

sannyāsa (सन्न्यास) — renunciation, abandonment; like deprecating the entire module — removing the executable, not just the output

nopapadyate (नोपपद्यते) — does not fit, is not proper, is inappropriate; like type error — the types don't match, the operation is invalid

moha (मोह) — illusion, delusion, infatuation; like the memory corruption — the pointer that points to the wrong address

tāmasa (तामस) — of the nature of ignorance; like dead code — code that executes but serves no purpose, only consumes resources

prabodhinī (प्रबोधिनी) — awakening, the one who awakens; like the interrupt handler — the signal that wakes the sleeping process
