🏹 BG 10.25 | 2026-07-25

 

महर्षीणां भृगुरहं गिरामस्म्येकमक्षरम् । यज्ञानां जपयज्ञोऽस्मि स्थावराणां हिमालय: ॥ २५ ॥

 

maharṣīṇāṁ bhṛgur ahaṁ
girām asmy ekam akṣaram
yajñānāṁ japa-yajño ’smi
sthāvarāṇāṁ himālayaḥ

 

Of the great sages I am Bhṛgu; of vibrations I am the transcendental oṁ. Of sacrifices I am the chanting of the holy names [japa], and of immovable things I am the Himālayas.

 

Today is Śukla Dwādaśī (12th lunar day of the waxing moon) — an auspicious tithi for spiritual practice, building toward full moon illumination. The nakṣatra is Jyeṣṭhā (until 10:04 PM), meaning "the eldest" or "most senior," ruled by Indra (king of devas). This morning's Brahma yoga (until 11:38 AM) transitions to Indra yoga, aligning with the deity of Jyeṣṭhā. Today is Śanivāra (Saturday), governed by Śani (Saturn), the planet of justice, discipline, and karmic return. BG 10.25 reveals Kṛṣṇa as Bhṛgu — the foremost among the great sages (maharṣīṇāṁ bhṛguḥ). Bhṛgu, one of the mind-born sons of Brahmā, is renowned for his piercing spiritual insight and authorship of the Bṛgu Saṃhitā. As "of vibrations I am the transcendental oṁ" (girām asmy ekam akṣaram), Kṛṣṇa identifies with praṇava, the primordial sound. This aligns perfectly with Jyeṣṭhā's energy — the foremost, eldest vibration from which all others emerge. The purport explains: "Of all the transcendental vibrations, oṁ (oṁ-kāra) represents Kṛṣṇa." Oṁ is not a symbol assigned by humans but an eternal designation inherent to the Divine — the sonic embodiment of the Absolute. Through japa of oṁ, Īśvara is praised directly (YS 1.27). The Vaiṣṇava tradition reveals this as the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra: the purest sacrifice (japa-yajña), free from violence, where sound vibration becomes direct communion. Today's panchanga illuminates this verse: - **Tithi:** Śukla Dwādaśī — auspicious for worship, japa, and spiritual disciplines. The waxing moon's growing light supports increasing devotion. - **Nakṣatra:** Jyeṣṭhā (until 10:04 PM) — "the eldest," ruled by Indra (king of gods, wielder of thunderbolt). Jyeṣṭhā's energy is penetrating, victorious, and aligned with overcoming obstacles through divine connection. This matches Bhṛgu's status as the foremost sage and oṁ as the foremost vibration. - **Yoga:** Brahma (until 11:38 AM), then Indra — Brahma (creator) shifts to Indra (king/sovereign), reflecting the journey from creative vibration to sovereign sound. Indra's vajra (thunderbolt) is the pure, unstoppable power of transcendental sound. - **Vāra:** Śanivāra (Saturday) — Śani's day. Śani governs karma, discipline, and slow, enduring results. The mountain Himālayas (mentioned in the verse) are Śani's domain — immovable, eternal, bearing the weight of time. Just as the Himālayas stand unmoved, oṁ-japa establishes the practitioner in unshakable devotion. - **Special:** Rāhu Kāla (9:27–11:14 AM) overlaps with Brahma→Indra yoga transition — a reminder to protect one's japa practice during inauspicious periods by fixing attention on oṁ, which neutralizes negative influences (YS 1.28-29). The sāṅkhya insight is striking: Prakṛti vibrates with innumerable frequencies (guna-play), but puruṣa realizes itself through the single, transcendental vibration — oṁ. As YS 1.28 states: taj-japas tad-artha-bhāvanam — the repetition of oṁ and contemplation on its meaning leads to one-pointedness. Japa-yajña (chanting the holy names) is the highest sacrifice because it offers the mind back to its source, transforming speech (vāk) from instrument of bondage to vehicle of liberation. For the bhakti-yogī, this is not abstract philosophy but urgent practice: in a world of noisy distractions, the mendicant's weapon is japa. The Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra is the sound incarnation of Kṛṣṇa Himself — non-different from His name, form, qualities, and pastimes. To chant is to directly associate. In Jyeṣṭhā's penetrating light, cut through illusion with the sword of oṁ. In Śani's sobering gaze, build an unshakable foundation of constant remembrance. The Himālayas do not rush; they simply are. So too the devotee fixed in oṁ-japa: immovable, eternal, ever-chanting.

 

PRACTICE FOR TODAY

Japa-Yajña Sadhana: Before sunrise (during Brahma muhūrta, 4:16–5:04 AM) or during the auspicious Abhijit muhūrta (12:33–1:31 PM), sit facing east and chant oṁ or Hare Kṛṣṇa for 108 beads with full concentration. Let each syllable be a deliberate offering — not mechanical repetition, but taj-japas tad-artha-bhāvanam (YS 1.28). Feel the vibration in your bones, as if the mountain itself is chanting through you.

Jyeṣṭhā Discernment: Since the nakṣatra is Jyeṣṭhā (the eldest, most senior) until 10:04 PM, before each important action, ask: "Is this the most mature, wisest choice? Would the eldest sage approve?" Let discrimination (viveka) sharpen like Indra's vajra.

Śani's Steadfastness: Honor Śani's energy by embracing discipline in your sadhana. If you miss a round, do not despair — simply begin again with sincerity. Śani rewards consistent effort over time, not perfection. Let your japa be like the Himālayas: unwavering through storms and seasons.
